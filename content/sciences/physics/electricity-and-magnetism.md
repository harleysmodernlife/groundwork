Electricity and magnetism are two manifestations of a single fundamental force — electromagnetism. Together they govern the behavior of charges and currents, make motors and generators possible, and underlie all of modern technology from power grids to smartphones.

---

## 1. Electric Charge and Coulomb's Law

Matter is made of atoms containing **protons** (positive charge) and **electrons** (negative charge). Normally atoms are electrically neutral. When electrons are transferred between objects, one becomes negatively charged and the other positively charged.

**Like charges repel; opposite charges attract.**

**Coulomb's Law** quantifies the force between charges:

**F = k · (q₁ · q₂) / r²**

- k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C² (Coulomb's constant)
- q₁, q₂ = magnitudes of the charges (coulombs, C)
- r = distance between charges (m)

This is mathematically identical in form to Newton's Law of Gravitation — both are inverse-square forces — but electrostatic forces are vastly stronger. The electrical repulsion between two electrons is about 10⁴² times stronger than their gravitational attraction.

---

## 2. Electric Fields and Potential

An **electric field** exists in the space around any charge, exerting a force on other charges placed there. Field lines point from positive to negative charges and indicate the direction of force on a positive test charge.

**Electric potential (V)** — measured in volts — is the potential energy per unit charge at a point in a field. A 9-volt battery maintains a 9V potential difference between its terminals.

**V = W/q** (potential = work done per unit charge)

---

## 3. Electric Current and Circuits

**Electric current (I)** is the flow of electric charge — specifically, electrons flowing through a conductor.

**I = Q/t** (current = charge per unit time, measured in amperes, A)

**Voltage (V)** drives current; **resistance (R)** opposes it.

### Ohm's Law

**V = IR**

Voltage equals current times resistance. Resistance is measured in ohms (Ω). Doubling the voltage doubles the current; doubling the resistance halves the current.

### Power in Circuits

**P = IV = I²R = V²/R**

Electrical power is the rate of energy transfer. A 60W bulb uses 60 joules per second.

### Series and Parallel Circuits

- **Series**: components connected end-to-end; same current flows through all. Total resistance = sum of individual resistances. If one component fails, the circuit breaks.
- **Parallel**: components connected across the same two nodes; same voltage across all. Total resistance is less than the smallest individual resistance. If one component fails, others continue to work. Household wiring uses parallel circuits.

---

## 4. Magnetism

Magnetism arises from moving electric charges — either moving charges in a wire (an **electromagnet**) or the spin of electrons in atoms (a **permanent magnet**). Magnetic force is the force on moving charges in a magnetic field.

**Magnetic poles** come in north and south pairs — and unlike electric charges, they cannot be isolated. Cut a magnet in half and you get two smaller magnets, each with a north and south pole.

Earth behaves as a giant magnet, with a magnetic field that extends into space. This field deflects charged solar wind particles, protecting Earth's surface from harmful radiation. Compass needles align with Earth's magnetic field lines.

---

## 5. Electromagnetism: The Connection

**Oersted's discovery (1820)**: an electric current produces a magnetic field around it. A straight wire carrying current creates concentric circular magnetic field lines.

A **solenoid** (coiled wire) concentrates this field, acting like a bar magnet when current flows. **Electromagnets** can be switched on and off, and their strength controlled — which is why they're used in electric motors, generators, MRI machines, maglev trains, and countless other devices.

**Faraday's Law of Induction (1831)**: a *changing* magnetic field through a loop of wire induces a voltage (and current) in the wire. This is the principle behind:
- **Generators**: mechanical rotation of coils in a magnetic field generates electricity
- **Transformers**: changing AC current in one coil induces current in an adjacent coil at a different voltage, allowing efficient long-distance power transmission
- **Electric motors**: current-carrying coils in magnetic fields produce rotation (the reverse of a generator)

---

## 6. Maxwell's Equations and Electromagnetic Waves

James Clerk Maxwell unified electricity and magnetism in four equations (1865) that described all electromagnetic phenomena. A key prediction was that oscillating electric and magnetic fields propagate as waves at the speed of light — and light *is* an electromagnetic wave. This unification of electricity, magnetism, and optics was one of the greatest achievements in physics.

Maxwell's equations predicted radio waves before they were discovered. Heinrich Hertz experimentally confirmed them in 1887. Every wireless communication system — radio, TV, WiFi, cellular — exists because of electromagnetic wave theory.

---

## Summary

Electric charge comes in positive and negative forms; opposite charges attract. Current flows when charges move under a potential difference, and Ohm's Law (V = IR) relates voltage, current, and resistance. Magnetism arises from moving charges, and a changing magnetic field induces current — the principle behind generators and transformers. Electricity and magnetism are aspects of a single force, unified by Maxwell's equations, which predicted electromagnetic waves including light itself.
