Energy is the capacity to do work. Work is the transfer of energy through force. These two concepts — along with the principle that energy is conserved — form the backbone of classical mechanics and underlie every machine, engine, and physical process in the universe.

---

## 1. Work

In everyday language, "work" means effort. In physics, it has a precise definition:

**W = F · d · cos(θ)**

- W = work (joules, J)
- F = magnitude of the applied force (N)
- d = displacement of the object in the direction the force is applied (m)
- θ = angle between the force vector and the displacement vector

**Key consequences of this definition:**
- If you carry a bag horizontally across a room, you do *no work* on the bag — your upward support force is perpendicular to the horizontal displacement (cos 90° = 0).
- Only the component of force *parallel to the displacement* does work.
- If the force opposes motion (friction), it does *negative work* — it removes energy from the system.

**1 joule = 1 newton × 1 meter** = the work done lifting a 100 g apple by about 1 meter.

---

## 2. Energy

**Energy** is a scalar quantity measured in joules. It comes in many forms, but two are central to mechanics:

### Kinetic Energy

**Kinetic energy (KE)** is the energy of motion:

**KE = ½mv²**

- m = mass (kg)
- v = speed (m/s)

Note that KE scales with the *square* of speed — doubling speed quadruples kinetic energy. This is why high-speed collisions are so destructive.

### Gravitational Potential Energy

**Gravitational potential energy (PE)** is stored energy due to an object's position above a reference height:

**PE = mgh**

- m = mass (kg)
- g = 9.8 m/s²
- h = height above the reference point (m)

The reference point is arbitrary — you choose it based on convenience (usually the lowest point in the problem).

### Other Forms of Energy

- **Elastic potential energy**: stored in stretched or compressed springs (PE = ½kx²)
- **Chemical energy**: stored in molecular bonds; released by combustion, metabolism, batteries
- **Thermal energy**: kinetic energy of random molecular motion
- **Electrical energy**: energy of moving electric charges
- **Nuclear energy**: stored in atomic nuclei; released by fission and fusion
- **Electromagnetic radiation**: energy carried by light and other electromagnetic waves

---

## 3. The Work-Energy Theorem

The **work-energy theorem** states that the net work done on an object equals the change in its kinetic energy:

**W_net = ΔKE = KE_final − KE_initial**

This is a direct consequence of Newton's Second Law and is one of the most useful relationships in mechanics. It allows you to find final speed from force and distance without needing to know acceleration explicitly.

---

## 4. Conservation of Energy

The **law of conservation of energy** is one of the most fundamental principles in all of science:

**Energy cannot be created or destroyed — only converted from one form to another.**

In a closed system with no friction or air resistance:

**KE + PE = constant**   (total mechanical energy is conserved)

As an object falls, potential energy converts to kinetic energy. At the highest point of a pendulum swing, all energy is PE; at the lowest point, all is KE; in between, it's a mix — but the total never changes.

When friction is present, mechanical energy is not conserved — but the "lost" mechanical energy has been converted to thermal energy (heat). Total energy, including thermal, is always conserved.

---

## 5. Power

**Power** is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred:

**P = W / t = F · v**

- P = power (watts, W)
- W = work (joules)
- t = time (seconds)
- v = velocity (m/s)

**1 watt = 1 joule per second**

A 100 W lightbulb uses 100 joules every second. A car engine producing 150 kW (≈200 horsepower) can sustain 150,000 J of work per second.

**1 horsepower ≈ 746 watts** (a historical unit based on the rate at which a horse could do work)

**Kilowatt-hours (kWh)** are units of energy (not power) used in electricity billing: 1 kWh = 1 kW × 1 hour = 3,600,000 J = 3.6 MJ.

---

## 6. Simple Machines and Mechanical Advantage

Simple machines (lever, pulley, inclined plane, wheel and axle, wedge, screw) make work easier by trading force for distance. They do not reduce the amount of work required.

**Mechanical advantage = output force / input force**

An inclined plane lets you move a heavy object to a height with less force — but you must push it a longer distance. The work is the same; you've redistributed it across more distance.

**Efficiency = useful work output / total work input × 100%**

Real machines are less than 100% efficient because some energy is always lost to friction and heat.

---

## Summary

Work is the transfer of energy by a force acting over a displacement — only the force component parallel to motion counts. Kinetic energy is ½mv²; gravitational potential energy is mgh. The work-energy theorem connects net work to change in kinetic energy. Conservation of energy means energy can change form but never appear or disappear. Power is the rate of energy transfer. Simple machines redistribute force and distance but cannot reduce total work required.
