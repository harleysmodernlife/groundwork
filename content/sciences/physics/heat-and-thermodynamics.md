Heat is not a substance — it is energy in transit. Thermodynamics is the study of how thermal energy behaves, transforms, and sets fundamental limits on what machines can accomplish. From refrigerators to car engines to the arrow of time itself, thermodynamics governs the physical world at every scale.

---

## 1. Temperature and Thermal Energy

**Temperature** is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles in a substance. Higher temperature means particles are moving faster, on average. Temperature is measured in:
- **Celsius (°C)**: water freezes at 0°C, boils at 100°C
- **Fahrenheit (°F)**: water freezes at 32°F, boils at 212°F
- **Kelvin (K)**: the scientific scale; 0 K (absolute zero) is the theoretical minimum temperature, where particle motion stops. K = °C + 273.15. There is no negative Kelvin.

**Thermal energy** is the total kinetic energy of all particles in a substance. A large, cool object can have more thermal energy than a small, hot one.

**Heat (Q)** is thermal energy that flows from a hotter object to a cooler one. It is a process, not a property of an object. You cannot say an object "contains heat" — it contains thermal energy.

---

## 2. Heat Transfer

Thermal energy moves by three mechanisms:

- **Conduction**: direct transfer of heat through physical contact. Faster in solids where particles are close together. Metals are excellent conductors; wood, air, and plastics are poor conductors (insulators).
- **Convection**: heat transfer through the bulk movement of fluid (liquid or gas). Hot fluid rises (lower density), cool fluid sinks — forming convection currents. Responsible for ocean currents, atmospheric circulation, and the mantle convection that drives plate tectonics.
- **Radiation**: heat transfer via electromagnetic waves (primarily infrared), requiring no medium. The sun heats Earth through radiation across the vacuum of space. All objects radiate heat; hotter objects radiate more intensely.

## 3. Specific Heat Capacity

**Specific heat capacity (c)** is the amount of energy needed to raise 1 kg of a substance by 1°C.

**Q = mcΔT**

- Q = heat energy added or removed (joules)
- m = mass (kg)
- c = specific heat (J/kg·°C)
- ΔT = temperature change (°C)

Water has an exceptionally high specific heat (4,186 J/kg·°C), which is why oceans moderate coastal climates — they absorb enormous amounts of heat with relatively little temperature change. This is also why it takes so long to boil a pot of water.

---

## 4. Phase Changes

When a substance changes phase (solid → liquid → gas), temperature stays constant while energy is absorbed or released as **latent heat** — energy used to break or form molecular bonds rather than change kinetic energy.

- **Latent heat of fusion**: energy to melt (or freeze) 1 kg of a substance at constant temperature
- **Latent heat of vaporization**: energy to vaporize (or condense) 1 kg at constant temperature

Water's high latent heat of vaporization (2,260 kJ/kg) explains why sweating is such an effective cooling mechanism — a small amount of evaporated water carries away a large amount of heat.

## 5. The Laws of Thermodynamics

### Zeroth Law

If object A is in thermal equilibrium with B, and B is in thermal equilibrium with C, then A and C are in thermal equilibrium. This defines temperature as a meaningful, transitive quantity and is the basis of thermometry.

### First Law: Conservation of Energy

**ΔU = Q − W**

The change in internal energy of a system equals the heat added to it minus the work it does on its surroundings. This is conservation of energy applied to thermodynamics: energy is never created or destroyed.

### Second Law: Entropy

Heat flows spontaneously from hot to cold, never the reverse. More generally: in any natural process, the total **entropy** (disorder, or number of possible arrangements) of a closed system either increases or stays the same — it never decreases spontaneously.

Consequences:
- No heat engine can be 100% efficient — some energy always becomes waste heat that cannot be converted to work
- You cannot build a refrigerator that cools without doing work
- The universe tends toward increasing disorder — this is why your desk gets messier without effort, why broken eggs don't reassemble, and why perpetual motion machines are impossible

### Third Law

As temperature approaches absolute zero (0 K), entropy approaches a minimum value (typically zero for a perfect crystal). Absolute zero is theoretically unreachable in a finite number of steps.

## 6. Heat Engines and Refrigerators

A **heat engine** absorbs heat from a hot reservoir (Q_H), converts some to work (W), and expels waste heat to a cold reservoir (Q_C):

**Efficiency = W / Q_H = 1 − (Q_C / Q_H)**

The maximum theoretical efficiency is given by the **Carnot efficiency**: η = 1 − (T_C / T_H), using absolute temperatures. Real engines are always less efficient than this ideal.

A **refrigerator** runs a heat engine in reverse — work is done to move heat from cold to hot (against the natural direction). Air conditioners, heat pumps, and refrigerators all operate on this principle.

---

## Summary

Temperature measures average particle kinetic energy; heat is thermal energy in transit. Energy transfers by conduction, convection, and radiation. Specific heat capacity determines how much energy changes a substance's temperature. Phase changes involve latent heat at constant temperature. The laws of thermodynamics state that energy is conserved, entropy always increases in natural processes, and no engine can convert heat to work with perfect efficiency.
