Kinematics is the branch of physics that describes motion — how fast things move, how their speed changes, and where they end up — without asking *why* they move that way. It is the vocabulary of motion, built from a handful of precisely defined quantities and the mathematical relationships between them.

---

## 1. Key Quantities

### Position and Displacement

**Position** is where an object is relative to a reference point. **Displacement** is the change in position — a vector quantity with both magnitude and direction.

- Displacement = final position − initial position
- Distance (scalar) measures total path length; displacement measures the net change from start to finish.

*Example: If you walk 3 km north, then 3 km south, your distance traveled is 6 km but your displacement is 0.*

### Speed and Velocity

**Speed** is how fast an object moves — distance divided by time. It is a scalar.

**Velocity** is speed with direction — displacement divided by time. It is a vector.

- Average speed = total distance ÷ total time
- Average velocity = total displacement ÷ total time

**Instantaneous velocity** is the velocity at a single moment in time — what a speedometer reads.

### Acceleration

**Acceleration** is the rate of change of velocity — how quickly velocity is changing, and in what direction. A car speeding up, slowing down, or turning is all experiencing acceleration.

- Average acceleration = (final velocity − initial velocity) ÷ time
- Units: m/s² (meters per second squared)

Acceleration is a vector. Acceleration in the direction of motion speeds an object up; acceleration opposing motion slows it down (**deceleration**).

---

## 2. The Kinematic Equations

For constant (uniform) acceleration, four equations relate position, velocity, acceleration, and time. These are the foundation of most introductory kinematics problems.

1. **v = v₀ + at**
   Final velocity equals initial velocity plus acceleration times time.

2. **x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at²**
   Position equals initial position plus initial velocity times time plus half acceleration times time squared.

3. **v² = v₀² + 2a(x − x₀)**
   Final velocity squared equals initial velocity squared plus twice acceleration times displacement. (Useful when time is unknown.)

4. **x = x₀ + ½(v₀ + v)t**
   Displacement equals the average of initial and final velocity multiplied by time.

*Where: v = final velocity, v₀ = initial velocity, a = acceleration, t = time, x = final position, x₀ = initial position.*

---

## 3. Free Fall

**Free fall** is the special case of constant acceleration in which only gravity acts on an object (no air resistance).

Near Earth's surface, all objects in free fall accelerate downward at the same rate regardless of mass:

**g = 9.8 m/s²** (approximately 10 m/s² for quick estimates)

Galileo established this counterintuitive fact by experiment. A feather and a hammer fall at the same rate in a vacuum. Air resistance, not different gravitational pull, is why a feather falls more slowly in normal conditions.

An object thrown horizontally undergoes **projectile motion**: constant horizontal velocity (no horizontal force) and constant downward acceleration (gravity). The two motions are completely independent.

---

## 4. Vectors and Graphs

### Vector Components

A velocity or displacement at an angle can be broken into **horizontal and vertical components** using trigonometry:
- Horizontal: v_x = v·cos(θ)
- Vertical: v_y = v·sin(θ)

This decomposition allows 2D motion problems to be solved as two separate 1D problems.

### Motion Graphs

- **Position–time graph**: slope = velocity. Flat line = at rest; steep slope = fast; curved line = accelerating.
- **Velocity–time graph**: slope = acceleration. Area under the curve = displacement.
- **Acceleration–time graph**: area under the curve = change in velocity.

## 5. Worked Example

A ball is dropped from rest off a 45-meter cliff. How long does it take to hit the ground? How fast is it moving when it lands?

Given: x₀ = 0, x = 45 m, v₀ = 0, a = 9.8 m/s²

Using **x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at²**:
45 = 0 + 0 + ½(9.8)t²
45 = 4.9t²
t² = 9.18
**t ≈ 3.03 seconds**

Using **v = v₀ + at**:
v = 0 + (9.8)(3.03)
**v ≈ 29.7 m/s** (~107 km/h)

---

## Summary

Kinematics describes motion using displacement, velocity, and acceleration. The kinematic equations connect these quantities for objects under constant acceleration. Free fall is constant downward acceleration at 9.8 m/s². Graphs of position, velocity, and time provide visual tools for understanding motion without calculation.
