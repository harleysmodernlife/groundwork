The Big Bang theory is the prevailing cosmological model for the origin and evolution of the universe. It describes how the universe began from an extremely hot, dense state approximately 13.8 billion years ago and has been expanding and cooling ever since. It is one of the best-tested theories in all of science, supported by multiple independent lines of observational evidence.

---

## 1. What the Big Bang Was (and Wasn't)

The Big Bang was not an explosion of matter into pre-existing empty space. It was the rapid expansion of space itself — all of space, matter, and energy. There was no "center" of the explosion; every point in the universe was the center. Every point in space is moving away from every other point, like dots on the surface of an expanding balloon.

The Big Bang theory does not describe what "caused" the Big Bang or what existed "before" it — those questions are at the limit of current physics, where our mathematical frameworks break down. The theory begins at a fraction of a second after the initial singularity, when the universe was already extremely hot and dense.

## 2. Key Evidence

### Hubble's Law and the Expanding Universe

In 1929, Edwin Hubble showed that galaxies are moving away from us — and the farther they are, the faster they recede (**Hubble's Law**: v = H₀d). Running this expansion backward in time implies that the universe was once far smaller and denser — the first major evidence for the Big Bang.

The **Hubble constant (H₀)** ≈ 70 km/s per megaparsec — for every additional megaparsec of distance, galaxies recede ~70 km/s faster. Current precise measurements show a slight tension between values derived from the early universe (CMB) and the local universe (Cepheids + supernovae), which may indicate new physics.

### The Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB)

Predicted in the 1940s and accidentally discovered in 1965 by Penzias and Wilson, the **Cosmic Microwave Background** is thermal radiation permeating all of space — the afterglow of the Big Bang.

About 380,000 years after the Big Bang, the universe cooled enough for electrons to combine with protons to form neutral hydrogen atoms (**recombination**). The universe became transparent — photons could travel freely for the first time. Those photons have been traveling ever since and, redshifted by cosmic expansion, now appear as microwave radiation at a temperature of **2.725 K** (−270.4°C), nearly uniform across the entire sky.

Tiny temperature fluctuations (one part in 100,000) in the CMB correspond to slight density variations in the early universe — the seeds of today's large-scale structure (galaxies, clusters, filaments). These fluctuations match precisely the predictions of Big Bang cosmology.

### Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN)

In the first three minutes after the Big Bang, the universe was hot enough for nuclear fusion. During this window, **protons and neutrons fused to form the lightest nuclei**: hydrogen, deuterium, helium-3, helium-4, and trace amounts of lithium-7.

**Predictions of BBN:**
- ~75% hydrogen, ~25% helium by mass (with tiny traces of others)

**Observed abundances of these primordial elements in old, unpolluted gas clouds and stars match BBN predictions with extraordinary precision** — one of the most precise quantitative tests of any cosmological theory.

Elements heavier than lithium were not produced in significant quantities during the Big Bang — they were made in stars.

## 3. Timeline of the Universe

**Planck Epoch (0 to 10⁻⁴³ seconds)**: beyond the reach of current physics; all four fundamental forces may have been unified.

**Grand Unification Epoch**: gravity separates; strong, weak, and electromagnetic forces still unified.

**Inflationary Epoch (~10⁻³⁶ to 10⁻³² seconds)**: the universe undergoes **cosmic inflation** — exponential expansion by a factor of at least 10²⁶. This solves several problems with the standard Big Bang:
- **Horizon problem**: why is the CMB so uniform if distant regions of the universe couldn't have been in causal contact?
- **Flatness problem**: why is the universe's geometry so precisely flat?
Inflation predicts the quantum fluctuations that seeded large-scale structure.

**Quark Epoch (~10⁻¹² to 10⁻⁶ seconds)**: as the universe cools, the Higgs field gives particles mass; quarks and gluons exist freely.

**Hadron Epoch (~10⁻⁶ to 1 second)**: quarks combine into hadrons (protons and neutrons). Matter slightly exceeds antimatter by 1 part in a billion — the survivors form all the matter in the universe. Most matter-antimatter pairs annihilate.

**Lepton Epoch (~1 to 10 seconds)**: leptons (electrons, neutrinos) dominate. Neutrinos decouple from matter and stream freely — the **cosmic neutrino background**.

**Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (~10 seconds to 3 minutes)**: protons and neutrons fuse into light nuclei.

**Photon Epoch (~3 minutes to 380,000 years)**: universe is an opaque plasma of ions and photons.

**Recombination (~380,000 years)**: electrons bind to nuclei; universe becomes transparent; CMB released.

**Dark Ages (~380,000 years to ~150 million years)**: no stars, no light sources. The universe cools and expands, with slight density variations growing through gravity.

**Reionization (~150 million to 1 billion years)**: the first stars and galaxies form, emitting intense UV radiation that reionizes the surrounding hydrogen gas.

**Present day (~13.8 billion years)**: galaxies, stars, planets, and life.

## 4. Dark Matter and Dark Energy

Modern cosmology requires two mysterious components to explain observations:

### Dark Matter

Normal (baryonic) matter — atoms — makes up only ~5% of the universe's total energy-mass content. **Dark matter** (~27%) is matter that interacts gravitationally but not electromagnetically (doesn't emit, absorb, or reflect light). Evidence:
- Galaxy rotation curves: stars in the outer regions of galaxies orbit faster than visible mass alone can explain
- Gravitational lensing: light is bent more than visible mass predicts
- Cosmic structure: the CMB fluctuations only produce the observed large-scale structure when dark matter is included in models

The identity of dark matter remains one of the biggest open questions in physics. Leading candidates include Weakly Interacting Massive Particles (WIMPs) and axions.

### Dark Energy

The universe's expansion is **accelerating** — discovered in 1998 through observations of distant Type Ia supernovae. The unknown cause of this acceleration is called **dark energy** (~68% of the universe's total content). The simplest model treats it as the **cosmological constant (Λ)** — an intrinsic energy density of empty space. Its nature is entirely unknown.

---

## 5. The Fate of the Universe

Current evidence suggests the universe will continue to expand, with dark energy driving acceleration:

- **Big Freeze/Heat Death**: the most likely scenario. Stars eventually exhaust their fuel; black holes slowly evaporate via Hawking radiation; the universe approaches maximum entropy — cold, dark, and diffuse.
- **Big Rip**: if dark energy strengthens over time, it could eventually overcome gravity and tear apart galaxies, then stars, planets, and finally atoms.
- **Big Crunch**: possible if dark energy weakens; expansion reverses and universe collapses.

---

## Summary

The Big Bang theory describes the universe beginning from an extremely hot, dense state 13.8 billion years ago and expanding ever since. Evidence includes the expanding universe (Hubble's Law), the Cosmic Microwave Background (afterglow of recombination), and primordial element abundances matching Big Bang nucleosynthesis predictions. Cosmic inflation explains the universe's uniformity. Normal matter is only 5% of the universe's content; dark matter (~27%) and dark energy (~68%) dominate. The universe's ultimate fate depends on dark energy's behavior over cosmic time.
