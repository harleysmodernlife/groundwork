Our solar system contains eight diverse planets orbiting the Sun — ranging from rocky, cratered worlds close to the Sun to giant balls of gas and ice billions of kilometers away. Each planet is distinct, shaped by its distance from the Sun, composition, and history of formation.

---

## 1. The Solar System's Structure

The solar system formed ~4.6 billion years ago from a rotating disk of gas and dust — the **solar nebula**. As material accreted, the Sun formed at the center. Planets formed from the remaining disk material through a process of collision and gravitational accumulation.

**Key distances and facts:**
- 1 **AU** (astronomical unit) = average Earth-Sun distance = 150 million km
- The eight planets orbit in nearly the same plane (the ecliptic) and in the same direction (counterclockwise as viewed from above the North Pole)
- The **frost line** (snow line) at ~2.7 AU from the Sun marks where water and other volatile compounds could condense to ice, enabling the formation of the massive outer planets

### The Terrestrial Planets (inner solar system)

Rocky, dense, relatively small, with solid surfaces. Close to the Sun. Few or no moons.

### The Jovian Planets (outer solar system)

Gas giants or ice giants: large, low density, no solid surface, many moons and rings. Formed beyond the frost line.

## 2. Mercury

- **Distance from Sun**: 0.39 AU
- **Diameter**: 4,879 km (smallest planet)
- **Day length**: 59 Earth days; **Year**: 88 Earth days
- **Surface temperature**: −180°C to 430°C (greatest temperature range of any planet)
- **Atmosphere**: virtually none (too small to hold one)
- **Moons**: 0

Mercury's eccentric orbit and slow rotation cause extreme temperature swings. Its heavily cratered surface resembles the Moon. It has a large iron core (disproportionate to its size), possibly due to a giant collision that stripped away its outer layers.

---

## 3. Venus

- **Distance**: 0.72 AU
- **Diameter**: 12,104 km (similar to Earth)
- **Day**: 243 Earth days (rotates backwards — east to west); **Year**: 225 Earth days
- **Surface temperature**: ~465°C (hottest planet, despite being farther from Sun than Mercury)
- **Atmosphere**: 96% CO₂, thick (92× Earth's pressure); sulfuric acid clouds
- **Moons**: 0

Venus is Earth's twin in size but an inferno due to a runaway greenhouse effect. Its retrograde rotation and extreme atmospheric pressure make it inhospitable. The thick cloud cover reflects most sunlight (making it the brightest object in the night sky besides the Moon), yet the greenhouse effect traps enough heat to melt lead on its surface.

---

## 4. Earth

- **Distance**: 1.0 AU
- **Diameter**: 12,742 km
- **Day**: 24 hours; **Year**: 365.25 days
- **Surface temperature**: average 15°C (range −88°C to 58°C)
- **Atmosphere**: 78% N₂, 21% O₂
- **Moons**: 1

Earth is the only known planet harboring life. Its **liquid water**, moderate temperature, protective atmosphere, strong magnetic field (shielding from solar radiation), and active plate tectonics (which recycles carbon and nutrients) all contribute to habitability. Earth's large Moon stabilizes its axial tilt, preventing extreme climate shifts.

---

## 5. Mars

- **Distance**: 1.52 AU
- **Diameter**: 6,779 km (about half Earth's)
- **Day**: 24 hours 37 minutes; **Year**: 687 Earth days
- **Surface temperature**: −153°C to 20°C (average −55°C)
- **Atmosphere**: 95% CO₂, very thin (0.6% of Earth's pressure)
- **Moons**: 2 (Phobos and Deimos — likely captured asteroids)

Mars shows evidence of ancient liquid water: river valleys, lake beds, and minerals that form only in water. It hosts **Olympus Mons** (the largest volcano in the solar system, 21 km high) and **Valles Marineris** (a canyon system 4,000 km long). Mars is the primary target for human exploration.

---

## 6. Jupiter

- **Distance**: 5.2 AU
- **Diameter**: 143,000 km (largest planet; 11× Earth)
- **Day**: 9 hours 55 minutes; **Year**: 11.9 Earth years
- **Composition**: ~90% hydrogen, ~10% helium (no solid surface)
- **Moons**: 95 known, including Ganymede (largest moon in solar system), Europa (potential subsurface ocean), Io (most volcanically active body in solar system)

Jupiter's **Great Red Spot** is a storm larger than Earth, persisting for centuries. Its powerful gravity perturbs asteroid orbits and has protected the inner solar system from many potential impactors. Jupiter's intense magnetic field creates radiation belts that would be lethal to unprotected astronauts.

---

## 7. Saturn

- **Distance**: 9.6 AU
- **Diameter**: 116,000 km
- **Day**: 10 hours 33 minutes; **Year**: 29.5 Earth years
- **Density**: 0.69 g/cm³ (less than water — Saturn would float)
- **Moons**: 146 known, including Titan (thick atmosphere, liquid methane lakes) and Enceladus (geysers of water vapor)

Saturn's **ring system** — made of ice and rock particles ranging from micrometers to meters — extends 280,000 km from the planet but is only ~10 meters thick on average. The rings are thought to be relatively young (100–400 million years old), possibly from a shattered moon or comet.

---

## 8. Uranus

- **Distance**: 19.2 AU
- **Diameter**: 51,000 km
- **Rotation**: axial tilt of 98° — Uranus essentially rotates on its side; **Year**: 84 Earth years
- **Composition**: water, ammonia, and methane ice (hence "ice giant") with a hydrogen-helium atmosphere
- **Temperature**: −224°C (coldest planetary atmosphere despite not being the farthest from the Sun)
- **Moons**: 28 known; **Rings**: 13

Uranus's extreme tilt is thought to result from a collision with an Earth-sized body early in solar system history. Its methane atmosphere absorbs red light, giving it a blue-green color.

---

## 9. Neptune

- **Distance**: 30.1 AU (~4.5 billion km from the Sun)
- **Diameter**: 49,000 km
- **Year**: 165 Earth years (completed its first orbit since discovery in 2011)
- **Composition**: similar to Uranus (ice giant); deep blue color from methane
- **Winds**: fastest in the solar system, up to 2,100 km/h
- **Moons**: 16 known, including Triton (orbits retrograde — captured Kuiper Belt object; has geysers of nitrogen)

Neptune was predicted mathematically before being observed — astronomers noticed Uranus deviated from its predicted orbit and calculated a perturbing body's position. Neptune was found within 1° of the predicted location in 1846.

---

## 10. Beyond the Planets

- **Asteroid Belt**: between Mars and Jupiter; rocky debris that never coalesced into a planet due to Jupiter's gravity
- **Kuiper Belt**: beyond Neptune; icy bodies including dwarf planets Pluto, Eris, and Makemake
- **Oort Cloud**: spherical shell extending ~1–2 light-years from the Sun; reservoir of long-period comets
- **Dwarf planets**: Pluto, Eris, Ceres, Makemake, Haumea — too small to gravitationally clear their orbital neighborhood

---

## Summary

The solar system's eight planets divide into terrestrial (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars — rocky, small, inner) and Jovian (Jupiter, Saturn — gas giants; Uranus, Neptune — ice giants; outer). Each planet formed from the same solar nebula but evolved differently based on distance, composition, and collision history. The frost line explains why gas giants formed in the outer system. Beyond the planets lie asteroid belts, the Kuiper Belt, and the Oort Cloud.
