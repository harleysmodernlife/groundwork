A cell is not a bag of fluid. It is a highly organized system of compartments, membranes, and molecular machines — each component with a specific function, all working together to sustain life. Understanding cell structure is understanding the hardware that all biology runs on.

---

## 1. The Cell Membrane

Every cell is bounded by the **plasma membrane** (cell membrane) — a thin, flexible barrier about 7–10 nanometers thick.

Its structure is a **fluid mosaic**: a **phospholipid bilayer** (two layers of phospholipids arranged tail-to-tail, with hydrophilic heads facing outward and hydrophobic tails facing inward) studded with proteins, cholesterol, and carbohydrates.

**Functions:**
- Controls what enters and exits the cell (**selective permeability**)
- Protein channels and pumps allow specific ions, nutrients, and waste to move across
- Receptor proteins on the outer surface receive signals from other cells and hormones
- Cholesterol stabilizes membrane fluidity across temperature changes

### Transport Across the Membrane

- **Passive transport** (no energy required): substances move from high to low concentration
  - **Diffusion**: random movement of molecules down a concentration gradient
  - **Osmosis**: diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane (toward higher solute concentration)
  - **Facilitated diffusion**: molecules move through protein channels down their gradient
- **Active transport** (energy/ATP required): moves substances against their concentration gradient, using protein pumps (e.g., sodium-potassium pump)

## 2. The Nucleus

The **nucleus** is the control center of the eukaryotic cell — the compartment that houses the cell's DNA.

- **Nuclear envelope**: double membrane surrounding the nucleus, with **nuclear pores** that regulate traffic of molecules in and out
- **Nucleoplasm**: the fluid inside the nucleus
- **Chromatin**: DNA wound around histone proteins; condenses into visible **chromosomes** during cell division
- **Nucleolus**: region inside the nucleus where **ribosomal RNA (rRNA)** is synthesized and ribosomes are assembled

---

## 3. Ribosomes

**Ribosomes** are the sites of **protein synthesis** — they read messenger RNA (mRNA) and assemble amino acids into proteins. They are found in all cells (prokaryotic and eukaryotic) because protein synthesis is universal.

- **Free ribosomes**: float in the cytoplasm; synthesize proteins used inside the cell
- **Bound ribosomes**: attached to the rough endoplasmic reticulum; synthesize proteins destined for export or insertion into membranes

---

## 4. Endomembrane System

Several organelles work together to produce, modify, and ship proteins and lipids:

### Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)

A vast network of membrane-enclosed channels:
- **Rough ER**: studded with ribosomes; synthesizes and processes proteins that will be secreted or sent to the membrane
- **Smooth ER**: no ribosomes; synthesizes lipids, detoxifies drugs and alcohol, stores calcium ions (especially in muscle cells)

### Golgi Apparatus

The cell's "post office" — a stack of flattened membrane sacs that receives proteins from the rough ER, modifies them (adds sugar chains, cleaves signal peptides), sorts them, and ships them to their final destination: the cell surface, lysosomes, or secretion outside the cell.

### Lysosomes

Membrane-bound sacs containing digestive enzymes. They break down worn-out organelles (**autophagy**), cellular debris, and materials brought in from outside the cell (**phagocytosis**). The low pH inside lysosomes activates their enzymes and protects the rest of the cell from accidental self-digestion.

---

## 5. Mitochondria

**Mitochondria** are the powerhouses of the cell — where **aerobic respiration** occurs, converting glucose and oxygen into **ATP** (adenosine triphosphate), the cell's energy currency.

Structure:
- **Outer membrane**: smooth, permeable to small molecules
- **Intermembrane space**: between the two membranes
- **Inner membrane**: highly folded into **cristae** to maximize surface area; contains the proteins of the electron transport chain
- **Matrix**: inner fluid; contains mitochondrial DNA and ribosomes (evidence of ancient bacterial origin), and the enzymes of the citric acid cycle

Cells with high energy demands (muscle cells, liver cells) contain thousands of mitochondria.

---

## 6. Chloroplasts (Plant Cells Only)

**Chloroplasts** carry out **photosynthesis** — converting light energy, CO₂, and water into glucose and oxygen.

Structure:
- **Outer and inner membranes**
- **Thylakoids**: flattened membrane discs stacked into **grana**; contain **chlorophyll** and other pigments; where light reactions occur
- **Stroma**: fluid surrounding the thylakoids; where the Calvin cycle (carbon fixation) occurs

Like mitochondria, chloroplasts have their own DNA and ribosomes — reflecting their prokaryotic ancestry.

---

## 7. The Cytoskeleton

The **cytoskeleton** is a dynamic network of protein filaments that gives the cell shape, enables movement, and organizes the interior.

- **Microtubules**: large, hollow tubes; form the mitotic spindle during cell division; serve as tracks for motor proteins transporting cargo; make up cilia and flagella
- **Microfilaments (actin filaments)**: thin fibers involved in cell movement, shape changes, and muscle contraction
- **Intermediate filaments**: strong, rope-like fibers that anchor organelles and reinforce the nuclear envelope

---

## 8. Plant Cell Additions

Plant cells have all eukaryotic structures plus:
- **Cell wall**: rigid outer layer of cellulose that supports and protects the cell; prevents osmotic bursting
- **Central vacuole**: large, fluid-filled organelle that stores water, nutrients, and waste products; provides turgor pressure that keeps plants upright
- **Chloroplasts**: for photosynthesis (animal cells have none)
- **Plasmodesmata**: channels through cell walls connecting adjacent plant cells

---

## Summary

The plasma membrane controls what enters and leaves. The nucleus houses DNA and directs gene expression. Ribosomes make proteins; the ER and Golgi apparatus process and ship them; lysosomes digest waste. Mitochondria generate ATP through respiration; chloroplasts (in plants) generate glucose through photosynthesis. The cytoskeleton provides shape and enables movement. Plant cells additionally have a cell wall, central vacuole, and chloroplasts.
