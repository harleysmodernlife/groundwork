## What to Do When Stopped by Police

Police interactions are stressful. People say things they didn't mean, make decisions they later regret, and sometimes make their situation significantly worse — not because they did anything criminal, but because they didn't know their rights or how to exercise them calmly.

This lesson is about practical knowledge for handling a police stop in a way that protects you.

---

## The Two Types of Stops

**Consensual encounter:** Police approach and start talking to you. They may ask questions, ask to see ID, or ask to search your bag. You are free to leave. You don't have to answer questions. You can walk away.

**Terry stop (investigatory stop):** Police stop and briefly detain you based on reasonable suspicion of criminal activity. You're not free to leave during this stop. They may ask questions and conduct a limited pat-down for weapons. They cannot arrest you without probable cause.

**Arrest:** You're in custody, not free to leave. Requires probable cause (a higher standard than reasonable suspicion).

The lines between these can blur in practice — it's not always obvious which type of stop you're in. Ask: "Am I free to leave?" If yes, you may leave (calmly and immediately). If no, you're being detained.

---

## During a Traffic Stop

- Pull over promptly and safely. Turn off the engine. Keep hands visible on the steering wheel.
- When the officer approaches, roll down your window.
- Be polite and calm. Don't argue, don't lecture, don't raise your voice.
- You'll need to provide your driver's license, vehicle registration, and proof of insurance when asked.
- If reaching for documents in the glove compartment or elsewhere, tell the officer first: "My registration is in the glove compartment — may I reach for it?"
- You are not required to answer questions beyond identifying yourself and providing required documents. "I'd rather not answer questions without a lawyer" is a complete, polite answer.
- If asked to step out: comply. In *Pennsylvania v. Mimms*, the Supreme Court ruled officers can order drivers out during a lawful stop.

---

## During a Pedestrian Stop

- Stay calm. Don't run.
- Ask: "Am I free to leave?" If yes, calmly walk away. If no, you're being detained.
- Many states have "stop and identify" laws requiring you to provide your name when lawfully stopped. Check your state's law.
- You don't have to answer questions about where you're going, where you've been, or what you're doing — though you may say "I'd rather not answer questions."
- Don't physically resist, even if you believe the stop is unlawful. Physical resistance can escalate the situation and result in additional charges. The place to challenge an unlawful stop is in court, not in the street.

---

## What to Say and Not Say

**Say:**
- "Am I free to leave?"
- "I am invoking my right to remain silent."
- "I do not consent to a search."
- "I want to speak with a lawyer."

**Don't say:**
- Explanations of where you were or what you were doing
- Defenses of your behavior ("I wasn't speeding, I was only going...")
- Anything you believe will help your case — police are not your allies in this conversation, and anything you say can be used against you
- Lies — lying to police is itself a crime in some circumstances, and gets complicated quickly

**Be polite but firm.** "I don't answer questions without my attorney present" is firm and non-confrontational. You're not insulting the officer. You're exercising a right.

---

## Documenting the Interaction

- Memorize or write down: badge number, officer name, patrol car number, agency, location, time
- If you have a passenger: they can observe, and their memory is a witness account
- You have the right to record police officers performing their duties in public, as long as you don't physically interfere. Courts have broadly upheld this right.
- After the stop: write down everything you remember as soon as possible. If something went wrong, this documentation matters for any complaint or legal challenge.

---

## If Something Goes Wrong

If you believe you were unlawfully stopped, searched, or treated wrongly:

- Do not resist in the moment — comply, and challenge it later
- Document everything immediately after
- Consult with an attorney about your options
- File a complaint with the police department's internal affairs division if appropriate
- The ACLU and civil rights organizations can provide guidance on next steps

Your body is more important than making a point. The time to fight an unlawful stop is in a courtroom, not in the street.
