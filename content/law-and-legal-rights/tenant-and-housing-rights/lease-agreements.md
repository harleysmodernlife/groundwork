## Understanding Your Lease

A lease is a contract between a tenant and landlord that sets the terms of your tenancy. Most people sign leases without reading them — and then are surprised when a landlord withholds a security deposit, enters without notice, or enforces a term they didn't notice.

Reading and understanding your lease before you sign is one of the most practical things you can do.

---

## What a Lease Must Cover

Every lease should clearly state:

**Parties:** Your full name and the landlord's name or company name.

**Property:** The specific address and unit number of what you're renting.

**Lease term:** When the lease starts, when it ends. Is it month-to-month or a fixed term (12 months)? Fixed-term leases lock in your rent but also lock you in — leaving early can have consequences.

**Rent:** How much, when it's due, acceptable payment methods, and grace period before late fees apply. How much are late fees? When exactly does rent need to be received versus postmarked?

**Security deposit:** How much, conditions for return, timeline for return after move-out. Most states have specific laws governing how much landlords can charge and how quickly they must return deposits.

**Utilities:** Which utilities are included in rent and which you pay? Water, gas, electric, trash, internet — get this in writing.

**Occupants:** Who is authorized to live there? Adding unauthorized occupants can be a lease violation.

**Pets:** Are they allowed? Under what conditions? Pet deposits or fees?

**Maintenance responsibilities:** What is the landlord responsible for? What are you responsible for?

---

## Month-to-Month vs. Fixed Term

**Fixed term lease:** Typically 12 months. Your rent can't be raised during the term (unless the lease allows it). You can't be asked to leave without cause during the term. But if you leave early, you may owe rent for the remaining months, or at minimum a lease-break fee.

**Month-to-month:** Renews automatically each month. Either party can end it with proper notice (usually 30 days). Rent can be raised with proper notice. More flexibility for both sides.

When a fixed-term lease ends, it often converts to month-to-month unless you sign a new lease.

---

## Red Flags in Leases

**No written lease:** Verbal rental agreements are generally legal but create disputes. Protect yourself with a written agreement.

**Clauses that waive your legal rights:** "Tenant waives all rights to habitability" or "Tenant waives the right to any notice before entry" — these may be unenforceable depending on your state, but they signal a landlord who will try to push you around.

**Excessive fees:** Application fees, admin fees, move-in fees piled on top of a security deposit. Know what you're paying and why.

**Automatic renewal with penalties:** Lease automatically renews for another full term unless you give 60 days notice. If you don't notice and give late notice, you're on the hook for another year.

**Broad restriction on guests:** Some leases limit how long guests can stay. Know the rules before having family visit for a week.

**Joint and several liability:** Common in multi-tenant leases — each tenant is individually responsible for the full rent. If your roommate doesn't pay, the landlord can come after you for their share.

---

## Before You Sign

1. Read the entire lease, not just the payment terms
2. Ask about anything unclear — in writing so you have their answer documented
3. Document the condition of the unit at move-in with photos and a written inspection report, signed by the landlord if possible
4. Get everything that's verbally promised added to the lease in writing. "The landlord promised to replace the carpet before move-in" is worthless if it's not in the lease.
5. Understand what happens if you need to leave early before signing a fixed-term lease

The lease is your primary protection as a tenant. Know what's in it.
