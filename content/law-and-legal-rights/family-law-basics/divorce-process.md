## The Divorce Process

Divorce legally ends a marriage and resolves the practical consequences: who gets what property, who pays what debts, where children live, whether anyone pays support. The process varies significantly by whether the divorce is contested or uncontested and whether children are involved.

---

## Grounds for Divorce

**No-fault divorce:** All states have no-fault divorce. You don't have to prove your spouse did something wrong. "Irreconcilable differences" or "irretrievable breakdown of the marriage" is sufficient. One spouse wanting a divorce is generally enough — the other spouse cannot legally prevent a divorce indefinitely.

**Fault-based divorce:** Some states still allow fault-based divorce (adultery, abuse, abandonment, etc.) alongside no-fault. Fault can sometimes affect property division or alimony in states that permit it.

---

## Residency Requirements

Most states require that at least one spouse have lived in the state for a minimum period before filing (typically 6 months to one year). You file in the county where you (or your spouse) live.

---

## Uncontested vs. Contested

**Uncontested divorce:** Both spouses agree on everything — property division, debt allocation, child custody and support, spousal support. This is faster, cheaper, and less emotionally damaging. A separation agreement is drafted, signed by both, and presented to the court. The judge reviews and approves it if reasonable.

For couples without significant assets and without children, an uncontested divorce can sometimes be completed for a few hundred dollars in filing fees with forms from the court website.

**Contested divorce:** The spouses disagree on one or more issues. This requires litigation — potentially a full trial. Contested divorces are expensive, slow (can take months to years), and emotionally punishing. Attorneys' fees can easily reach tens of thousands of dollars or more.

Most divorces that start contested eventually settle — statistics suggest fewer than 5% of divorces go all the way through trial.

---

## The Major Issues in Divorce

**Property division:** How marital assets and debts are divided. Two basic systems:

- **Community property states (9 states):** Most property acquired during marriage is jointly owned 50/50. Arizona, California, Idaho, Louisiana, Nevada, New Mexico, Texas, Washington, Wisconsin.
- **Equitable distribution states (most states):** Marital property is divided "fairly" but not necessarily equally. Courts consider length of marriage, contributions of each spouse, earning capacity, and other factors.

**Separate property** (owned before marriage, or received as a gift or inheritance during marriage) is generally not divided. But separate property can become marital property if commingled with marital assets.

**Alimony (spousal support):** One spouse may be ordered to pay the other financial support. Factors include:
- Length of the marriage
- Standard of living during marriage
- Each spouse's earning capacity
- Whether one spouse sacrificed career advancement for the family

Modern alimony is often temporary — designed to help the lower-earning spouse become self-sufficient, not necessarily permanent.

**Child custody and support:** Covered in separate lessons, but custody and support are the most contested and consequential issues in divorces involving children.

---

## The Process Steps

1. **Filing:** One spouse (the petitioner) files a petition for divorce with the court and serves it on the other spouse (the respondent).

2. **Response:** The respondent files an answer, either agreeing or contesting.

3. **Temporary orders:** While the divorce is pending, the court may issue temporary orders for custody, support, and who stays in the marital home.

4. **Discovery:** In contested divorces, both sides exchange financial information — tax returns, bank statements, retirement accounts, business valuations, debts.

5. **Negotiation and mediation:** Most contested divorces settle through negotiation between attorneys, often assisted by a mediator.

6. **Trial (if necessary):** The judge hears evidence and makes decisions on contested issues.

7. **Final decree:** The court issues a final divorce decree stating the terms of the divorce. This legally ends the marriage.

---

## Do You Need an Attorney?

**For simple, uncontested divorces without children:** Possibly not. Many states have court self-help centers and online resources. If both spouses agree, the paperwork is manageable.

**If children are involved:** Strong recommendation to have at least one attorney, and ideally both spouses have representation. Child custody arrangements have long-term consequences.

**If significant assets or debts are involved:** An attorney is important to protect your financial interests.

**If there's any domestic violence or power imbalance:** Get an attorney. Don't negotiate your own settlement.

**For contested divorces:** Legal representation is essentially necessary to navigate litigation effectively.

---

## Separation vs. Divorce

**Legal separation:** A court order that establishes rights and obligations between spouses without ending the marriage. Used for religious reasons, health insurance needs, or as an intermediate step. Legally separated spouses are still married.

**Trial separation:** Informal, not a legal status. No legal protections.

Some states have a waiting period before a divorce is finalized — typically 6 months to one year for divorces involving children. This is sometimes called a cooling-off period.
