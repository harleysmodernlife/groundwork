The 21st century began with the September 11 attacks and the wars that followed. It has included the worst economic crisis since the Great Depression, the election of the first Black president, intensifying partisan polarization, a global pandemic, and continuing reckonings with racial inequality, democratic institutions, and the United States' role in the world.

---

## 1. September 11, 2001, and the "War on Terror"

On September 11, 2001, Al-Qaeda terrorists hijacked four commercial airplanes, crashing two into the World Trade Center towers in New York City, one into the Pentagon, and one (Flight 93, after passengers fought back) into a Pennsylvania field. Nearly 3,000 people were killed.

The attacks transformed American politics:
- Congress passed the **USA PATRIOT Act** (2001), significantly expanding government surveillance powers
- The **Department of Homeland Security** was created
- Congress authorized force against those responsible for the attacks; the Bush administration invoked this broadly

**Afghanistan (2001–2021)**: The U.S. invaded Afghanistan, host to Al-Qaeda and governed by the Taliban, which had sheltered the attackers. The Taliban was quickly displaced; but counterinsurgency continued for twenty years. The Biden administration withdrew remaining U.S. forces in 2021; the Taliban retook the country within weeks.

**Iraq (2003–2011)**: The Bush administration argued that Iraq possessed weapons of mass destruction and had links to Al-Qaeda — both claims proved false. The invasion removed Saddam Hussein but destabilized the country and the region; sectarian violence followed; ISIS emerged partly from the chaos. The last U.S. combat troops left in 2011; a residual presence remained.

## 2. The 2008 Financial Crisis and Great Recession

The 2008 financial crisis was the worst economic crisis since the Great Depression. It was triggered by the collapse of a housing bubble built on risky mortgage lending, complex financial instruments that distributed risk in ways few understood, and inadequate regulation of financial institutions.

Major financial institutions collapsed (Lehman Brothers) or required government bailout (AIG, Citigroup, Bank of America). The stock market lost half its value; unemployment reached 10%; the recession spread globally.

The **TARP** (Troubled Asset Relief Program) and the Obama administration's 2009 stimulus (the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act) prevented a full depression, but recovery was slow and uneven. Inequality increased; the housing losses disproportionately affected Black and Hispanic families who had been targeted by predatory lending.

## 3. Barack Obama's Presidency (2009–2017)

Barack Obama became the first Black president of the United States in 2009 — an event of profound historic significance given the country's history of slavery and racial exclusion.

Major Obama policies:
- **Affordable Care Act (2010)**: The most significant expansion of health insurance coverage since Medicare and Medicaid, extending coverage to 20+ million Americans through Medicaid expansion, insurance marketplaces, and protections for pre-existing conditions
- **Dodd-Frank Act (2010)**: Financial regulation reform in response to the 2008 crisis
- **American Recovery and Reinvestment Act (2009)**: $787 billion stimulus
- **Ending the combat mission in Iraq**; the **killing of Osama bin Laden (2011)**

Obama faced intense opposition — including the rise of the Tea Party movement, unprecedented use of the Senate filibuster, and what became open partisan conflict over every major initiative.

---

## 4. Growing Polarization

Political polarization — partisan sorting, ideological distance between parties, geographic and cultural division — intensified throughout the early 21st century. Factors included:
- **Media fragmentation**: The rise of partisan media and social media created separate information environments
- **Geographic sorting**: Americans increasingly live among others who share their political views
- **Economic inequality**: Diverging economic fortunes produced diverging political interests
- **Cultural conflict**: Debates over immigration, LGBTQ+ rights, guns, abortion, and racial identity became increasingly central to political identity

---

## 5. The Trump Era (2017–2021) and January 6th

Donald Trump's election in 2016, defeating Hillary Clinton (who won the popular vote but lost the Electoral College), shocked many and reflected deep discontent with political and economic establishment institutions.

Trump's presidency:
- Enacted major tax cuts, deregulation, and conservative judicial appointments
- Pursued aggressive immigration restrictions
- Withdrew from international agreements (Paris Climate Accord, Trans-Pacific Partnership, Iran nuclear deal)
- Was impeached twice — for Ukraine pressure (2019) and for incitement related to the January 6, 2021 Capitol attack

**January 6, 2021**: Trump and allies contested the 2020 election results (Biden won; election officials and courts found no fraud that affected the outcome). On January 6, a rally near the White House preceded a mob's assault on the U.S. Capitol, disrupting the certification of electoral votes. Five people died in the attack; the certification was completed hours later. The event raised profound questions about democratic institutions' durability.

---

## 6. COVID-19 and Biden's Presidency

The COVID-19 pandemic, which began in early 2020, killed over 1 million Americans and transformed daily life globally. The pandemic exposed and exacerbated existing inequalities — disproportionately killing Black, Hispanic, and Native Americans; devastating low-wage workers; accelerating remote work for knowledge workers.

Biden's presidency (2021–2025) was marked by significant legislation: the **American Rescue Plan**, the **Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act**, and the **Inflation Reduction Act** (the largest climate investment in U.S. history). The administration also managed the end of the Afghanistan war and navigated support for Ukraine after Russia's 2022 invasion.

---

## Summary

The 21st century United States has been shaped by the September 11 attacks and the wars that followed; the 2008 financial crisis; intensifying partisan polarization; the historic election of Barack Obama; the Trump presidency and January 6th assault on the Capitol; and the COVID-19 pandemic. These events have raised fundamental questions about American democracy, economic inequality, racial justice, global leadership, and the durability of institutions. The story is ongoing and deeply contested.
