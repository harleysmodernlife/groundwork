## Designing a Workout Plan

A workout plan doesn't have to be complicated, but having one — rather than going to the gym and doing whatever you feel like — produces dramatically better results. A plan applies progressive overload systematically, prevents skipping important muscle groups, and removes decision fatigue.

---

## Start with Your Goal

Different goals require different emphasis:

**General health and longevity:** 150+ minutes of moderate cardio per week + 2 strength sessions. You don't need to be an athlete — meeting guidelines has most of the health benefits.

**Building muscle (hypertrophy):** Prioritize resistance training. 3-5 days per week. Higher volume (sets and reps). Progressive overload. Adequate protein (1.6-2.2g/kg body weight).

**Building strength:** Prioritize compound lifts, heavier weights, lower reps (3-5 range), longer rest periods. Programs like Starting Strength, StrongLifts 5x5, and GZCLP are beginner-friendly.

**Improving endurance:** Build aerobic base with consistent moderate-intensity cardio. Gradually increase volume. Add some higher-intensity work.

**Weight loss:** Cardio helps with calorie expenditure; strength training preserves muscle during a caloric deficit. Both matter. But diet drives weight loss more than exercise — you can't out-train a poor diet.

---

## Basic Program Structures

**Full body (3 days per week):** Work all major muscle groups each session. Good for beginners — each muscle gets worked 3x per week, improving neural adaptations. Monday/Wednesday/Friday is a common split.

**Upper/lower split (4 days):** Alternate upper body and lower body sessions. More total volume than full body. Example: Mon upper, Tue lower, Thu upper, Fri lower.

**Push/pull/legs split (PPL, 3 or 6 days):** Push = chest, shoulders, triceps. Pull = back, biceps. Legs = quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves. 3-day version hits each group once per week; 6-day version runs through the cycle twice.

**For most beginners:** Full body 3 days per week is most effective — frequency of stimulus is more important than volume per session at this stage.

---

## Building a Session

**Warm up:** 5-10 minutes of light cardio + dynamic movements (leg swings, arm circles, bodyweight squats). Gets blood flow to muscles, increases core temperature, reduces injury risk.

**Compound movements first:** Do the big lifts when you're fresh — squat, deadlift, bench press, row, overhead press, pull-up. These require the most energy and coordination.

**Isolation exercises after:** Bicep curls, tricep extensions, lateral raises. These are finishing work, not the foundation.

**Cool down / stretching:** Light activity to bring heart rate down + static stretching of major muscles worked. Reduces delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS) modestly.

**Session length:** 45-75 minutes is adequate for most purposes. Longer isn't better — intensity and consistency matter more than time.

## The Major Movements to Include

For a balanced strength program, cover these movement patterns:

- **Squat pattern:** Squat, goblet squat, leg press (quad-dominant)
- **Hip hinge:** Deadlift, Romanian deadlift, hip thrust (hamstring and glute dominant)
- **Horizontal push:** Bench press, push-up, dumbbell press
- **Horizontal pull:** Row (barbell, dumbbell, cable, or body weight)
- **Vertical push:** Overhead press (barbell or dumbbell)
- **Vertical pull:** Pull-up, lat pulldown, chin-up
- **Core:** Plank, dead bug, anti-rotation exercises

A program that covers all of these develops balanced strength and reduces injury risk.

## Cardio Integration

If you're doing both strength and cardio:
- Doing cardio immediately before a heavy strength session reduces strength performance
- For general health, separate sessions (morning and evening, or different days) works well
- Low-intensity cardio (walking) doesn't meaningfully interfere with strength training
- High-intensity cardio on the same day as heavy leg training is more problematic than upper body strength days

Many people do cardio on off days from strength training — effective and simple.

## Simple Beginner Programs

**StrongLifts 5×5:** Three exercises per session (squat, bench or press, deadlift or row). 5 sets of 5 reps. Add weight every session. Alternates between two workouts. Simple, proven, and effective for building strength quickly.

**Reddit's Basic Beginner Routine:** Similar in structure, widely documented, beginner-friendly.

**Couch to 5K (C25K):** A structured running program that takes someone from no running to 30 minutes continuous running over 9 weeks. Widely used, app-based.

You don't need to design an original program from scratch. Using a proven beginner program and following it consistently for several months produces better results than designing your own before you understand training principles.

---

## Tracking Progress

Track your workouts. At minimum: what exercise, what weight, how many sets and reps. This is how you apply progressive overload systematically — you know what you did last time and aim to beat it.

A simple notebook, spreadsheet, or app works. The act of tracking also increases consistency — it creates a feedback loop and a record of progress that reinforces the habit.
