## The Cardiovascular System: Heart and Blood

The cardiovascular system is the body's delivery network. It moves oxygen, nutrients, hormones, and immune cells to every tissue, and carries waste products away. Every other system depends on it — which is why cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death worldwide.

---

## The Components

**The heart:** A fist-sized muscle that pumps blood continuously. It has four chambers:
- *Right atrium and right ventricle:* Receive oxygen-depleted blood from the body and pump it to the lungs
- *Left atrium and left ventricle:* Receive oxygen-rich blood from the lungs and pump it to the entire body

The left ventricle is larger and more muscular — it has to pump blood against higher pressure to reach the whole body.

**Blood vessels:**
- *Arteries:* Carry blood away from the heart. Thick, muscular walls handle the high pressure of each heartbeat. The largest artery is the aorta, which carries blood out of the left ventricle.
- *Veins:* Carry blood back to the heart. Thinner walls, lower pressure. Contain one-way valves to prevent backflow. The largest veins — the venae cavae — return blood to the right atrium.
- *Capillaries:* Microscopic vessels where exchange actually happens. Walls only one cell thick allow oxygen, nutrients, and waste to pass between blood and surrounding tissues.

**Blood:** The transport medium. Blood is about 55% plasma (mostly water with proteins, hormones, and nutrients dissolved in it) and 45% cells:
- *Red blood cells (erythrocytes):* Carry oxygen using hemoglobin — a protein that binds oxygen in the lungs and releases it in tissues
- *White blood cells (leukocytes):* Immune cells that fight infection
- *Platelets:* Cell fragments that help blood clot when a vessel is damaged

## The Cardiac Cycle

The heart beats about 60-100 times per minute at rest — roughly 100,000 times per day. Each beat is a two-phase cycle:

**Diastole (relaxation):** The heart muscle relaxes and chambers fill with blood.

**Systole (contraction):** The heart muscle contracts. The ventricles squeeze, pushing blood out — to the lungs from the right ventricle, to the body from the left ventricle.

**Heart rate vs. stroke volume:** The amount of blood pumped per minute (cardiac output) depends on how fast the heart beats (heart rate) and how much it pumps per beat (stroke volume). Fit people tend to have lower resting heart rates because their hearts pump more blood per beat.

**Heart sounds (lub-dub):** The sounds of a stethoscope are the valves closing. The first sound (lub) is the mitral and tricuspid valves closing at the start of systole. The second sound (dub) is the aortic and pulmonary valves closing at the end of systole.

## Blood Pressure

Blood pressure is the force blood exerts against vessel walls. It's measured in two numbers:
- **Systolic pressure (top number):** Pressure during ventricular contraction
- **Diastolic pressure (bottom number):** Pressure between beats when the heart is relaxed

Normal: below 120/80 mmHg. High blood pressure (hypertension) damages vessel walls over time, increasing risk of heart attack, stroke, and kidney damage.

## Common Cardiovascular Conditions

**Coronary artery disease:** Plaque (atherosclerosis) builds up inside coronary arteries — the arteries that supply the heart muscle itself. Reduced blood flow causes chest pain (angina). Complete blockage causes a heart attack — part of the heart muscle dies from lack of oxygen.

**Heart attack (myocardial infarction):** A coronary artery is completely blocked, typically by a plaque rupture and blood clot. Time is critical — every minute without blood flow kills more heart muscle. Heart attack symptoms include chest pain or pressure, shortness of breath, pain radiating to the arm or jaw, sweating, nausea.

**Stroke:** Similar to a heart attack, but in the brain. A blood vessel is blocked or ruptures. Brain tissue dies. Symptoms: sudden numbness or weakness on one side, facial drooping, slurred speech, confusion, severe headache. FAST — Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call 911.

**Hypertension:** Often symptomless until damage is done. Regular measurement is the only way to know.

## Protecting Cardiovascular Health

The major modifiable risk factors for cardiovascular disease:
- High blood pressure
- High LDL cholesterol
- Smoking
- Diabetes
- Physical inactivity
- Obesity

Most of these respond to lifestyle changes — diet, exercise, not smoking. Medication helps when lifestyle alone isn't enough. The earlier you address these factors, the more damage you prevent.
