## The Immune System

Your body is constantly under attack — bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites, and abnormal cells are always present and potentially harmful. The immune system is your defense: a complex network of cells, tissues, and proteins that identifies threats and eliminates them while leaving healthy cells alone.

---

## Two Layers of Defense

**Innate immunity (non-specific):** The first response. Fast, immediate, but not targeted to specific pathogens.

Physical barriers: Skin (keeps most pathogens out), mucus membranes (trap and expel pathogens), cilia (sweep pathogens out of the respiratory tract), stomach acid (kills most pathogens in food).

Cellular response: When pathogens get past physical barriers, innate immune cells respond immediately:
- *Neutrophils:* The most common white blood cells. Rush to infection sites and engulf pathogens (phagocytosis).
- *Macrophages:* Engulf and destroy pathogens and cellular debris. Also display pathogen fragments to activate the adaptive immune response.
- *Natural killer cells:* Detect and destroy cells that are infected with viruses or have become cancerous.

Inflammation: A key innate immune response. Blood vessels dilate, more blood flows to the affected area, and fluid and immune cells flood the tissue. Causes redness, heat, swelling, and pain — but also creates conditions that help fight infection and initiate repair. Chronic inflammation, however, contributes to heart disease, diabetes, cancer, and autoimmune conditions.

**Adaptive immunity (specific):** The second layer, which takes days to fully activate but is precisely targeted and builds lasting memory.

---

## Adaptive Immunity: Lymphocytes

The adaptive immune system is built around two types of lymphocytes (white blood cells):

**B cells:** Produce antibodies — Y-shaped proteins that bind to specific antigens (molecules on the surface of pathogens). Antibodies neutralize pathogens, mark them for destruction, and activate other immune components. Each B cell is designed to recognize one specific antigen.

**T cells:** Two major types:
- *Helper T cells (CD4+):* Coordinate the immune response. They activate B cells to produce antibodies and stimulate cytotoxic T cells.
- *Cytotoxic T cells (CD8+):* Kill cells directly — infected cells, cancer cells, and transplanted foreign cells.

**How the adaptive immune response works:**

1. A macrophage engulfs a pathogen and displays fragments of it on its surface (antigen presentation)
2. Helper T cells with matching receptors recognize the fragment and activate
3. Activated helper T cells signal B cells and cytotoxic T cells to proliferate
4. B cells produce antibodies; cytotoxic T cells kill infected cells
5. The pathogen is eliminated

**Immunological memory:** After infection resolves, most immune cells die off, but some become memory cells — long-lived cells that remember the pathogen. If the same pathogen appears again, the immune response is faster and stronger, often eliminating it before you feel sick. This is why you only get chickenpox once. It's also how vaccines work.

---

## Vaccines

Vaccines train the immune system to recognize a pathogen without causing disease. They do this by exposing the immune system to:
- A weakened or killed form of the pathogen
- A piece of the pathogen (a protein or fragment)
- Instructions for the body to produce a piece of the pathogen (mRNA vaccines)

The immune system mounts a response, creates memory cells, and is prepared to fight the real pathogen rapidly if exposure occurs later. No currently used vaccine contains live, fully virulent pathogens that can cause the disease they protect against.

## When the Immune System Goes Wrong

**Autoimmune diseases:** The immune system attacks the body's own tissues. Examples:
- *Rheumatoid arthritis:* Attacks joint linings
- *Type 1 diabetes:* Attacks insulin-producing cells in the pancreas
- *Lupus:* Can attack multiple organ systems
- *Multiple sclerosis:* Attacks myelin around nerve fibers

**Allergies:** An exaggerated immune response to harmless substances. The immune system treats pollen, peanut proteins, or cat dander as dangerous pathogens. In anaphylaxis (severe allergic reaction), this response is life-threatening — epinephrine (an EpiPen) is the emergency treatment.

**Immunodeficiency:** When the immune system doesn't function adequately. HIV destroys helper T cells, progressively weakening the immune system until AIDS develops. Other immunodeficiencies can be inherited or caused by medications (chemotherapy, immunosuppressants used in transplant patients).

---

## Supporting Immune Function

- **Sleep:** Cytokines (immune signaling proteins) are produced during sleep. Chronic sleep deprivation impairs immune response.
- **Nutrition:** Vitamins C, D, and zinc are particularly important for immune function.
- **Exercise:** Moderate exercise improves immune surveillance; excessive exercise without recovery can temporarily suppress immunity.
- **Stress management:** Chronic stress suppresses immune function through cortisol and other stress hormones.
- **Vaccination:** The most effective way to prime the immune system against specific dangerous pathogens.
