## The Nervous System: Brain and Nerves

The nervous system is the body's communication and control network. It receives input from the environment, processes it, and coordinates responses — from breathing and heartbeat to thought, emotion, and movement. Everything you experience as a conscious being happens in this system.

---

## Two Main Divisions

**Central nervous system (CNS):** The brain and spinal cord. The CNS receives, processes, and integrates all information. It's the decision-maker — the seat of thought, memory, emotion, and voluntary control.

**Peripheral nervous system (PNS):** All the nerves outside the brain and spinal cord. The PNS is the communication highway between the CNS and the rest of the body — carrying signals in (sensory information) and out (motor commands).

The PNS is further divided:

**Somatic nervous system:** Controls voluntary skeletal muscle movement and carries sensory information (touch, temperature, pain, vision, etc.) to the brain.

**Autonomic nervous system (ANS):** Controls involuntary functions — heart rate, digestion, breathing, blood pressure, gland secretions. The ANS has two branches:
- *Sympathetic:* "Fight or flight." Activates in response to stress or threat. Increases heart rate, dilates airways, redirects blood to muscles, suppresses digestion.
- *Parasympathetic:* "Rest and digest." Active during calm states. Slows heart rate, promotes digestion, conserves energy.

---

## The Neuron

The neuron is the nerve cell — the basic unit of the nervous system. The human brain contains roughly 86 billion neurons.

**Dendrites:** Branch-like extensions that receive signals from other neurons.

**Cell body (soma):** Contains the nucleus and processes incoming signals.

**Axon:** A long extension that transmits the signal outward toward other neurons, muscles, or glands. Some axons are over a meter long (from the spinal cord to the foot).

**Myelin sheath:** A fatty coating around many axons that dramatically speeds up signal transmission. Multiple sclerosis (MS) is a disease that destroys myelin, causing progressive loss of nervous system function.

**Synapses:** The gaps between neurons. Neurons communicate by releasing neurotransmitters (chemical messengers) across synapses. Common neurotransmitters include dopamine (reward, motivation), serotonin (mood, sleep), norepinephrine (alertness, stress response), and GABA (inhibition, calming). Many psychiatric medications work by altering neurotransmitter levels.

## The Brain

**Cerebral cortex:** The outer layer — the wrinkled gray matter. Site of higher functions: thought, language, memory, perception, decision-making. Divided into four lobes:
- *Frontal:* Planning, decision-making, impulse control, personality, voluntary movement
- *Parietal:* Processing sensory input from skin (touch, temperature, pain, pressure), spatial awareness
- *Temporal:* Hearing, language comprehension, memory formation
- *Occipital:* Vision

**Limbic system:** Deeper structures involved in emotion, motivation, and memory. Includes the amygdala (emotional processing, especially fear) and hippocampus (converting short-term memories to long-term storage).

**Brainstem:** Controls vital involuntary functions: breathing, heart rate, blood pressure, sleep. Also where many cranial nerves originate. Damage to the brainstem is typically fatal.

**Cerebellum:** Coordinates movement, balance, and fine motor control. Integrates sensory input to produce smooth, coordinated movements. Alcohol particularly affects the cerebellum — which is why intoxication causes unsteady gait and impaired coordination.

## Common Neurological Conditions

**Stroke:** Blood flow to part of the brain is blocked or a vessel ruptures. Brain cells begin dying within minutes without oxygen. Effects depend on location — speech, movement, or cognition may be affected. Time to treatment is critical.

**Alzheimer's disease:** Progressive degeneration of neurons, particularly in memory-forming areas. Plaques and tangles accumulate in brain tissue. No cure, but some medications slow progression.

**Parkinson's disease:** Loss of dopamine-producing neurons in the substantia nigra. Causes tremors, rigidity, and slowed movement. Dopamine replacement therapies help manage symptoms.

**Epilepsy:** Abnormal, synchronized electrical activity in the brain — seizures. Many types, from brief absence seizures to full convulsive events. Controlled with anti-epileptic medications in most cases.

---

## Protecting Your Nervous System

The brain needs a constant, uninterrupted supply of oxygen and glucose — it has almost no energy reserves. Anything that disrupts blood flow (stroke, cardiac arrest) causes rapid, irreversible damage.

Sleep is critical for nervous system health — the brain clears metabolic waste products during sleep through the glymphatic system. Chronic sleep deprivation impairs cognition, memory consolidation, and emotional regulation. Exercise increases blood flow and promotes neuroplasticity — the brain's ability to rewire and adapt.
