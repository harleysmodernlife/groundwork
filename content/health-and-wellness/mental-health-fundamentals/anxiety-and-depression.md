## Anxiety and Depression: What They Are

Anxiety and depression are the two most common mental health conditions. Most people have a vague sense of what they are — but that vague sense often doesn't match what these conditions actually look like or feel like, leading to underrecognition and delayed help.

---

## Anxiety Disorders

Anxiety is a normal emotion — a response to perceived threat that prepares the body for action. This is adaptive. The problem is when anxiety becomes excessive, persistent, or disproportionate to the actual threat, and when it significantly interferes with daily life.

**Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD):** Persistent, excessive worry about multiple areas of life (work, health, family, money, everyday events) that's difficult to control, present most days, and accompanied by physical symptoms like restlessness, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, irritability, muscle tension, and sleep problems. This isn't occasional worry — it's a near-constant state that's hard to turn off.

**Panic Disorder:** Recurrent unexpected panic attacks — sudden surges of intense fear with physical symptoms including racing heart, sweating, trembling, shortness of breath, chest pain, dizziness, and fear of dying or "going crazy." People often think they're having a heart attack. After attacks, people often develop ongoing worry about having more attacks and change their behavior to avoid triggering situations.

**Social Anxiety Disorder:** Intense fear of social or performance situations where one might be scrutinized, judged, or embarrassed. Not shyness — it's severe enough to avoid important social situations, cause significant distress, and impair work or relationships.

**Specific Phobias:** Intense, irrational fear of specific objects or situations (heights, dogs, needles, flying, blood) that causes avoidance and impairs functioning.

**PTSD and OCD** are related conditions covered separately.

**Physical manifestations of anxiety:** Anxiety doesn't just feel emotional — it produces real physical symptoms: muscle tension, headaches, gastrointestinal problems, chest tightness, fatigue, heart palpitations. Many people with anxiety first seek help for physical symptoms, not realizing the cause.

## Depression

Depression (major depressive disorder) is more than sadness. It's a pervasive condition that affects mood, thinking, physical function, and behavior. It often feels like numbness, emptiness, or the inability to feel pleasure more than it feels like "being sad."

**Core symptoms:** A depressive episode requires either depressed mood or loss of interest/pleasure (anhedonia) most of the day, nearly every day, for at least two weeks, along with at least four of:
- Significant weight change or appetite disturbance
- Sleep problems (insomnia or hypersomnia)
- Psychomotor changes (moving or speaking slower, or agitation)
- Fatigue and loss of energy
- Feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt
- Difficulty thinking, concentrating, or making decisions
- Recurrent thoughts of death or suicidal thoughts

**What it actually feels like:** People with depression often describe it not as intense sadness but as a void — nothing feels meaningful or enjoyable, everything requires enormous effort, the future feels hopeless, and basic functioning (getting out of bed, showering, eating) can feel overwhelming.

**Depression is not a choice or a mood.** Telling someone with depression to "cheer up," "think positive," or "just get outside more" is roughly equivalent to telling someone with a broken leg to "just walk it off."

**Course and duration:** A depressive episode typically lasts months if untreated. Many people have recurrent episodes. Some people develop persistent depressive disorder (dysthymia) — a less severe but chronic form lasting at least two years.

## What Causes These Conditions

The causes are complex and interacting:

**Biological:** Disruptions in serotonin, dopamine, norepinephrine, GABA, and other neurotransmitter systems. Structural and functional brain changes. Genetic predisposition (depression and anxiety both run in families). Hormonal factors (postpartum depression, thyroid disorders).

**Psychological:** Thinking patterns (catastrophizing, negative self-talk, avoidance behaviors) that perpetuate and intensify symptoms. Learned responses to stress.

**Environmental:** Trauma, adverse childhood experiences, chronic stress, loss, isolation, abuse. Economic hardship and instability.

These interact — genes load the gun, environment pulls the trigger, and psychological patterns can be what keeps it firing.

## Treatment

Anxiety disorders and depression are among the most treatable mental health conditions. Treatment typically involves:

**Psychotherapy:** Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT) is strongly evidence-based for both anxiety and depression. It works by identifying and changing thought patterns and behaviors that maintain symptoms. Other effective approaches include exposure therapy (for anxiety), behavioral activation, and others.

**Medication:** SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors) and SNRIs are first-line medications for both anxiety and depression. They often take 4-6 weeks to show full effect. Other medication classes are used when these don't work adequately.

**Combination:** Often more effective than either alone.

**Lifestyle:** Exercise has substantial evidence as an adjunct treatment for depression in particular — effect sizes comparable to antidepressants in some studies. Sleep, social connection, and reduced alcohol use also matter.

If symptoms are persistent and impairing, these conditions warrant professional evaluation and treatment, not just willpower.
