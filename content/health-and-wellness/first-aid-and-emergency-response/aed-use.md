## AED Use

An AED — automated external defibrillator — is a device that analyzes the heart's electrical activity and, if appropriate, delivers an electric shock to restore normal rhythm. It's designed to be used by bystanders with no medical training. Combining CPR with an AED dramatically increases survival from cardiac arrest.

---

## Why AEDs Matter

Most sudden cardiac arrests are caused by ventricular fibrillation (VF) — a chaotic, disorganized electrical rhythm where the heart quivers instead of pumping. CPR circulates blood, but it doesn't fix VF. Only a defibrillator can.

The probability of survival from VF decreases by approximately 10% for every minute without defibrillation. After 10 minutes with no defibrillation, survival is unlikely. With CPR plus early defibrillation, survival rates in some settings exceed 50%.

AEDs are designed to be life-saving tools anyone can use — they speak to you in plain language, analyze the rhythm themselves, and will not deliver a shock unless a shockable rhythm is present. You cannot accidentally shock someone who doesn't need it.

---

## Finding an AED

AEDs are required or strongly recommended in many public places:
- Airports
- Gyms and fitness centers
- Shopping malls
- Schools
- Office buildings
- Sports arenas
- Hotels
- Churches

They're typically stored in bright orange or green cases mounted on walls, often near public spaces, entrances, or near emergency equipment. Look for the AED symbol (a heart with a lightning bolt) or signage.

When 911 is called, ask the dispatcher where the nearest AED is — they often know.

## How to Use an AED: Step by Step

**Step 1: Call 911 and start CPR**

While someone else retrieves the AED, continue CPR. Don't stop compressions to go get the AED yourself if you're the only person doing CPR — someone else should get it.

**Step 2: Turn on the AED**

Open the case and turn it on — either a power button or it powers on automatically when you open the lid. The AED will begin talking and giving instructions. Follow them.

**Step 3: Expose the chest**

Remove or cut clothing from the chest. Dry the chest if the person is wet (moisture conducts electricity across the chest rather than through it). If the person has a lot of chest hair, the pads may not adhere — shave if a razor is in the AED kit, or press firmly and accept reduced contact.

**Step 4: Attach the pads**

The AED will have two pads with diagrams showing placement:
- **One pad:** Upper right chest (below the collarbone, to the right of the breastbone)
- **One pad:** Lower left side (below and to the left of the nipple, on the side of the chest)

Peel backing off and press firmly to bare skin. The AED will often have pictures on the pads themselves.

**Special situations:**
- *Implanted pacemaker or ICD:* You'll see a small lump under the skin. Don't place a pad directly over it — place it at least 1 inch away.
- *Medication patch:* Remove it (with gloves if possible) before placing pads.
- *Children under 8 or 55 lbs:* Use pediatric pads if available (smaller with a dose-attenuating cable). If only adult pads are available, use them — it's better than nothing.

**Step 5: Let the AED analyze**

The AED will say "Analyzing heart rhythm — do not touch the patient." Everyone must be clear of the victim during analysis. The AED needs still contact with the person — movement or touching can interfere with the reading.

**Step 6: Deliver shock if advised**

If a shockable rhythm is detected, the AED will charge and tell you to deliver a shock. It will say something like "Shock advised. Stand clear. Press the flashing button."

Loudly announce: "Everyone clear!" — and visually confirm no one is touching the victim before pressing the shock button.

**If no shock is advised:** The AED has determined the heart is not in a shockable rhythm. Continue CPR immediately — the AED will tell you when to resume.

**Step 7: Resume CPR immediately after shock**

Immediately after the shock, resume CPR starting with compressions — even if the person appears to have recovered. The AED will guide you to analyze again after 2 minutes of CPR.

---

## Continuing Until Help Arrives

Follow the AED's prompts — it will continue guiding you through cycles of CPR and analysis until:
- The person shows clear signs of recovery
- Professional help takes over
- You're unable to continue

Leave the AED attached and powered on when paramedics arrive — it provides them with rhythm analysis data.

---

## Getting Comfortable in Advance

AEDs are specifically designed to require no training — the device does the thinking. But being unfamiliar with the device in a high-stress situation creates hesitation.

Taking a CPR/AED course, or even just watching AED training videos, helps you act faster under stress. Many courses can be completed in 2-3 hours and make a real difference in how quickly you can act.
