## Overdose and Poisoning Response

Overdose and poisoning are medical emergencies. The response depends on the substance, the route of exposure, and the person's condition. Most cases require calling 911 — but there are specific actions that save lives while waiting.

---

## General Principles

**Call 911 for:**
- Any loss of consciousness
- Difficulty breathing or stopped breathing
- Seizures
- Confusion or extreme agitation
- Suspected large ingestion or unknown substance

**Identify the substance if possible.** Tell paramedics what was taken, when, how much, and by what route. Bring the container, pill bottle, or substance to the hospital. This directly affects treatment.

**Do not induce vomiting** unless specifically instructed by Poison Control or a physician. For some substances, vomiting causes additional harm (corrosives burn the esophagus on the way back up; some substances absorb faster when vomited).

**Poison Control Center:** 1-800-222-1222 (U.S.). Available 24/7 with expert guidance for poisoning situations that don't appear immediately life-threatening. They can tell you what to do and whether emergency care is needed.

---

## Opioid Overdose

Opioids include heroin, fentanyl, prescription painkillers (oxycodone, hydrocodone, morphine, codeine), and many others. Opioid overdose is the leading cause of accidental death in the United States.

**Recognizing opioid overdose:**
- Unconscious or unresponsive
- Slow, shallow, or stopped breathing
- Gurgling or choking sounds (sometimes called the "death rattle")
- Blue or purple lips and fingernails (cyanosis)
- Pinpoint (very small) pupils
- Pale, clammy skin

**Responding to opioid overdose:**

1. **Try to wake the person.** Sternal rub (knuckles rubbed firmly on the breastbone) or pinching.

2. **Call 911.**

3. **Administer naloxone (Narcan) if available.** Naloxone is a medication that rapidly reverses opioid overdose. It's available without a prescription at most pharmacies.

*Nasal spray (Narcan):* Tilt head back, support neck, spray one dose in one nostril. If no response after 2-3 minutes, give a second dose in the other nostril. May repeat every 2-3 minutes.

*Injectable:* Follow product instructions. Administer into the muscle of the outer thigh or upper arm.

4. **Begin rescue breathing if not breathing.** Tilt head back, lift chin, seal mouth and deliver one breath every 5 seconds, watching for chest rise. If no pulse, begin CPR.

5. **Recovery position** if breathing but unconscious: Roll them onto their side (left side if possible) to prevent choking on vomit.

**Important:** Naloxone wears off in 30-90 minutes — shorter than many opioids. The person can go back into overdose. Emergency care is required even if naloxone works.

**Good Samaritan laws:** Most U.S. states have laws that provide some legal protection to people who call 911 for an overdose. Don't let fear of legal consequences stop you from calling for help.

## Alcohol Poisoning

Alcohol is a depressant that suppresses breathing at high doses.

**Signs of alcohol poisoning:**
- Unconscious and can't be woken
- Breathing is slow (fewer than 8 breaths per minute), irregular, or stopped
- Vomiting while unconscious
- Seizures
- Pale, cold, bluish skin
- Very low body temperature (hypothermia)

**Call 911.** Don't leave the person alone.

**Recovery position:** If unconscious but breathing, roll onto their side to prevent choking on vomit.

**What not to do:**
- Coffee does not sober someone up — caffeine just makes a drunk person more awake
- Cold showers don't help and can cause hypothermia
- "Sleeping it off" is appropriate for mild intoxication, not alcohol poisoning
- Time is the only way alcohol metabolizes — but with alcohol poisoning, the person needs medical monitoring

## Carbon Monoxide Poisoning

Carbon monoxide (CO) is colorless and odorless — undetectable without a detector. Sources: malfunctioning furnaces, gas appliances, cars running in enclosed spaces, generators.

**Symptoms:** Headache, dizziness, confusion, nausea, vomiting — often described as flu-like without fever. Multiple people in the same space becoming sick simultaneously is a red flag.

**Response:**
1. Get out immediately. Leave doors open as you exit.
2. Call 911 from outside.
3. Don't go back in.
4. Anyone who was exposed needs medical evaluation — CO poisoning can have delayed effects.

**Prevention:** CO detectors on every floor, especially near sleeping areas.

## Chemical and Household Product Ingestion

**If swallowed:** Call Poison Control (1-800-222-1222). Don't induce vomiting unless directed. If they're unconscious or having trouble breathing, call 911.

**If on skin:** Remove contaminated clothing (protect yourself from exposure). Flush skin with large amounts of water for 15-20 minutes.

**If inhaled:** Move to fresh air. Call Poison Control or 911 for significant exposure.

**If in eyes:** Flush with water for 15-20 minutes — hold eye open and pour water across it. Seek medical care after flushing.
