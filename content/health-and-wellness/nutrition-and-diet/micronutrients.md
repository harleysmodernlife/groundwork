## Micronutrients: Vitamins and Minerals

Macronutrients get the most attention, but micronutrients — vitamins and minerals — are equally essential. They don't provide energy, but they enable almost every process in the body. Deficiency causes real, measurable disease.

---

## What Vitamins Are

Vitamins are organic compounds (made by plants, animals, or bacteria) that the body needs in small amounts and can't make in sufficient quantities itself. They're divided into two categories:

**Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K):** Dissolve in fat, stored in fatty tissues and the liver. Because they're stored, deficiency takes longer to develop — but toxicity (from supplement overdose) is also possible. They require dietary fat for absorption.

**Water-soluble vitamins (C and the B vitamins):** Dissolve in water, not significantly stored (except B12). Excess is excreted in urine. Deficiency can develop faster than with fat-soluble vitamins, but toxicity from food sources is rare.

---

## Key Vitamins

**Vitamin A:** Vision (particularly night vision), immune function, cell growth, skin health. Sources: liver, dairy, eggs; beta-carotene in orange/yellow vegetables (carrots, sweet potato, squash) is converted to vitamin A. Deficiency causes night blindness; severe deficiency is a leading cause of preventable blindness in children worldwide.

**Vitamin D:** Calcium absorption, bone health, immune function, muscle function. Also functions as a hormone. Produced in skin when exposed to UVB sunlight. Sources: fatty fish, fortified dairy, eggs. Deficiency is extremely common (especially in northern climates and among people who spend little time outdoors). Linked to rickets in children (soft, deformed bones) and osteoporosis in adults.

**Vitamin E:** Antioxidant — protects cells from oxidative damage. Sources: vegetable oils, nuts, seeds, leafy greens.

**Vitamin K:** Blood clotting, bone metabolism. Two main forms: K1 in leafy greens; K2 in fermented foods and some animal products. Blood thinners like warfarin work by blocking vitamin K's role in clotting.

**Vitamin C:** Antioxidant, immune function, collagen synthesis (connective tissue, skin, wound healing), iron absorption. Sources: citrus, peppers, strawberries, broccoli. Deficiency causes scurvy — weakness, gum disease, bleeding — historically significant in sailors without access to fresh produce.

**B vitamins** (a family of 8): Collectively involved in energy metabolism, DNA synthesis, nerve function, and red blood cell production.
- *B12:* Nerve function, red blood cell production, DNA. Found almost exclusively in animal products — vegans and vegetarians need supplementation or fortified foods. Deficiency causes nerve damage (peripheral neuropathy) and megaloblastic anemia.
- *Folate (B9):* DNA synthesis, cell division. Critical during pregnancy — deficiency causes neural tube defects (spina bifida) in the developing fetus. Pregnant women need 400-800 mcg/day.
- *B6:* Protein metabolism, neurotransmitter production.
- *Thiamine (B1):* Energy metabolism, nerve function. Severe deficiency causes beriberi; deficiency in alcoholics causes Wernicke's encephalopathy (serious neurological damage).

## Key Minerals

**Calcium:** Bone and teeth structure (99% of the body's calcium is in bone), muscle contraction, nerve signaling, blood clotting. Sources: dairy, fortified plant milks, leafy greens, canned fish with bones. Deficiency leads to osteoporosis over time.

**Iron:** Essential component of hemoglobin (carries oxygen in blood). Sources: red meat (heme iron, highly bioavailable), legumes, spinach, fortified cereals (non-heme iron, less bioavailable — absorption improved by vitamin C). Iron deficiency anemia is the most common nutritional deficiency worldwide. Symptoms: fatigue, weakness, pale skin, shortness of breath.

**Magnesium:** Involved in over 300 enzymatic reactions — energy production, protein synthesis, muscle and nerve function, blood sugar regulation. Sources: nuts, seeds, leafy greens, whole grains. Often underconsumed.

**Zinc:** Immune function, wound healing, enzyme function, protein synthesis, smell and taste. Sources: meat, shellfish, legumes, nuts, seeds. Deficiency impairs immune response and growth.

**Potassium:** Fluid balance, muscle contraction, nerve signals, blood pressure regulation. Sources: bananas, potatoes, leafy greens, beans, dairy. Adequate potassium is associated with lower blood pressure.

**Sodium:** Fluid balance, nerve and muscle function. The body requires some sodium, but the typical American diet provides far more than needed. Excess sodium contributes to high blood pressure in sodium-sensitive individuals.

**Iodine:** Required for thyroid hormone synthesis. Thyroid hormones regulate metabolism, growth, and development. Iodine deficiency causes goiter (enlarged thyroid) and hypothyroidism. Iodized salt has dramatically reduced iodine deficiency in many countries.

## Getting Micronutrients from Food

A varied diet of whole foods — vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds, and modest amounts of animal products — provides most people with adequate micronutrients.

The nutrients most commonly insufficient in Western diets:
- Vitamin D (limited sun exposure + few dietary sources)
- Calcium (dairy avoidance without alternatives)
- Magnesium (low vegetable and whole grain consumption)
- Potassium (low vegetable and fruit consumption)
- Fiber (technically not a vitamin or mineral, but consistently underconsumed)

Supplements can address specific deficiencies, but food sources are generally preferable — food provides micronutrients in combinations with other compounds that improve absorption and function.
