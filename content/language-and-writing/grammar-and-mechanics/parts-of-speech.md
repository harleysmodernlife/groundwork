Every word in English belongs to a category called a **part of speech**. These categories describe what a word does in a sentence — what job it performs. Knowing the parts of speech gives you the vocabulary to understand grammar rules, identify errors, and make deliberate choices in your writing.

---

## 1. Nouns

A **noun** names a person, place, thing, or idea.

- **Common nouns**: General names — *city, teacher, dog, freedom*
- **Proper nouns**: Specific names, always capitalized — *Chicago, Ms. Rivera, Labrador*
- **Concrete nouns**: Things you can sense — *table, rain, music*
- **Abstract nouns**: Concepts and ideas — *justice, happiness, time*
- **Collective nouns**: Name a group as a unit — *team, flock, jury*

Nouns function as subjects, objects, and complements in sentences.

---

## 2. Pronouns

A **pronoun** replaces a noun to avoid repetition. The noun it replaces is called its **antecedent**.

Types of pronouns:
- **Personal**: *I, you, he, she, it, we, they*
- **Possessive**: *my, your, his, her, its, our, their*
- **Reflexive**: *myself, yourself, himself, themselves*
- **Relative**: *who, whom, which, that* (connect clauses)
- **Indefinite**: *someone, each, everyone, none, all*
- **Demonstrative**: *this, that, these, those*

Pronoun **agreement**: Pronouns must agree with their antecedents in number and gender. *Everyone finished **their** homework* is now widely accepted as correct, using the singular "their" for gender-neutral reference.

---

## 3. Verbs

A **verb** expresses action or state of being. It is the engine of every sentence.

- **Action verbs**: *run, write, consider, decide*
- **Linking verbs**: Connect subject to a description — *is, are, was, seem, become, feel*
- **Helping (auxiliary) verbs**: Precede main verbs — *have, has, will, would, can, should, must*

Verbs carry **tense** (when the action occurs), **voice** (active vs. passive), and **mood** (indicative, imperative, subjunctive).

---

## 4. Adjectives

An **adjective** modifies a noun or pronoun — it describes, quantifies, or identifies it.

*The **tall** woman held **three** heavy boxes.*

Adjectives answer: *What kind? How many? Which one?*

**Articles** (*a, an, the*) are technically adjectives. *The* is the definite article (a specific thing); *a/an* are indefinite articles (any one of a kind).

Adjectives can be **comparative** (*taller*) or **superlative** (*tallest*).

---

## 5. Adverbs

An **adverb** modifies a verb, an adjective, or another adverb. It answers *how, when, where, how much, or to what degree*.

- Modifying a verb: *She ran **quickly**.*
- Modifying an adjective: *He was **unusually** calm.*
- Modifying an adverb: *She spoke **very** softly.*

Many adverbs end in *-ly* (softly, carefully) but not all: *soon, here, often, never, just, very*.

---

## 6. Prepositions

A **preposition** shows the relationship between a noun (or pronoun) and another word in the sentence — typically indicating location, direction, time, or manner.

*The book is **on** the shelf. She left **before** noon. He walked **through** the door.*

Common prepositions: *in, on, at, by, for, with, about, between, through, during, under, over, after, before*

A preposition is always followed by its **object** (a noun or pronoun): *on **the shelf**, before **noon***.

---

## 7. Conjunctions

A **conjunction** joins words, phrases, or clauses.

- **Coordinating conjunctions** (FANBOYS): *for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so* — join equal elements
  - *I like coffee, **but** she prefers tea.*
- **Subordinating conjunctions**: Introduce dependent clauses — *because, although, if, when, since, unless*
  - ***Because** it was raining, we stayed inside.*
- **Correlative conjunctions**: Work in pairs — *both/and, either/or, neither/nor, not only/but also*

---

## 8. Interjections

An **interjection** expresses emotion and has no grammatical connection to the rest of the sentence.

*Oh! Well, that's surprising. Ouch! Hey, wait.*

Strong interjections take an exclamation mark; mild ones take a comma.

---

## Why Parts of Speech Matter

The same word can function as different parts of speech depending on context:

- *I need a **light** jacket.* (adjective)
- *Please **light** the candle.* (verb)
- *Turn on the **light**.* (noun)

Identifying the part of speech a word is performing — not just the word itself — is what allows you to apply grammar rules correctly and communicate with precision.

---

## Summary

The eight parts of speech are nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions, and interjections. Each describes the function a word performs in a sentence. Mastering them gives you the foundation for understanding grammar, diagnosing errors, and making deliberate choices in your writing.
