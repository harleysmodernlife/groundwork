## Parallel Lines and Transversals

When a line called a **transversal** crosses two parallel lines, it creates eight angles. These angles have predictable relationships — specific pairs are equal, others are supplementary — and these relationships can be used to find unknown angles and prove that lines are parallel. This is one of the most systematic topics in geometry and the basis for understanding polygon angles.

---

## Parallel Lines: Definition and Notation

Two lines in the same plane are **parallel** if they never intersect, no matter how far extended. Parallel lines have the same slope (in coordinate geometry) and maintain a constant distance from each other.

Notation: l ∥ m means line l is parallel to line m.

The symbol ∥ is a pair of vertical bars. On diagrams, parallel lines are marked with matching arrow symbols (→ on each line).

---

## Transversals

A **transversal** is any line that intersects two or more other lines at different points.

When a transversal crosses two parallel lines:
- 8 angles are formed (4 at each intersection)
- These angles have specific names based on their positions

---

## The Eight Angles

Label the two parallel lines as p and q, and the transversal as t. At the first intersection (with p), label the angles 1, 2, 3, 4 going clockwise from the upper-right. At the second intersection (with q), label them 5, 6, 7, 8 the same way.

```
           1 | 2
          ---+---  line p
           4 | 3

           5 | 6
          ---+---  line q
           8 | 7
```

**Interior angles:** Between the two parallel lines — angles 3, 4, 5, 6.
**Exterior angles:** Outside the parallel lines — angles 1, 2, 7, 8.

---

## Angle Pairs and Their Relationships

### Corresponding Angles (Equal)

Corresponding angles are in the same position at each intersection — both upper-right, both lower-left, etc.
- ∠1 and ∠5 are corresponding (both upper-right)
- ∠2 and ∠6, ∠3 and ∠7, ∠4 and ∠8 are corresponding pairs

**When lines are parallel:** Corresponding angles are congruent (equal). This is the Corresponding Angles Postulate.

### Alternate Interior Angles (Equal)

Alternate interior angles are between the parallel lines, on opposite sides of the transversal.
- ∠3 and ∠6 are alternate interior angles
- ∠4 and ∠5 are alternate interior angles

**When lines are parallel:** Alternate interior angles are congruent. They form a Z-pattern on the diagram.

### Alternate Exterior Angles (Equal)

Alternate exterior angles are outside the parallel lines, on opposite sides of the transversal.
- ∠1 and ∠8 are alternate exterior angles
- ∠2 and ∠7 are alternate exterior angles

**When lines are parallel:** Alternate exterior angles are congruent.

### Co-Interior (Same-Side Interior) Angles (Supplementary)

Also called consecutive interior angles or co-interior angles. They are between the parallel lines, on the same side of the transversal.
- ∠3 and ∠5 are co-interior angles
- ∠4 and ∠6 are co-interior angles

**When lines are parallel:** Co-interior angles are supplementary (sum to 180°). They form a C-pattern on the diagram.

---

## Summary Table

| Pair Type | Position | When Lines Are Parallel |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Corresponding | Same position, same side | Equal |
| Alternate Interior | Between lines, opposite sides | Equal |
| Alternate Exterior | Outside lines, opposite sides | Equal |
| Co-Interior (Same-Side Interior) | Between lines, same side | Supplementary |
| Linear Pair | Adjacent, at same intersection | Always supplementary (180°) |
| Vertical | Across from each other | Always equal |

## Using These Relationships to Find Angles

**Example:** Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One angle is 65°. Find all eight angles.

If the known angle is ∠1 = 65°:
- ∠2 = 180° - 65° = 115° (linear pair)
- ∠3 = 65° (vertical angles with ∠1)
- ∠4 = 115° (vertical angles with ∠2)
- ∠5 = 65° (corresponding to ∠1)
- ∠6 = 115° (corresponding to ∠2, or linear pair with ∠5)
- ∠7 = 65° (corresponding to ∠3, or vertical with ∠5)
- ∠8 = 115° (corresponding to ∠4)

The eight angles come in only two distinct measures: the original angle and its supplement.

**Example with algebra:** Two parallel lines cut by a transversal. Alternate interior angles are (3x + 10)° and (5x - 14)°. Find x.
- Alternate interior angles are equal: 3x + 10 = 5x - 14
- 24 = 2x → x = **12**
- Angle = 3(12) + 10 = **46°**

**Example with co-interior angles:** Co-interior angles are (2x + 30)° and (4x - 10)°. Find x.
- Co-interior angles are supplementary: (2x + 30) + (4x - 10) = 180
- 6x + 20 = 180 → 6x = 160 → x = **80/3 ≈ 26.7**
- Angles: 2(80/3) + 30 = 83.3° and 4(80/3) - 10 = 96.7°. Check: 83.3 + 96.7 = 180°. ✓

---

## Proving Lines Are Parallel

The relationships work in reverse: if you can show a pair of angles satisfies one of these conditions, you can conclude the lines are parallel.

**Converses:**
- If corresponding angles are equal → lines are parallel
- If alternate interior angles are equal → lines are parallel
- If co-interior angles are supplementary → lines are parallel

**Example:** A transversal crosses two lines. It creates alternate interior angles of 73° and 73°. Can you conclude the lines are parallel?
- Yes. Equal alternate interior angles are proof of parallel lines.

---

## The Triangle Angle Sum (Derived from Parallel Lines)

The fact that a triangle's angles sum to 180° can be proven using parallel lines. Draw a line through one vertex parallel to the opposite side. The angles of the triangle correspond to angles in the parallel-line configuration, which sum to 180° along the straight line.

## Practice

1. Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One angle is 117°. Find the measure of each of the other seven angles.
2. Alternate interior angles are (6x - 5)° and (4x + 25)°. Find x and both angle measures.
3. Co-interior angles are (3x + 12)° and (5x + 8)°. Find x.
4. If corresponding angles measure (2x + 8)° and (3x - 12)°, find x.
5. What type of angle pair is formed by angles that are between the parallel lines and on opposite sides of the transversal?

**Answers:**
1. Other angles: 117°, 63°, 117°, 63°, 117°, 63°, 117° (four at 117°, four at 63°, alternating around each intersection).
2. 6x - 5 = 4x + 25 → 2x = 30 → x = **15**. Angles: 6(15)-5 = **85°** each.
3. 3x + 12 + 5x + 8 = 180 → 8x + 20 = 180 → 8x = 160 → x = **20**.
4. 2x + 8 = 3x - 12 → 20 = x → x = **20**. Angles = 48°.
5. **Alternate interior angles.**
