## Angles and Angle Relationships

An angle is formed when two rays share a common endpoint. The shared endpoint is the **vertex**, and the two rays are the **sides** of the angle. Angles are measured in degrees and are named using the vertex or by three points: the vertex is always in the middle. Angle ABC (written ∠ABC) has vertex at B.

---

## Measuring Angles with a Protractor

A protractor is a semicircular measuring tool marked 0° to 180°. To measure an angle:
1. Place the center of the protractor on the vertex.
2. Align one ray with the 0° line.
3. Read the degree where the other ray crosses the scale.

Use the inner scale if measuring from the right, the outer scale if from the left. Angles can be measured to any degree of precision.

---

## Types of Angles

| Type | Measure | Visual |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Zero | 0° | Two rays overlap |
| Acute | 0° to 90° | Sharp, narrow |
| Right | exactly 90° | Perfect corner; marked with a small square |
| Obtuse | 90° to 180° | Blunt, wide |
| Straight | exactly 180° | A straight line |
| Reflex | 180° to 360° | More than half-rotation |

## Angle Relationships

These relationships allow you to find unknown angles using known ones — the foundation of geometric problem-solving.

### Complementary Angles

Two angles are **complementary** if they sum to 90°.

- 35° and 55° are complementary
- If ∠A = 28°, its complement = 90° - 28° = 62°

Complements don't have to be adjacent (sharing a side). Two angles are complementary if they add to 90° regardless of position.

### Supplementary Angles

Two angles are **supplementary** if they sum to 180°.

- 110° and 70° are supplementary
- If ∠B = 143°, its supplement = 180° - 143° = 37°

A straight angle is exactly 180°, so any two angles that together form a straight angle are supplementary.

### Linear Pair

A **linear pair** is two adjacent supplementary angles that form a straight line together. They are always supplementary: they always sum to 180°.

If you know one angle in a linear pair, subtract from 180° to find the other.

### Vertical Angles

When two lines intersect, four angles are formed. The angles across the intersection from each other are **vertical angles**, and they are always congruent (equal in measure).

```
Line 1 and Line 2 intersect at point P:
     A
     |
C -- P -- D
     |
     B
```
∠APC and ∠BPD are vertical angles (equal). ∠APD and ∠CPB are vertical angles (equal). ∠APC and ∠APD are a linear pair (supplementary).

**Example:** Two lines intersect. One angle is 67°. Find the other three angles.
- Vertical to 67°: **67°**
- Linear pair with 67°: 180° - 67° = **113°**
- Last angle (vertical to 113°): **113°**
- Four angles: 67°, 113°, 67°, 113°.

## Angle Bisectors

An **angle bisector** is a ray that divides an angle into two equal halves.

If ∠ABC = 80° and ray BD bisects ∠ABC, then ∠ABD = ∠DBC = 40°.

**Example:** BD bisects ∠ABC. If ∠ABD = (3x + 5)° and ∠DBC = (5x - 7)°, find x and ∠ABC.
- Equal angles: 3x + 5 = 5x - 7 → 12 = 2x → x = 6
- ∠ABD = 3(6) + 5 = 23°
- ∠ABC = 2 × 23° = **46°**

---

## Adjacent Angles

**Adjacent angles** share a common vertex and a common side, but do not overlap. They sit "next to" each other.

**Example:** Rays OA, OB, OC from common point O. If ∠AOB = 40° and ∠BOC = 55°, then ∠AOC = 40° + 55° = **95°** (angle addition postulate).

---

## Solving for Unknown Angles

Most geometry angle problems are algebra problems in disguise. Set up an equation using the angle relationship, then solve.

**Example:** Two supplementary angles are in a 2:7 ratio. Find each angle.
- Let angles be 2x and 7x. They're supplementary: 2x + 7x = 180
- 9x = 180 → x = 20
- Angles: 2(20) = **40°** and 7(20) = **140°**

**Example:** ∠MNO = (6x - 12)° and ∠ONP = (4x + 2)°. Together they form a straight angle. Find both.
- Linear pair: 6x - 12 + 4x + 2 = 180
- 10x - 10 = 180 → 10x = 190 → x = 19
- ∠MNO = 6(19) - 12 = **102°**; ∠ONP = 4(19) + 2 = **78°**. Check: 102 + 78 = 180°. ✓

**Example:** Vertical angles. One is (2x + 30)° and its vertical is (4x - 10)°. Find x.
- Vertical angles are equal: 2x + 30 = 4x - 10 → 40 = 2x → x = **20**
- Angle = 2(20) + 30 = **70°**

---

## Degrees, Minutes, Seconds (DMS)

In precise surveying and navigation, angles are measured in degrees, minutes, and seconds:
- 1 degree = 60 minutes (written 60')
- 1 minute = 60 seconds (written 60")

**Example:** 47°23'15" means 47 degrees, 23 minutes, 15 seconds.

For most geometry, decimal degrees are sufficient. But knowing this system exists helps when reading maps, astronomical data, or GPS coordinates.
