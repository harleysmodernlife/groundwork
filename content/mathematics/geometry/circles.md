## Circles: Circumference, Area, and Arcs

A circle is the set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a given center point. That distance is the **radius**. Circles appear throughout nature, engineering, and daily life — wheels, pipes, pulleys, clocks, satellite dishes, and orbital paths are all circular. Understanding circle geometry gives you the tools to calculate any measurement involving circular shapes.

---

## Essential Circle Vocabulary

**Center:** The fixed point equidistant from all points on the circle.

**Radius (r):** The distance from the center to any point on the circle. All radii of a circle are equal.

**Diameter (d):** The distance across the circle through the center. d = 2r, or r = d/2.

**Chord:** A line segment with both endpoints on the circle. The diameter is the longest chord.

**Arc:** A portion of the circle's circumference. A **minor arc** is less than a semicircle; a **major arc** is more than a semicircle; a **semicircle** is exactly half.

**Sector:** A "pie slice" region bounded by two radii and an arc.

**Tangent:** A line that touches the circle at exactly one point (the **point of tangency**). A tangent is always perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency.

**Secant:** A line that intersects the circle at two points.

---

## Pi (π)

Pi is the ratio of any circle's circumference to its diameter:

```
π = circumference / diameter ≈ 3.14159...
```

This ratio is the same for every circle, regardless of size. Pi is irrational — its decimal expansion never ends and never repeats. For most calculations, use π ≈ 3.14 or the π button on a calculator for more precision.

---

## Circumference

Circumference is the distance around a circle — its perimeter.

```
C = πd   or   C = 2πr
```

**Example:** A circle has radius 7 cm. Find the circumference.
- C = 2π(7) = 14π ≈ 14 × 3.14 ≈ **43.96 cm**

**Example:** A wheel has circumference 94.2 inches. Find the diameter.
- 94.2 = πd → d = 94.2/π ≈ 94.2/3.14 ≈ **30 inches**

---

## Area of a Circle

```
A = πr²
```

**Example:** A circle with radius 5 feet.
- A = π(5²) = 25π ≈ 25 × 3.14 ≈ **78.5 ft²**

**Example:** A circular pool has diameter 16 meters. Find the area.
- r = 8 m; A = π(8²) = 64π ≈ **201.1 m²**

**Example:** A semicircle with diameter 12 cm.
- r = 6 cm; A of full circle = π(36); semicircle = 36π/2 = 18π ≈ **56.55 cm²**

---

## Arc Length

An arc's length depends on the arc's degree measure and the circle's circumference. The arc is a fraction of the full circle.

```
Arc length = (central angle / 360°) × 2πr
```

**Example:** A circle with radius 10 has a central angle of 90°. Find the arc length.
- Arc = (90/360) × 2π(10) = (1/4) × 20π = 5π ≈ **15.71 units**

**Example:** A circle with radius 6. Find the arc length for a 120° central angle.
- Arc = (120/360) × 2π(6) = (1/3) × 12π = 4π ≈ **12.57 units**

---

## Area of a Sector

A sector is the "pie slice" region enclosed by two radii and an arc.

```
Sector area = (central angle / 360°) × πr²
```

**Example:** A circle with radius 9 and central angle 80°.
- Sector area = (80/360) × π(9²) = (2/9) × 81π = 18π ≈ **56.55 units²**

---

## Central and Inscribed Angles

**Central angle:** An angle whose vertex is at the center of the circle. The central angle equals the degree measure of its intercepted arc.
- A central angle of 70° intercepts a 70° arc.

**Inscribed angle:** An angle whose vertex is on the circle and whose sides are chords. The inscribed angle equals **half** the intercepted arc.
- An inscribed angle intercepting a 70° arc measures 35°.

**Inscribed Angle Theorem:** An inscribed angle is half the central angle that intercepts the same arc.

**Corollary:** All inscribed angles that intercept the same arc are equal.

**Corollary:** An inscribed angle that intercepts a semicircle (180° arc) is always 90°. Any triangle inscribed in a semicircle is a right triangle.

---

## Tangent Lines and Their Properties

A tangent line touches the circle at exactly one point and is perpendicular to the radius drawn to that point.

**Two tangents from an external point:** If two tangent lines are drawn from the same external point, the two tangent segments (from the external point to the tangency points) are equal in length.

**Example:** From point P outside a circle, two tangent segments are drawn. One has length (3x + 1) and the other has length (5x - 7). Find x.
- Equal tangents: 3x + 1 = 5x - 7 → 8 = 2x → x = **4**
- Segment length: 3(4) + 1 = **13**

---

## Chords and Their Properties

**Chord length and distance from center:** A chord closer to the center is longer. The diameter (passing through the center) is the longest chord.

**Perpendicular bisector:** The perpendicular bisector of any chord passes through the center. This is useful for finding the center of a circle given any two chords.

## Practice

1. A circle has diameter 24 cm. Find the circumference and area.
2. Find the arc length of a 60° arc in a circle with radius 12.
3. A sector has a central angle of 45° and radius 8. Find its area.
4. An inscribed angle measures 38°. What is the measure of the intercepted arc?
5. A circular pizza has diameter 16 inches and is cut into 8 equal slices. Find the area of one slice.
6. From an external point, two tangent segments measure (4x - 3) and (2x + 9). Find x and the segment length.

**Answers:**
1. C = π(24) = 24π ≈ **75.4 cm**; A = π(12²) = 144π ≈ **452.4 cm²**
2. (60/360) × 2π(12) = (1/6)(24π) = 4π ≈ **12.57 units**
3. (45/360) × π(8²) = (1/8)(64π) = 8π ≈ **25.13 units²**
4. Inscribed angle = half arc, so arc = 2 × 38° = **76°**
5. Sector: radius = 8, central angle = 360°/8 = 45°. Area = (45/360) × π(64) = (1/8)(64π) = 8π ≈ **25.13 in²**
6. 4x - 3 = 2x + 9 → 2x = 12 → x = **6**; segment = 4(6) - 3 = **21**
