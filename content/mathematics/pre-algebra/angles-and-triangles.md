## Angles and Triangles

An angle is formed when two rays share an endpoint. Angles are everywhere in the physical world — every corner, every slope, every turn is an angle. Triangles are the simplest closed polygon, and they have a set of properties so reliable and powerful that they underpin all of structural engineering and navigation. Understanding angles and triangles is foundational for all higher geometry and trigonometry.

---

## Measuring Angles

Angles are measured in **degrees** (°). A full rotation is 360°. This is an arbitrary convention, but it's universal.

**Types of angles:**

| Type | Measure | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Zero angle | 0° | Two rays pointing the same direction |
| Acute | 0° to 90° | Sharp, less than a right angle |
| Right | exactly 90° | A perfect corner, marked with a small square |
| Obtuse | 90° to 180° | Blunt, more than a right angle |
| Straight | exactly 180° | A straight line |
| Reflex | 180° to 360° | More than half a rotation |

---

## Angle Relationships

### Complementary Angles
Two angles are **complementary** if they sum to 90°. Each is the complement of the other.
- If one angle is 35°, its complement is 55°.

### Supplementary Angles
Two angles are **supplementary** if they sum to 180°. They form a straight line together.
- If one angle is 110°, its supplement is 70°.

### Vertical Angles
When two straight lines intersect, they form four angles. The angles across from each other (not adjacent) are **vertical angles**, and they are always equal.

### Adjacent Angles
Adjacent angles share a common side and vertex. Together with vertical angles, these relationships let you find unknown angles without measuring.

**Example:** Two lines intersect. One angle is 65°. Find the other three.
- The angle directly across is also 65° (vertical angles).
- The two adjacent angles each equal 180° - 65° = 115° (supplementary).
- Four angles: 65°, 115°, 65°, 115°.

## Triangles: The Basics

A triangle has three sides and three angles. Two facts hold for every triangle without exception:

**1. The Triangle Angle Sum:** The three interior angles always add up to exactly 180°.

If you know two angles, the third is determined: third angle = 180° - (angle 1 + angle 2).

**Example:** A triangle has angles of 47° and 82°. Find the third.
- Third angle = 180° - 47° - 82° = **51°**

**2. The Triangle Inequality:** Any two sides must sum to more than the third side. (Otherwise the triangle can't close.)
- Sides 3, 4, 5: 3+4=7 > 5; 3+5=8 > 4; 4+5=9 > 3. ✓ Valid triangle.
- Sides 1, 2, 10: 1+2=3, not > 10. ✗ Invalid — can't form a triangle.

---

## Classifying Triangles by Angle

**Acute triangle:** All three angles are less than 90°.

**Right triangle:** One angle is exactly 90°. The side opposite the right angle is the **hypotenuse** — always the longest side. The other two sides are called **legs**.

**Obtuse triangle:** One angle is greater than 90°. A triangle can have at most one obtuse angle (since three would sum to more than 180°).

---

## Classifying Triangles by Side

**Equilateral:** All three sides equal → all three angles are 60° each.

**Isosceles:** Two sides equal → the two angles opposite those sides (called base angles) are also equal.

**Scalene:** All three sides different → all three angles different.

---

## Exterior Angles

An **exterior angle** is formed by extending one side of a triangle beyond the vertex. The exterior angle equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles (the two angles that are "across" from it).

**Example:** A triangle has angles 50° and 70°. The exterior angle at the third vertex is 50° + 70° = **120°**.

Verification: the interior angle at that vertex is 180° - 50° - 70° = 60°. Exterior angle + interior angle = 180° (straight line): 120° + 60° = 180°. ✓

---

## Isosceles Triangle Properties

Because two sides are equal in an isosceles triangle, two angles are also equal. This can be used to find missing angles.

**Example:** An isosceles triangle has a vertex angle of 40°. Find the base angles.
- The two base angles are equal and must sum to 180° - 40° = 140°.
- Each base angle = 140° ÷ 2 = **70°**

---

## Using Angle Relationships to Solve Problems

**Example:** Lines AB and CD intersect. One angle is (3x + 5)° and its vertical angle is (4x - 15)°. Find x and both angle measures.
- Vertical angles are equal: 3x + 5 = 4x - 15
- Subtract 3x: 5 = x - 15
- Add 15: x = 20
- Angle = 3(20) + 5 = **65°**. Check: 4(20) - 15 = 65°. ✓

**Example:** In a triangle, one angle is 90°, another is (2x + 10)°, and the third is (x - 10)°. Find all angles.
- Sum = 180°: 90 + (2x + 10) + (x - 10) = 180
- 90 + 3x = 180
- 3x = 90
- x = 30
- Angles: 90°, 2(30) + 10 = **70°**, 30 - 10 = **20°**
- Check: 90 + 70 + 20 = 180°. ✓

## Practice

1. Find the complement of a 37° angle.
2. Find the supplement of a 105° angle.
3. A triangle has angles of 55° and 80°. Find the third angle.
4. Is a triangle with sides 5, 6, and 12 possible?
5. An isosceles triangle has a base angle of 55°. Find the vertex angle.
6. An exterior angle of a triangle is 135°. One of the non-adjacent interior angles is 70°. Find the other non-adjacent interior angle and all three interior angles.

**Answers:**
1. 90° - 37° = **53°**
2. 180° - 105° = **75°**
3. 180° - 55° - 80° = **45°**
4. 5 + 6 = 11, not > 12. **Not possible.**
5. 180° - 2(55°) = 180° - 110° = **70°**
6. Other non-adjacent interior angle = 135° - 70° = **65°**; Interior angles: 70°, 65°, 45° (since 180° - 135° = 45°). Check: 70 + 65 + 45 = 180°. ✓
