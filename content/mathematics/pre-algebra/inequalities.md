## Inequalities

An inequality is a mathematical statement that compares two expressions using a symbol other than equals. Instead of stating that two quantities are exactly equal, an inequality says one is larger, smaller, or at least as large as the other. Inequalities model countless real situations: budget limits, minimum requirements, age restrictions, weight capacity limits.

---

## Inequality Symbols

| Symbol | Meaning | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| > | greater than | x > 5 (x is more than 5) |
| < | less than | y < 3 (y is less than 3) |
| ≥ | greater than or equal to | n ≥ 10 (n is at least 10) |
| ≤ | less than or equal to | m ≤ 20 (m is at most 20) |
| ≠ | not equal to | a ≠ 0 (a is not zero) |

**The arrow trick:** The symbol always points to the smaller quantity. 3 < 7: the arrow points left, at the 3 (smaller). 9 > 4: the arrow points right, at the 4 (smaller).

---

## Solutions to Inequalities

An equation typically has one solution. An inequality has infinitely many.

- x = 5 has exactly one solution: x is 5.
- x > 5 has infinitely many solutions: 6, 7, 8, 5.1, 5.001, 1000...

The **solution set** is all values that make the inequality true.

---

## Graphing Inequalities on a Number Line

A number line shows the full range of solutions visually.

**Open circle (◦):** Used for strict inequalities (< or >) — the boundary value is NOT included.
- x > 3: open circle at 3, arrow pointing right.

**Closed circle (●):** Used for inclusive inequalities (≤ or ≥) — the boundary value IS included.
- x ≤ -1: closed circle at -1, arrow pointing left.

**Examples:**
- n ≥ 2: closed circle at 2, arrow right → includes 2, 3, 4, 5...
- y < -4: open circle at -4, arrow left → includes -5, -6, -7... but NOT -4.

## Solving One-Step Inequalities

The process mirrors solving equations, with one critical additional rule.

**Addition/Subtraction — same as equations:**

Solve: x + 7 > 12
- Subtract 7 from both sides: x > **5**
- Graph: open circle at 5, arrow right.

Solve: n - 4 ≤ 3
- Add 4 to both sides: n ≤ **7**
- Graph: closed circle at 7, arrow left.

**Multiplication/Division — with the critical twist:**

Solve: 3y < 21
- Divide both sides by 3: y < **7** ✓

Solve: -2x > 8

Divide both sides by -2, BUT: **when you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a negative number, you must flip the inequality symbol.**

- -2x > 8 ÷ (-2) on both sides: x **<** -4

The direction flips. Why? Think of it this way: 3 > 1. Multiply both sides by -1: -3 and -1. Now -3 < -1. The relationship reverses when you multiply by a negative.

**Always flip the inequality symbol when multiplying or dividing by a negative number. This is the most important rule in inequalities.**

---

## Solving Two-Step Inequalities

Same procedure as two-step equations, but watch for sign changes.

Solve: 4x - 3 > 13
- Add 3: 4x > 16
- Divide by 4: x > **4**

Solve: -3n + 6 ≤ -9
- Subtract 6: -3n ≤ -15
- Divide by -3 (flip!): n ≥ **5**

Check: Try n = 6 (should satisfy): -3(6) + 6 = -12. Is -12 ≤ -9? Yes. ✓
Try n = 4 (should NOT satisfy): -3(4) + 6 = -6. Is -6 ≤ -9? No. ✓

## Writing Inequalities from Words

**Key phrases:**

| Words | Inequality |
| :--- | :--- |
| at least, no less than, minimum | ≥ |
| at most, no more than, maximum | ≤ |
| more than, exceeds, above | > |
| less than, below, under | < |

**Examples:**

"You must be at least 18 to vote." → age ≥ 18

"The elevator holds at most 2,000 pounds." → weight ≤ 2,000

"A worker must complete more than 40 parts per hour to qualify." → parts > 40

"The temperature must stay below 32°F for ice to form." → temp < 32

## Real-World Problems with Inequalities

**Example:** A company wants to spend no more than $5,000 on supplies. Each unit costs $45 and there's a $200 shipping fee. How many units can they buy?

- 45u + 200 ≤ 5,000
- 45u ≤ 4,800
- u ≤ 4,800 ÷ 45 ≈ 106.7

Since you can't buy a fraction of a unit: u ≤ **106 units**

**Example:** A driver must average more than 55 mph to arrive on time for a 330-mile trip. What minimum time limit applies?

- distance/time > 55 → 330/t > 55 → 330 > 55t → t < 330/55 → t < **6 hours**

The driver must complete the trip in less than 6 hours.

## Compound Inequalities

A compound inequality combines two inequalities.

**"And" inequality (both must be true):**
- Written: -3 < x < 7 (x is between -3 and 7)
- Means: x > -3 AND x < 7
- Graph: open circles at -3 and 7, segment between them.

**"Or" inequality (at least one must be true):**
- Written: x < -2 or x > 5
- Graph: two separate arrows pointing outward.

**Example:** A product must be packaged in containers weighing between 5 and 8 pounds.
- 5 ≤ weight ≤ 8
- Graph: closed circles at 5 and 8, segment between them.
