## Whole Numbers and Place Value

A whole number is any non-negative number with no fractional or decimal part: 0, 1, 2, 3, and so on forever. These are the numbers you use to count objects — three apples, fifteen people, a thousand dollars. Before you can do anything useful in mathematics, you need to understand how our number system works and why it is built the way it is.

---

## The Place Value System

Every number you write is a code. Each digit has a value determined not just by what it is, but by *where it sits*. This is the place value system, and it uses powers of ten.

Reading from right to left, the positions are:

```
Millions | Hundred-Thousands | Ten-Thousands | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones
```

The number **4,372** means:
- 4 in the thousands place → 4 × 1,000 = 4,000
- 3 in the hundreds place → 3 × 100 = 300
- 7 in the tens place → 7 × 10 = 70
- 2 in the ones place → 2 × 1 = 2
- Total: 4,000 + 300 + 70 + 2 = 4,372

This is called **expanded form**. Any whole number can be broken apart this way, and understanding it makes arithmetic much easier because every operation works column by column.

---

## Reading and Writing Large Numbers

Commas are inserted every three digits from the right to make large numbers readable. Without them, **1000000** requires careful counting. With them, **1,000,000** is immediately one million.

| Number | Name |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1,000 | One thousand |
| 10,000 | Ten thousand |
| 100,000 | One hundred thousand |
| 1,000,000 | One million |
| 1,000,000,000 | One billion |

**Reading a number aloud:** Read each group of three digits as a number, then say the group name.
- 2,450,317 → "two million, four hundred fifty thousand, three hundred seventeen"

---

## Zero as a Placeholder

Zero is not "nothing" — it is a placeholder that holds a position. Without it, 305 and 35 would be indistinguishable. The zero in the tens place of 305 says there are no tens, keeping the 3 firmly in the hundreds position.

This is why 1,004 is not the same as 14. The two zeros hold the hundreds and tens places, ensuring the 1 stays in thousands.

## Comparing Whole Numbers

To compare two whole numbers:
1. The number with more digits is always larger. 9,999 < 10,000, even though the 9s look imposing.
2. If they have the same number of digits, compare digit by digit from left to right.

**Symbols:**
- `>` means "greater than": 500 > 200
- `<` means "less than": 200 < 500
- `=` means "equal to": 400 = 400

**Example:** Compare 7,843 and 7,829.
Both have four digits. Starting left: 7 = 7, 8 = 8, then 4 > 2. Stop — 7,843 > 7,829.

---

## Ordering a List of Numbers

To sort from least to greatest:
1. Group by digit count — fewer digits means smaller.
2. Within the same digit count, compare from the leftmost digit.

**Example:** Order 412, 3,100, 89, 3,014, 500 from least to greatest.
- 89 (2 digits)
- 412 (3 digits) — 412 < 500
- 500 (3 digits)
- 3,014 (4 digits) — 3,014 < 3,100
- 3,100 (4 digits)

Result: **89, 412, 500, 3,014, 3,100**

---

## Rounding Whole Numbers

Rounding replaces a number with a nearby "cleaner" value. You use this constantly in real life — estimating a grocery bill, checking whether a calculation seems right, or communicating an approximate quantity.

**The procedure:**
1. Identify the digit in the place you're rounding to.
2. Look at the digit immediately to its right.
3. If that digit is 5 or greater, increase the rounding digit by 1 (round up).
4. If that digit is 4 or less, leave the rounding digit alone (round down).
5. Replace all digits to the right of the rounding place with zeros.

**Examples — rounding 6,748:**
- To the nearest ten: ones digit is 8 (≥ 5) → round up → **6,750**
- To the nearest hundred: tens digit is 4 (< 5) → round down → **6,700**
- To the nearest thousand: hundreds digit is 7 (≥ 5) → round up → **7,000**

---

## Why Place Value Is the Foundation

Place value is not just a lesson about big numbers — it underlies every arithmetic operation you will ever perform. When you add, you line up digits by place and carry into the next column. When you multiply, each partial product is offset by place. When you work with decimals, you extend this same system to the right of the ones place. Everything builds from here.

## Practice

1. Write "three million, forty-two thousand, six" in standard form.
2. What is the value of the digit 8 in 48,302?
3. Round 93,647 to the nearest thousand.
4. Order from greatest to least: 1,201; 989; 1,198; 1,210; 1,009.
5. Write 70,000 + 400 + 9 in standard form.

**Answers:**
1. 3,042,006
2. 8,000 (8 is in the ten-thousands place)
3. 94,000
4. 1,210; 1,201; 1,198; 1,009; 989
5. 70,409
