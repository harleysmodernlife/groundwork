## Decimals and Place Value

A decimal is a fraction written in a different notation — one that extends the place value system to the right of the ones place. Every decimal can be written as a fraction, and every fraction whose denominator is a power of 10 can be written as a decimal. Understanding decimals is essential for working with money, measurements, and any calculation that involves quantities between whole numbers.

---

## Extending the Place Value System

To the left of the decimal point, place values are ones, tens, hundreds, and so on (multiplying by 10 each step left). To the right, each position divides by 10 instead.

```
Hundreds | Tens | Ones . Tenths | Hundredths | Thousandths | ...
```

The decimal **4.725** means:
- 4 in the ones place
- 7 in the tenths place: 7/10
- 2 in the hundredths place: 2/100
- 5 in the thousandths place: 5/1000
- Total: 4 + 7/10 + 2/100 + 5/1000

---

## Reading Decimals

Read the whole number part normally, say "and" for the decimal point, then read the decimal part as a whole number followed by the place name of the final digit.

- 3.7 → "three and seven tenths"
- 0.42 → "forty-two hundredths"
- 12.005 → "twelve and five thousandths"

**Common error:** Saying "three point seven" is casual but imprecise. "Three and seven tenths" makes the place value explicit.

---

## Fractions and Decimals

Any fraction with a denominator that's a power of 10 converts directly:
- 3/10 = 0.3
- 47/100 = 0.47
- 9/1000 = 0.009

For other fractions, divide the numerator by the denominator:
- 1/4: 1 ÷ 4 = 0.25
- 1/3: 1 ÷ 3 = 0.333... (repeating)
- 2/5: 2 ÷ 5 = 0.4

A **terminating decimal** ends (1/4 = 0.25). A **repeating decimal** has a digit or group of digits that repeats forever (1/3 = 0.333..., written 0.3̄).

---

## Comparing Decimals

A critical mistake: treating 0.9 as smaller than 0.15 because 9 < 15. This ignores place value.

**Procedure:**
1. Align the decimal points.
2. Compare digit by digit from left to right.
3. Add trailing zeros if needed to make the comparison easier.

**Example:** Compare 0.9 and 0.15.
- 0.90 vs 0.15
- Tenths column: 9 > 1
- Therefore **0.9 > 0.15**

**Example:** Compare 3.072 and 3.07.
- 3.072 vs 3.070
- First two digits after decimal match (07)
- Third decimal place: 2 > 0
- Therefore **3.072 > 3.070**

---

## Adding and Subtracting Decimals

**Rule:** Align the decimal points, then add or subtract as with whole numbers.

```
  14.75
+  3.40
------
  18.15
```

Adding trailing zeros (like the 0 in 3.40) helps keep columns aligned. The decimal point in the answer goes directly below the others.

**Example with borrowing:**
```
  10.00
-  4.37
-------
   5.63
```

---

## Multiplying Decimals

Multiply as if the decimal points don't exist, then place the decimal point in the result.

**The rule:** Count the total number of decimal places in both factors. The product has that many decimal places.

**Example:** 3.4 × 2.5
- Multiply: 34 × 25 = 850
- Decimal places: 3.4 has 1, 2.5 has 1. Total: 2.
- Place decimal 2 from the right: **8.50** = 8.5

**Example:** 0.06 × 0.3
- Multiply: 6 × 3 = 18
- Decimal places: 2 + 1 = 3
- Place decimal 3 from the right: **0.018**

---

## Dividing Decimals

**Dividing by a whole number:** Place the decimal point in the quotient directly above its position in the dividend.

```
      4.2
    -----
3 | 12.6
    12
    ---
     0.6
     0.6
     ---
      0
```

**Dividing by a decimal:** Multiply both dividend and divisor by the same power of 10 to make the divisor a whole number, then divide.

**Example:** 7.2 ÷ 0.4
- Multiply both by 10: 72 ÷ 4 = **18**

**Example:** 3.5 ÷ 0.07
- Multiply both by 100: 350 ÷ 7 = **50**

---

## Rounding Decimals

Same rule as rounding whole numbers — look at the digit immediately to the right of where you're rounding.

Round 3.7462:
- To the nearest tenth: look at hundredths (4 < 5) → round down → **3.7**
- To the nearest hundredth: look at thousandths (6 ≥ 5) → round up → **3.75**
- To the nearest thousandth: look at ten-thousandths (2 < 5) → round down → **3.746**

---

## Decimals and Money

Money is a decimal system. $4.75 means 4 dollars and 75 cents — 75 hundredths of a dollar. Every calculation involving money uses decimal arithmetic, which is why understanding hundredths is particularly important.

Prices are always to the hundredths place. When calculating tax, tips, or percentages, you will routinely get results like 3.8745 and need to round to the nearest cent (hundredth): $3.87.

## Practice

1. Write 0.045 in words.
2. Compare: 0.8 vs. 0.79 — which is larger?
3. Add: 12.4 + 3.87 + 0.9
4. Multiply: 0.04 × 1.5
5. Divide: 9.6 ÷ 0.3
6. Round 7.4853 to the nearest hundredth.

**Answers:**
1. Forty-five thousandths
2. 0.80 > 0.79, so **0.8** is larger
3. 17.17
4. 0.060 = **0.06** (2 + 1 = 3 decimal places; 4 × 15 = 60 → 0.060)
5. 96 ÷ 3 = **32**
6. **7.49** (thousandths digit 5 ≥ 5, round up)
