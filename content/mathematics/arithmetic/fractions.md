## Fractions: What They Are and How to Use Them

A fraction represents a part of a whole. When something is divided into equal pieces and you take some of those pieces, a fraction describes what you have. Understanding fractions is essential for everything from cooking to carpentry to finance — anywhere you deal with quantities that aren't whole numbers.

---

## The Anatomy of a Fraction

```
  3    ← numerator (how many parts you have)
 ---
  4    ← denominator (how many equal parts the whole is divided into)
```

The fraction 3/4 means: the whole is divided into 4 equal parts, and you have 3 of them.

The **denominator** names the fraction — fourths, thirds, eighths, etc. The **numerator** counts how many of that named unit you have.

---

## Types of Fractions

**Proper fraction:** Numerator is less than denominator. Value is between 0 and 1.
- 2/5, 7/8, 1/4

**Improper fraction:** Numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator. Value is 1 or greater.
- 9/4, 11/3, 5/5

**Mixed number:** A whole number combined with a proper fraction.
- 2 and 1/4 (written 2¼) means two whole units plus one-fourth of another.

**Converting improper fractions to mixed numbers:** Divide the numerator by the denominator.
- 9 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 1 → 2 and 1/4

**Converting mixed numbers to improper fractions:** Multiply whole number by denominator, add numerator, keep denominator.
- 3 and 2/5 → (3 × 5 + 2) / 5 = 17/5

---

## Equivalent Fractions

Different fractions can represent the same value. 1/2, 2/4, 3/6, and 4/8 all name the same amount.

**To create an equivalent fraction:** Multiply or divide both numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number.
- 1/2 × 3/3 = 3/6 (multiply both by 3)
- 4/8 ÷ 4/4 = 1/2 (divide both by 4)

This is valid because multiplying or dividing by 1 (in the form of 3/3 or 4/4) doesn't change the value.

---

## Simplifying (Reducing) Fractions

A fraction is in **simplest form** when the numerator and denominator share no common factor other than 1.

**Procedure:** Find the Greatest Common Factor (GCF) of the numerator and denominator, then divide both by it.

**Example:** Simplify 18/24.
- Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18
- Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24
- GCF = 6
- 18 ÷ 6 = 3; 24 ÷ 6 = 4 → **3/4**

---

## Comparing Fractions

To compare fractions with the **same denominator**, compare numerators. 5/8 > 3/8 because 5 > 3.

To compare fractions with **different denominators**, find a common denominator first.

**Example:** Compare 3/4 and 5/6.
- Common denominator of 4 and 6: LCD = 12
- 3/4 = 9/12 (multiply by 3/3)
- 5/6 = 10/12 (multiply by 2/2)
- 9/12 < 10/12, so **3/4 < 5/6**

---

## Adding and Subtracting Fractions

**Same denominator:** Add or subtract numerators, keep the denominator.
- 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8
- 7/9 - 4/9 = 3/9 = 1/3

**Different denominators:** Find a common denominator, convert, then operate.

**Example:** 1/3 + 1/4
- LCD of 3 and 4 = 12
- 1/3 = 4/12; 1/4 = 3/12
- 4/12 + 3/12 = **7/12**

**Example:** 5/6 - 1/4
- LCD of 6 and 4 = 12
- 5/6 = 10/12; 1/4 = 3/12
- 10/12 - 3/12 = **7/12**

For mixed numbers, operate on the whole numbers and fractions separately, then recombine. If the fraction subtraction requires borrowing, convert 1 whole into the appropriate fraction.

---

## Multiplying Fractions

Multiply numerators together and denominators together. No common denominator needed.

- 2/3 × 3/4 = (2 × 3) / (3 × 4) = 6/12 = **1/2**

**Tip:** Simplify before multiplying (cross-cancel) to keep numbers small.
- 2/3 × 3/4: The 3 in the numerator and denominator cancel → 2/1 × 1/4 = 2/4 = 1/2

**Multiplying mixed numbers:** Convert to improper fractions first.
- 1 and 1/2 × 2 and 2/3 = 3/2 × 8/3 = 24/6 = **4**

---

## Dividing Fractions

To divide by a fraction, multiply by its **reciprocal** (flip the second fraction).

- 3/4 ÷ 1/2 = 3/4 × 2/1 = 6/4 = **3/2** = 1 and 1/2

The phrase: **"Keep, Change, Flip"** — keep the first fraction, change division to multiplication, flip the second fraction.

**Why does this work?** Dividing by 1/2 is asking "how many halves fit in 3/4?" Two halves make a whole, so 3/4 contains 1.5 halves. The reciprocal method gives this result automatically.

**Example with mixed numbers:**
2 and 1/3 ÷ 1 and 3/4 = 7/3 ÷ 7/4 = 7/3 × 4/7 = 28/21 = **4/3** = 1 and 1/3

---

## Fractions in Real Life

- **Recipe scaling:** A recipe calls for 3/4 cup of flour and you want to double it: 3/4 × 2 = 3/2 = 1 and 1/2 cups.
- **Measuring:** A board needs to be cut at 2 and 5/8 inches from the end. This is a fraction of a whole inch.
- **Discount:** A sale offers 1/3 off. To find the discount on a $60 item: 1/3 × 60 = $20 off.
