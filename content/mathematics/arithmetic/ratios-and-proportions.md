## Ratios and Proportions

A ratio compares two quantities. A proportion states that two ratios are equal. These concepts appear constantly in real life: mixing concrete, scaling a recipe, reading a map, calculating unit prices, and interpreting statistics all rely on ratios and proportions.

---

## What Is a Ratio?

A ratio is a comparison of two quantities by division. If a class has 12 boys and 18 girls, the ratio of boys to girls is 12 to 18.

**Three ways to write a ratio:**
- Using "to": 12 to 18
- Using a colon: 12:18
- As a fraction: 12/18

All three mean the same thing. Ratios can be simplified like fractions: 12/18 = 2/3. So the ratio of boys to girls is 2:3 — for every 2 boys, there are 3 girls.

### Order Matters

The ratio of boys to girls (12:18 = 2:3) is different from the ratio of girls to boys (18:12 = 3:2). Always pay attention to which quantity comes first.

---

## Part-to-Part vs. Part-to-Whole Ratios

**Part-to-part:** Compares one group to another. Boys to girls: 2:3.

**Part-to-whole:** Compares one group to the total. Boys to total students: 12/30 = 2/5.

When mixing or splitting something, you typically use both types:
- Concrete mix: cement to sand to gravel in ratio 1:2:3. Total parts = 6.
- Cement is 1/6 of the total mix.
- Sand is 2/6 = 1/3 of the total.
- Gravel is 3/6 = 1/2 of the total.

---

## Unit Rates

A unit rate is a ratio with a denominator of 1. It tells you "how much per one."

- 240 miles in 4 hours → 240/4 = **60 miles per hour**
- $3.60 for 12 eggs → 360¢/12 = **30¢ per egg**
- Printer does 360 pages in 12 minutes → **30 pages per minute**

Unit rates allow direct comparison. If Store A sells 5 bottles for $8 and Store B sells 8 bottles for $11, which is better?
- Store A: $8 ÷ 5 = $1.60 per bottle
- Store B: $11 ÷ 8 = $1.375 per bottle
- **Store B** is the better deal.

---

## What Is a Proportion?

A proportion is a statement that two ratios are equal:

```
a/b = c/d
```

This reads: "a is to b as c is to d."

**Example:** If 3 workers build 12 feet of fence per day, how many feet will 5 workers build?
- 3 workers : 12 feet = 5 workers : x feet
- 3/12 = 5/x

---

## Solving Proportions: Cross-Multiplication

If a/b = c/d, then a × d = b × c.

This is called **cross-multiplication** — multiply diagonally across the equals sign.

**Solving the fence example:**
- 3/12 = 5/x
- 3 × x = 12 × 5
- 3x = 60
- x = 60 ÷ 3 = **20 feet**

**Example:** A car travels 156 miles on 6 gallons. At the same rate, how far will it travel on 10 gallons?
- 156/6 = x/10
- 156 × 10 = 6 × x
- 1560 = 6x
- x = 1560 ÷ 6 = **260 miles**

---

## Scaling Recipes and Mixtures

Proportions are the tool behind scaling anything up or down.

**Example:** A recipe serves 4 and requires 3 cups of flour. To serve 10:
- 3/4 = x/10
- 4x = 30
- x = 7.5 → **7 and 1/2 cups of flour**

**Example:** A paint mixture uses 2 parts blue to 5 parts white to make 28 gallons total. How much blue paint?
- Blue is 2 out of 7 total parts: 2/7 of 28 = **8 gallons blue**
- White: 5/7 of 28 = **20 gallons white**

---

## Maps and Scale Drawings

Maps use a scale ratio. If 1 inch represents 50 miles:

- A 3.5-inch distance on the map → 3.5 × 50 = **175 miles** in real life
- An actual distance of 225 miles → 225 ÷ 50 = **4.5 inches** on the map

Blueprints and architectural drawings work the same way. A 1:24 scale means every 1 inch on the drawing represents 24 inches (2 feet) in reality.

---

## Direct vs. Inverse Variation

**Direct variation:** As one quantity increases, the other increases proportionally.
- More hours worked → more pay. If 5 hours = $75, then 8 hours = $120. (Both multiplied by 1.6.)

**Inverse variation:** As one quantity increases, the other decreases proportionally.
- More workers → fewer days to complete the job. 3 workers take 8 days; 6 workers take 4 days. The product (workers × days = 24) stays constant.

Recognizing which type of relationship you're dealing with determines whether you use a direct proportion or an inverse one.

---

## Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1:** Setting up the proportion in the wrong order.
- "6 workers build 12 feet" doesn't mean 6/12 = 5/x. It means workers/feet = workers/feet: 6/12 = 5/x or feet/workers = feet/workers: 12/6 = x/5. Either works, but you must be consistent.

**Mistake 2:** Confusing part-to-part ratios with fractions of the whole.
- A ratio of 3:5 means 3 + 5 = 8 total parts. The first quantity is 3/8 of the total, not 3/5.

## Practice

1. Simplify the ratio 36:48.
2. A car travels 390 miles on 13 gallons. What is the fuel efficiency in miles per gallon?
3. If 4 pounds of apples cost $5.20, how much do 7 pounds cost?
4. A recipe for 6 servings uses 1.5 cups of sugar. How much sugar is needed for 10 servings?
5. A map has a scale of 1 inch = 30 miles. Two cities are 4.5 inches apart on the map. What is the real distance?

**Answers:**
1. GCF of 36 and 48 = 12. 36/12 : 48/12 = **3:4**
2. 390 ÷ 13 = **30 mpg**
3. 5.20/4 = x/7 → 4x = 36.4 → x = **$9.10**
4. 1.5/6 = x/10 → 6x = 15 → x = **2.5 cups**
5. 4.5 × 30 = **135 miles**
