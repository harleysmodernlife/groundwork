## Slope and the Equation of a Line

Slope measures how steep a line is — specifically, how much the y-value changes for each unit of change in x. It is the mathematical language for describing rates: miles per hour, cost per unit, calories per serving. Every straight-line relationship has a slope, and being able to find, interpret, and use slope is one of the core skills of algebra.

---

## What Is Slope?

Slope is the ratio of **rise** (vertical change) to **run** (horizontal change) between any two points on a line.

```
slope = rise / run = (change in y) / (change in x) = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁)
```

The slope is typically denoted by the letter **m**.

**Example:** Find the slope between (2, 3) and (6, 11).
- Rise: 11 - 3 = 8
- Run: 6 - 2 = 4
- Slope: 8/4 = **2**

For every 1 unit you move right, the line rises 2 units.

**Order doesn't matter — as long as you're consistent:** Using (6, 11) as point 1 and (2, 3) as point 2: (3 - 11)/(2 - 6) = -8/-4 = 2. Same result.

---

## Interpreting Slope

**Positive slope:** Line rises left to right. As x increases, y increases.
**Negative slope:** Line falls left to right. As x increases, y decreases.
**Zero slope:** Perfectly horizontal line. y never changes regardless of x. (y = constant)
**Undefined slope:** Perfectly vertical line. x never changes. You'd be dividing by zero.

**Real-world interpretation:** If a company's revenue increases by $4,000 for each new employee hired, the slope of the revenue-versus-employees line is 4,000. It means: every +1 on the x-axis (one more employee) produces +4,000 on the y-axis (more revenue).

---

## Slope-Intercept Form

The most common way to write the equation of a line:

```
y = mx + b
```

Where:
- **m** is the slope
- **b** is the **y-intercept** — the value of y when x = 0 (where the line crosses the y-axis)

**Example:** y = 3x + 2
- Slope m = 3: the line rises 3 for every 1 unit right
- Y-intercept b = 2: the line crosses the y-axis at (0, 2)

**Writing a line from slope and y-intercept:** If slope = -1/2 and y-intercept = 5:
- y = -1/2 x + 5

---

## Finding the Equation from Two Points

**Step 1:** Calculate the slope using the two points.
**Step 2:** Use one point and the slope to find b.
**Step 3:** Write the equation.

**Example:** Find the equation of the line through (1, 4) and (3, 10).

Step 1: m = (10 - 4)/(3 - 1) = 6/2 = 3

Step 2: Use point (1, 4) in y = mx + b:
- 4 = 3(1) + b
- 4 = 3 + b
- b = 1

Step 3: **y = 3x + 1**

Verify with (3, 10): y = 3(3) + 1 = 10. ✓

---

## Point-Slope Form

When you know a point on the line and the slope, you can use point-slope form:

```
y - y₁ = m(x - x₁)
```

**Example:** Line with slope 2 passing through (-1, 3):
- y - 3 = 2(x - (-1))
- y - 3 = 2(x + 1)
- y - 3 = 2x + 2
- **y = 2x + 5**

This is useful when you don't know the y-intercept directly.

---

## Standard Form

Lines can also be written in standard form:

```
Ax + By = C
```

Where A, B, and C are integers and A is positive.

**Converting slope-intercept to standard form:** y = 2x - 5
- Subtract 2x from both sides: -2x + y = -5
- Multiply by -1: **2x - y = 5**

Standard form is commonly used in systems of equations.

---

## Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

**Parallel lines** have the **same slope** and different y-intercepts. They never intersect.
- y = 3x + 1 and y = 3x - 4 are parallel. Both have slope 3.

**Perpendicular lines** have slopes that are **negative reciprocals** of each other. They intersect at a 90° angle.
- If slope m₁ = 3, the perpendicular slope m₂ = -1/3.
- If slope m₁ = -2/5, the perpendicular slope m₂ = 5/2.
- Product of perpendicular slopes: m₁ × m₂ = -1 (always).

**Example:** Find the equation of a line perpendicular to y = 4x - 7 passing through (2, 1).
- Slope of given line: 4. Perpendicular slope: -1/4.
- y - 1 = -1/4(x - 2)
- y - 1 = -x/4 + 1/2
- **y = -x/4 + 3/2**

---

## Special Lines

**Horizontal lines:** y = k (where k is a constant). Slope = 0.
- y = 4 means every point on the line has y-coordinate 4, regardless of x.

**Vertical lines:** x = k. Slope is undefined.
- x = -3 means every point on the line has x-coordinate -3, regardless of y.

## Practice

1. Find the slope between (-2, 5) and (4, -1).
2. Write in slope-intercept form: slope = -3, y-intercept = 7.
3. Find the equation of the line through (0, -4) and (5, 6).
4. Determine if these lines are parallel, perpendicular, or neither: y = 2x + 1 and y = -1/2 x + 4.
5. A company's cost is $200 for startup plus $15 per unit. Write the cost equation. What is the cost for 30 units?
6. Find the equation of a line through (3, -2) with slope 4.

**Answers:**
1. m = (-1 - 5)/(4 - -2) = -6/6 = **-1**
2. **y = -3x + 7**
3. m = (6 - (-4))/(5 - 0) = 10/5 = 2; b = -4; **y = 2x - 4**
4. Slopes 2 and -1/2 are negative reciprocals (2 × -1/2 = -1). **Perpendicular.**
5. C = 15u + 200; for 30 units: C = 15(30) + 200 = **$650**
6. y - (-2) = 4(x - 3) → y + 2 = 4x - 12 → **y = 4x - 14**
