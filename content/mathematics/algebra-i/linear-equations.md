## Solving Linear Equations

A linear equation is an equation in which the variable appears to the first power only — no x², no x³, just x. The graph of a linear equation is always a straight line. Solving a linear equation means finding the value (or values) of the variable that make the equation true. This is one of the most fundamental skills in algebra and appears in virtually every mathematical application.

---

## The Fundamental Principle: Maintaining Balance

Solving any equation rests on one idea: whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other side. Both sides must remain equal throughout the process.

The goal is always to isolate the variable — get it alone on one side of the equation with a coefficient of 1.

---

## Multi-Step Linear Equations

Building on one-step and two-step equations, multi-step equations require:
1. Simplify each side if needed (distribute, combine like terms)
2. Move all variable terms to one side
3. Move all constants to the other side
4. Divide by the coefficient

**Example:** 5x + 3 = 3x + 11

Step 1 — Move variable terms to one side (subtract 3x from both sides):
- 5x - 3x + 3 = 11
- 2x + 3 = 11

Step 2 — Move constants to the other side (subtract 3):
- 2x = 8

Step 3 — Divide:
- x = **4**

Check: 5(4) + 3 = 23. And 3(4) + 11 = 23. ✓

---

## Equations with Parentheses

Use the distributive property to clear parentheses first, then solve.

**Example:** 3(x - 4) + 2x = 23

Distribute: 3x - 12 + 2x = 23
Combine like terms: 5x - 12 = 23
Add 12: 5x = 35
Divide: x = **7**

Check: 3(7 - 4) + 2(7) = 3(3) + 14 = 9 + 14 = 23. ✓

**Example with nested parentheses:** 2[3(x + 1) - 4] = 10

Inner parentheses first: 2[3x + 3 - 4] = 10
Simplify inside: 2[3x - 1] = 10
Distribute: 6x - 2 = 10
Solve: 6x = 12 → x = **2**

---

## Equations with Fractions

To clear fractions, multiply every term on both sides by the Least Common Denominator (LCD).

**Example:** x/3 + 1/2 = 5/6

LCD of 3, 2, and 6 is 6. Multiply every term by 6:
- 6(x/3) + 6(1/2) = 6(5/6)
- 2x + 3 = 5
- 2x = 2
- x = **1**

Check: 1/3 + 1/2 = 2/6 + 3/6 = 5/6. ✓

---

## Equations with Decimals

Multiply every term by a power of 10 to eliminate decimals, then solve.

**Example:** 0.5x + 1.2 = 3.7

Multiply by 10: 5x + 12 = 37
Solve: 5x = 25 → x = **5**

---

## Special Cases: No Solution and Infinite Solutions

Most linear equations have exactly one solution. But two special cases exist.

**No solution:** The variable cancels out and you're left with a false statement.

**Example:** 3x + 5 = 3x - 2
- Subtract 3x: 5 = -2
- 5 ≠ -2. This is always false. **No solution.**

This means the lines are parallel — they never intersect.

**Infinite solutions:** The variable cancels and you're left with a true statement.

**Example:** 2(x + 3) = 2x + 6
- Distribute: 2x + 6 = 2x + 6
- Subtract 2x: 6 = 6
- 6 = 6 is always true. **Infinitely many solutions** — any value of x works.

This means both sides are identical equations — the same line.

## Setting Up Equations from Word Problems

The skill of translating real situations into equations is more important than the algebra itself.

**Step-by-step translation process:**
1. Identify what you're solving for — define your variable with a label.
2. Translate relationships into mathematical terms.
3. Write the equation.
4. Solve and check that the answer makes sense in context.

**Example:** Two truck drivers are 480 miles apart, driving toward each other. One drives 55 mph, the other 65 mph. When will they meet?

- They move toward each other, so their speeds add: 55 + 65 = 120 mph combined.
- Let t = time in hours. Distance = rate × time.
- 120t = 480
- t = **4 hours**

**Example:** One number is 3 more than twice another. Their sum is 33. Find both numbers.
- Let n = the smaller number.
- Larger number = 2n + 3
- Sum: n + (2n + 3) = 33
- 3n + 3 = 33 → 3n = 30 → n = **10**
- Larger: 2(10) + 3 = **23**
- Check: 10 + 23 = 33. ✓

---

## Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1:** Forgetting to distribute to every term in parentheses.
- 3(x + 4) ≠ 3x + 4. It equals 3x + 12.

**Mistake 2:** Moving a term to the other side without changing its sign.
- If 2x + 5 = 15, you don't write 2x = 15 + 5. Subtract 5: 2x = 15 - 5 = 10.

**Mistake 3:** Not checking the solution. A two-second check catches most errors.

Solve each equation:

1. 6x - 5 = 4x + 9
2. 4(x + 2) - 3x = 11
3. x/4 - 3/2 = 1/4
4. 0.3x + 0.6 = 2.1
5. 5(2x - 1) = 10x + 3
6. A cable is cut into two pieces. One piece is 4 times longer than the other. The total length is 85 feet. Find both lengths.

**Answers:**
1. 2x = 14 → x = **7**
2. 4x + 8 - 3x = 11 → x + 8 = 11 → x = **3**
3. Multiply by 4: x - 6 = 1 → x = **7**
4. Multiply by 10: 3x + 6 = 21 → 3x = 15 → x = **5**
5. 10x - 5 = 10x + 3 → -5 = 3. **No solution.**
6. Shorter piece = s; longer piece = 4s. s + 4s = 85 → 5s = 85 → s = **17 ft** and 4s = **68 ft**
