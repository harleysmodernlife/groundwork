## Payday Loans, Rent-to-Own, and Predatory Lending

Some financial products are specifically designed to extract money from people who have very little of it. They're legal. They're widely available. They target people in financial distress and make that distress significantly worse.

Knowing how they work is how you avoid them.

---

## Payday Loans

A payday loan is a short-term, small-dollar loan — typically $200-500 — that's due in full on your next payday, usually in two weeks.

**How they work:** You write the lender a postdated check (or authorize a bank withdrawal) for the loan amount plus a fee. The typical fee is $15-30 per $100 borrowed.

**The math:** $15 per $100 for a two-week loan sounds small. But annualized, that's an APR of approximately 390%. A $300 loan for two weeks costs $45 in fees.

**The trap:** Most people who take a payday loan can't pay it back in full two weeks later — because if they had $345 available on payday, they wouldn't have needed the $300 loan. So they "roll over" the loan: pay another fee to extend it another two weeks. This can repeat indefinitely. It's not uncommon for people to pay $500-700 in fees while still owing the original $300.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau found that 80% of payday loans are rolled over or renewed, and most borrowers spend more in fees than the original loan amount.

**Better alternatives:**
- Credit union payday alternative loans (PALs) — specifically designed as safer alternatives, capped at 28% APR
- Negotiating a payment plan directly with whoever you owe
- Community assistance programs (utility assistance, food banks, nonprofit emergency funds)
- Asking an employer for a paycheck advance
- Personal loan from a credit union at rates vastly lower than a payday lender

## Rent-to-Own

Rent-to-own stores (Rent-A-Center is the largest) let you take home furniture, electronics, and appliances with small weekly or monthly payments — and no credit check.

**The math:** A $500 television might rent for $25/week with ownership after 78 weeks. Total paid: $1,950. You've paid four times the item's retail price.

The effective APR on rent-to-own arrangements often exceeds 100% when calculated properly. It's an extremely expensive way to get something.

**The marketing:** Rent-to-own stores advertise affordability ("only $25 a week!") while obscuring the total cost. They know their customers are comparing to the weekly payment, not the total they'll pay.

**Better alternatives:**
- Buying second-hand on Facebook Marketplace or Craigslist — often 50-70% below retail
- Waiting and saving until you can purchase
- Shopping refurbished electronics from manufacturers or reputable resellers
- Asking if family or friends have something they're not using

## Buy Here, Pay Here Auto Lots

"Buy here, pay here" car dealers sell to people with poor or no credit, financing the purchase themselves rather than through a bank.

**How they work:** High prices (often above market value for the vehicle), high interest rates (frequently 20-29%), with weekly or biweekly payments made directly to the dealer. GPS tracking is common so they can repossess quickly. Many vehicles need immediate repairs despite just being sold.

**The risk:** Default and repossession rates are very high. When you miss a payment and lose the car, you still may owe the deficiency balance — the difference between what you owed and what they could sell the car for.

**Better alternative:** A used car purchased through a private seller with an inspection by an independent mechanic, financed through a credit union if needed, at a significantly lower rate than a buy-here-pay-here lot.

## High-Interest Personal Loans

Online lenders and some storefronts offer personal loans to subprime borrowers at rates that can range from 36% to 300%+ APR. These are marketed with language like "fast funding," "no credit check," and "get up to $5,000."

A $2,000 loan at 150% APR with a 12-month term would have monthly payments over $350 — and you'd pay more than $4,000 by the end.

**Rule:** Any personal loan above 36% APR should be treated with extreme skepticism. Explore every other option first.

---

## Tax Refund Anticipation Loans

Some tax preparers offer to give you your refund immediately for a fee — effectively lending you your own money while the IRS processes your return.

The IRS now processes most refunds in 21 days or less. A refund anticipation loan costs money to get your refund a couple weeks faster than you would otherwise. It's almost never worth it.

---

## The Pattern

Predatory financial products share common features:
- Targeting people in financial stress who feel they have no other options
- High fees or interest rates obscured by focusing on small periodic payments
- Automatic renewal or rollover mechanisms that extend the obligation
- Weak or nonexistent credit checks (which is how they justify the high rates)

The best protection is knowing they exist, understanding how the math works, and knowing what alternatives are available before you're in a desperate situation — because desperation is exactly when these products are most appealing and most dangerous.
