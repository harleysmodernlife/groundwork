## What to Avoid: Day Trading, Hot Tips, and Crypto Hype

The investing industry generates enormous amounts of content designed to make you think active trading, sophisticated strategies, and novel assets are the path to wealth. They largely aren't — and for typical investors, they're reliably ways to lose money, time, and mental energy.

---

## Day Trading

Day trading is buying and selling securities within the same day, trying to profit from short-term price movements.

**The data:** Studies of day traders consistently show that the vast majority lose money. A landmark study of Taiwanese day traders found that fewer than 1% were consistently profitable over multiple years. U.S. studies show similar results.

Why? Several reasons:

**Transaction costs:** Even with commission-free trading, the bid-ask spread (the gap between what you can buy and sell at) is a constant tax on every trade.

**Competition:** When you trade, you're competing against professional algorithms, quant funds, and people who do nothing else. You don't have an edge.

**Taxes:** Short-term capital gains (assets held less than a year) are taxed at ordinary income rates — often 22-37%. Long-term gains are taxed at 0-20%. Frequent trading destroys tax efficiency.

**The time cost:** Day trading is a job. A bad-paying job with no benefits and negative expected returns for most practitioners.

The appeal is real — it feels active, exciting, like being in the game. It's largely a way to feel productive while losing money.

## Hot Tips and Stock Picks

A neighbor who made money on a stock. A Reddit post about the next big thing. An email newsletter promising 20x returns. A financial influencer picking stocks on YouTube.

The problem isn't just that the tipster might be wrong. It's that:

**If information were that valuable, it wouldn't be shared freely.** If someone genuinely had information that made a stock a sure thing, they wouldn't post about it — they'd invest everything they have first.

**Markets are highly efficient at incorporating public information.** By the time you hear about a hot stock, thousands of professional investors have already evaluated it and traded on it. The edge is gone before you can act on it.

**Even if it works once, it doesn't scale into a strategy.** Someone who got lucky on a stock tip often attributes skill to what was chance, takes bigger bets, and eventually loses.

The alternative — boring index funds — regularly beats the professionals who pick stocks for a living. There's no shame in it.

## Cryptocurrency and Speculative Assets

Cryptocurrency isn't inherently illegitimate, but it has attracted more hype, fraud, and poor decision-making than almost any other asset class in recent decades.

**What's real:** Cryptocurrency exists, blockchain technology has legitimate applications, some people have made significant money.

**What's also real:** The crypto space has produced an enormous number of scams, rug pulls, failed exchanges, and fraudulent projects (FTX, Luna/Terra, countless others). Many people have lost life savings. The volatility is extreme — assets that go up 10x can and do go down 90%.

**The honest assessment:** Cryptocurrency is highly speculative. If you invest in it, treat it as a speculation, not an investment — put in only money you can genuinely afford to lose entirely, because that outcome is plausible.

Allocating 5% or less of your portfolio to crypto as speculation is defensible. Putting your savings, emergency fund, or retirement money into it is not.

---

## Leverage and Margin

Buying on margin means borrowing from your broker to buy more investments than you could with your own money. Leveraged ETFs amplify returns — and losses — by 2x or 3x.

These are tools for sophisticated investors with specific risk management needs. For most people, they're ways to turn a bad market into a catastrophic personal loss.

A 50% market decline in a 2x leveraged ETF isn't a 100% loss — it's actually worse, because of how compounding works on leveraged products. These instruments can go to zero in ways that an unleveraged index fund cannot.

---

## "Get Rich Quick" Anything

The pattern: a compelling story about extraordinary returns, urgency, a limited opportunity, and someone who wants you to act now.

Whether it's a trading course, a crypto project, an MLM investment, or a too-good-to-be-true annuity — the structure is the same. The only reliable outcome is that money flows from you to them.

Real wealth is built slowly, through consistent contributions, low costs, and time. That's not exciting. It doesn't make for viral content. But it works.

---

## The Alternative

Index funds. Low cost. Broad diversification. Automatic contributions. Leave it alone.

This produces better outcomes than active trading for most individual investors. The research is clear on this. The hard part is accepting that boring investing is better investing — and that the excitement of active trading is a cost, not a benefit.
