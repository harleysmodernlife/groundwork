## How to Start Investing with Almost Nothing

The most common reason people give for not investing: "I don't have enough money to start."

This is false in most cases, and understanding why matters — because delaying is the one thing that genuinely costs you long-term.

---

## There Is No Minimum

Fractional shares, zero-minimum funds, and commission-free trading have eliminated the capital barriers that once existed.

**At Fidelity:** You can buy fractional shares of any stock or ETF starting at $1. The FZROX (total market index fund) has no minimum initial investment. You can open an account with $0 and start with your first paycheck.

**At Schwab:** Same model. No account minimums, fractional shares, zero-minimum index funds.

**At Vanguard:** The ETF versions of their funds (VTI, VOO, etc.) can be purchased for the price of one share — often $100-250. Their traditional mutual funds have minimums ($1,000 or $3,000), but the ETF equivalent has no meaningful minimum.

**At most 401(k) providers:** The minimum contribution is whatever you set — even 1% of your paycheck.

If you have $50, you can invest $50.

---

## Where to Start

**If your employer offers a 401(k) with a match:** Start here. This is the most important first step. If your employer matches 50% of your contributions up to 6% of your salary, and you're not contributing at least 6%, you're leaving free money on the table. A $50,000 salary with a 6% contribution is $3,000/year from you and $1,500 from your employer. That $1,500 match is an instant 50% return before any market return.

Contribute at least enough to get the full match. This is the single highest-return financial decision available to most employed people.

**If no employer match or no employer plan:** Open a Roth IRA (more on this in the retirement course). You contribute after-tax dollars; the money grows tax-free; qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free. For most people early in their career — when their tax bracket is likely lower than it will be in their peak earning years — the Roth IRA is the right starting account.

The annual contribution limit for a Roth IRA is $7,000 (as of 2024). You don't have to contribute the maximum to benefit. $50/month is $600/year and meaningfully better than nothing.

**If you have no retirement account:** Open a taxable brokerage account at Fidelity, Schwab, or Vanguard. Select a broad index fund. Set up automatic monthly contributions.

---

## Automating the Contribution

The behavioral enemy of investing is inertia. If you have to make an active decision every month to invest, some months you won't.

Automation removes the decision. Set up automatic contributions:

- From your paycheck into your 401(k) — done at the employer level
- Monthly transfer from checking to your Roth IRA
- Monthly purchase of your chosen index fund in a brokerage

Once automated, the contributions happen whether or not you remember, whether or not the market is up or down, whether or not you feel like it. This is one of the most powerful things you can do for your financial future.

---

## Dollar-Cost Averaging

Investing a fixed amount on a regular schedule — regardless of market conditions — is called dollar-cost averaging (DCA).

When prices are low, your fixed contribution buys more shares. When prices are high, it buys fewer. Over time, this averages out and reduces the risk of putting a large sum in at a market peak.

For someone investing $200/month, this happens automatically. You don't need to think about it. The math takes care of itself.

---

## The Only Thing That Prevents Starting

Starting with little is straightforward. The main obstacle isn't capability — it's getting past the feeling that you need to know more before you start, or that you need to have more before you start.

You don't. Open an account. Set up a monthly automatic contribution to a broad index fund. Increase the amount as your income grows. Leave it alone.

The investing knowledge you accumulate over years will make you better at it. But the time spent learning is not a substitute for actually starting. The compounding clock starts the day you make your first contribution. Every month you wait is a month of growth you can't get back.

Start with $50 if that's what you have. Start today.
