## Inflation: Why Saving Alone Isn't Enough

Saving money is essential. But saving money in a low-interest account while inflation runs at 3-4% means your savings are losing purchasing power every year. You have more dollars, but each one buys less.

This is the invisible tax that makes simply saving money in a traditional bank account insufficient as a long-term financial strategy.

---

## What Inflation Is

Inflation is the general increase in prices over time. When inflation is 3%, something that cost $100 this year costs $103 next year, on average. Wages, goods, rent, healthcare — everything tends to rise.

The U.S. Federal Reserve targets 2% annual inflation as healthy for the economy. Real inflation rates vary: the 2021-2023 period saw 6-9% inflation, well above the Fed's target. The 2010s saw relatively modest inflation of 1-2%.

Over long periods, even 2% inflation compounds significantly:

- $100 in 1990 had the purchasing power of approximately $230 in 2024
- $100 saved in a mattress in 1990 is worth $43 in 1990 dollars today

---

## The Math of Losing Ground

If you have $10,000 in a savings account earning 0.5% interest, and inflation is 3%:

- Your account grows to $10,050 after one year
- But the purchasing power of $10,000 a year ago now costs $10,300 to replicate
- Your real purchasing power has declined: you're effectively $250 poorer

The nominal value of your account went up. The real value went down.

This continues every year. Over 30 years, the accumulated effect is substantial. $50,000 kept in a 0.5% savings account for 30 years at 3% inflation would have the purchasing power of roughly $20,000 in today's dollars. You didn't spend it — inflation spent it for you.

## The CPI and Measuring Inflation

The most commonly cited inflation measure is the **Consumer Price Index (CPI)**, calculated by the Bureau of Labor Statistics. It tracks prices across a basket of goods and services representative of typical consumer spending: food, housing, transportation, healthcare, education, and more.

The **core CPI** excludes food and energy prices because they're volatile — it's used by economists to identify underlying inflation trends.

**Personal inflation rates vary.** The CPI is an average. If you spend a high proportion of your income on healthcare or education (which have historically inflated faster than overall CPI), your personal experience of inflation is worse than the headline number. If you spend minimally on categories with high inflation, yours may be lower.

## What Keeps Up with Inflation

**High-yield savings accounts.** When savings rates are above inflation — as they were in 2023-2024 when HYSAs were paying 4-5% and CPI had fallen back toward 3% — savings accounts can keep pace or slightly beat inflation. But this isn't guaranteed; rate environments change.

**I-Bonds.** Treasury inflation-protected bonds that adjust with inflation. There's a $10,000 annual purchase limit per person. They're a specialized inflation hedge, not a comprehensive solution.

**Stocks and diversified investments.** Historically, the U.S. stock market has returned approximately 10% annually before inflation, or roughly 7% after inflation. Over long periods, this has far outpaced inflation. This is the primary reason long-term investing is important — not just as a way to earn returns, but as a way to preserve and grow purchasing power.

**Real assets.** Real estate, commodities, and other physical assets have historically held value against inflation better than cash.

---

## The Practical Implication

Money you won't need for more than 5-10 years should generally be invested, not held in savings. Savings accounts are for:
- Your emergency fund (3-6 months of expenses)
- Money you'll need within 1-3 years (down payment, car, near-term goals)

Money with a longer time horizon should be invested — and why will be clear in the next lesson, on compound interest.

The goal isn't to abandon savings. It's to understand that saving alone is not a complete financial plan. Inflation silently erodes wealth held as cash. Investing is how you prevent that erosion and build actual long-term purchasing power.
