## Closing Costs and What to Expect at Closing

Closing is the final step in buying a home — the day you sign the paperwork, pay what you owe, and get the keys. It's also the day a significant amount of money changes hands beyond the down payment. Understanding what you're paying and why prevents the shock of discovering you owe $12,000 you weren't planning for.

---

## What Are Closing Costs?

Closing costs are fees associated with finalizing the mortgage and completing the real estate transaction. They're paid at the closing table (or transferred beforehand) and typically total 2-5% of the purchase price.

On a $300,000 purchase, that's $6,000-15,000 in addition to your down payment.

---

## Buyer's Closing Costs

**Loan origination and lender fees:**
- Origination fee: 0.5-1% of loan amount
- Underwriting fee: $400-900
- Application fee: $0-500
- Discount points (if you chose to buy down your rate)

**Third-party service fees:**
- Appraisal: $400-700
- Home inspection: $300-600 (often paid before closing)
- Title search: $150-400
- Title insurance — owner's policy: 0.5-1% of purchase price (one-time, protects you)
- Title insurance — lender's policy: Required by lender; separate from owner's policy
- Survey: $500-1,000 (sometimes required or recommended)
- Attorney or settlement agent: $500-1,500 (required in some states)
- Pest/termite inspection: $75-150

**Prepaid items (not fees, but cash you provide upfront):**
- Prepaid homeowners insurance: First year's premium, paid at closing
- Prepaid mortgage interest: Interest from closing date to end of the month
- Initial escrow deposit: Usually 2-3 months of property taxes and homeowners insurance, held in escrow to start the account

---

## Seller's Closing Costs

Sellers typically pay:
- Real estate commissions (5-6% of sale price — the largest cost)
- Transfer taxes and recording fees
- Prorated property taxes (taxes accrued through the closing date)
- Any agreed-upon seller concessions (paying part of buyer's closing costs)
- Attorney fees

---

## The Closing Disclosure

Three business days before your closing date, your lender is required to send you the Closing Disclosure — a detailed statement of all final loan terms, fees, and costs.

Compare it carefully to your Loan Estimate. Most costs should be very close to what was estimated. Items that can change: third-party service fees (within limits). Items that can't change: your interest rate and origination fee (if you locked the rate).

Flag anything that looks different from your Loan Estimate. You have the right to ask for explanations and can delay closing to resolve discrepancies.

---

## Seller Concessions

In negotiations, you can ask the seller to pay a portion of your closing costs. This is called a seller concession.

Sellers may agree to concessions when:
- The market favors buyers
- The home has been sitting unsold
- You've requested it as part of the offer or post-inspection negotiation

Most loan programs cap seller concessions at 3-6% of the loan amount. Concessions allow you to roll some closing cost burden onto the seller — effectively reducing your cash needed at closing.

---

## What You Need at Closing

**Cashier's check or wire transfer:** Personal checks aren't accepted for amounts over a small threshold. Closing costs and down payment typically require a cashier's check from your bank or a wire transfer. Get wiring instructions directly from your title company (confirmed verbally) — wire fraud targeting home buyers is common.

**Government-issued ID:** You're signing legal documents. Multiple forms of ID may be required.

**All documentation requested by your lender:** They'll notify you if anything is outstanding.

---

## What Happens at Closing

Closing can be in person with the title agent/attorney, or in some cases remote with a notary. It typically takes 1-2 hours.

You'll sign a significant amount of paperwork — the mortgage note, the deed of trust, various disclosures, the settlement statement. Your agent or attorney can answer questions about anything you're signing.

After everything is signed and funds are transferred, the deed is recorded with the county and you receive the keys. You own the house.

---

## Reducing Cash Needed at Closing

Several strategies reduce your out-of-pocket at closing:
- Negotiate seller concessions
- Choose a closing date at the beginning of the month (less prepaid interest)
- Compare lenders' closing cost estimates — they vary
- Check for down payment assistance programs that cover closing costs
- Ask if your lender offers a no-closing-cost option (costs are rolled into a slightly higher rate)

The no-closing-cost option makes sense if you don't plan to keep the loan long enough to recoup the higher rate. Run the math for your expected time horizon.
