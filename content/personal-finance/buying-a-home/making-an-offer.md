## Making an Offer and Negotiating

The offer is where you commit to your interest in a home and negotiate the terms of the purchase. Understanding the components of an offer — and how negotiations typically work — helps you act decisively and strategically.

---

## Components of a Purchase Offer

**Purchase price:** The amount you're willing to pay. May be at, above, or below the listing price depending on market conditions, property condition, and how long it's been listed.

**Earnest money deposit:** A good-faith deposit — typically 1-3% of the purchase price — paid shortly after your offer is accepted. Held in escrow until closing, where it applies toward your down payment. If the deal falls through for a reason covered by your contingencies, you get it back. If you back out for reasons not covered by contingencies, you typically forfeit it.

**Closing date:** When you want to close and take possession. Usually 30-60 days from acceptance, though this can be negotiated. Sellers sometimes prioritize offers with flexible closing dates if they need time to move.

**Contingencies:** Conditions that must be met for the deal to proceed. If they're not met, you can exit without penalty and recover your earnest money. Standard contingencies include:

- *Inspection contingency:* You have the right to conduct a home inspection and negotiate repairs or exit.
- *Financing contingency:* The purchase is contingent on your loan being approved. Protects you if your financing falls through.
- *Appraisal contingency:* If the home appraises below the purchase price, you can renegotiate or exit.
- *Sale contingency:* Your purchase is contingent on selling your current home first. Weakens your offer significantly.

In competitive markets, buyers sometimes waive contingencies to be more attractive. Waiving an inspection contingency is risky — you're buying the home as-is without legal recourse for undisclosed defects you would have discovered.

**Inclusions and exclusions:** Items to be included with the sale (appliances, fixtures, window treatments) or explicitly excluded.

---

## Reading the Market

**Seller's market (low inventory, high competition):** Homes receive multiple offers quickly, often at or above asking price. Waiving contingencies, offering above asking, larger earnest money deposits, and flexible closing dates make offers more competitive.

**Buyer's market (high inventory, slow sales):** Buyers have more leverage. Offers below asking price are reasonable. Sellers may negotiate more on repairs and concessions.

**How to gauge:** Ask your agent how quickly similar homes are selling and whether they're selling above or below asking.

---

## How to Determine Your Offer Price

**Comparable sales analysis (comps):** Your agent pulls recent sales of similar homes (size, condition, location) to anchor your offer. A home priced at $325,000 in a market where comps are at $310,000 is overpriced.

**Days on market:** A home that's been sitting for 60+ days has more negotiating room. A home listed 2 days ago in a hot market has less.

**Condition:** Factor in significant repairs identified in your pre-offer walkthrough. A home needing a new roof has lower value than the listing price implies.

---

## After Your Offer Is Submitted

The seller can:
- **Accept** as written
- **Counter** with different terms (usually price, closing date, or contingencies)
- **Reject**

Counteroffers are normal. Each counter brings you closer or reveals that you're too far apart.

**Negotiating repairs after inspection:** After the inspection, you can request that the seller:
- Repair specific items before closing
- Provide a credit (reducing your closing costs or price) to compensate for issues
- Reduce the purchase price

Sellers don't have to agree. In a hot market, they may decline and let you walk away. In a slow market, they're more motivated to accommodate. Your agent provides guidance based on current conditions.

---

## Things That Can Derail a Deal

- Inspection reveals major undisclosed problems
- Appraisal comes in significantly below purchase price
- Financing falls through (changes in your financial picture during underwriting)
- Title issues discovered during title search
- Seller can't vacate by closing date
- Either party has a change of heart

Many deals encounter bumps. An experienced agent has navigated these before and can help you handle them without losing your head.

---

## Trust Your Agent, But Know What You're Signing

Your agent will draft the offer — but you're the one signing it and assuming the financial obligation. Read what you're signing. Understand the contingencies. Know the timeline. Ask questions if something is unclear.

The offer and purchase contract are legally binding documents. "I didn't understand" is not an excuse that protects you.
