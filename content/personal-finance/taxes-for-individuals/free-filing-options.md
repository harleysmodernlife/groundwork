## Free Filing Options

Tax preparation is a multi-billion-dollar industry built largely on the fact that most people don't know they can file their taxes for free. A significant portion of Americans pay $150-400 to a tax preparer or software company for returns that qualify for free filing options.

---

## IRS Free File

If your **adjusted gross income (AGI) is $79,000 or less** (the limit may adjust annually), you qualify for IRS Free File — a program where the IRS partners with commercial tax software companies to offer their products for free.

Access it at **irs.gov/freefile**. This is the official IRS page — go directly here rather than to the software companies' websites, because the companies often use deceptive practices to steer people toward paid products.

**What's included:** Federal return preparation and filing. State return varies by provider — some include it free, others charge.

**The catch:** The free tier may have fewer features than the paid version. If your return is simple (W-2 income, standard deduction, basic credits), the free version is more than adequate.

---

## IRS Free File Fillable Forms

If your AGI is **above $79,000** or you just prefer to do it yourself, the IRS offers **Free File Fillable Forms** — electronic versions of the actual tax forms with basic math calculations.

This is closer to doing your taxes by hand than using guided software. It doesn't ask you interview questions or explain the forms. But it's genuinely free, with no income limit, and adequate for people who understand their return.

Access at irs.gov/freefile → "Use Free File Fillable Forms."

---

## IRS Direct File

The IRS now offers **Direct File** — a government-run free filing tool where you can file directly with the IRS without going through a third-party company.

As of 2024-2025, Direct File is available in a growing number of states. It handles W-2 income, Social Security income, interest income, and common credits (earned income credit, child tax credit). It doesn't yet handle all situations (Schedule C self-employment, complex investments, etc.).

Check irs.gov/directfile to see if it's available in your state and whether your situation is supported. For eligible filers, it's the simplest, most direct option — file directly with the IRS for free, no commercial software involved.

## VITA: Volunteer Income Tax Assistance

VITA provides **free in-person tax preparation** from IRS-certified volunteers for people who generally make $67,000 or less, have disabilities, or speak limited English.

VITA volunteers are trained and certified. They prepare your return while you watch — you walk away with your return filed. This is valuable for people who are uncomfortable doing it themselves or who have situations they're unsure how to handle.

Find a VITA site at irs.gov/VITA or call 211.

**TCE (Tax Counseling for the Elderly):** Similar program specifically for seniors age 60+, often focusing on pension and retirement-related tax issues.

---

## Commercial Software (When You Pay)

TurboTax, H&R Block, TaxAct, and similar companies offer genuinely useful software with good guidance — but they charge for it. Their free tiers often have significant limitations (no Schedule C, no investment income), and their upselling is aggressive.

If you have a complex return (self-employment income with multiple deductions, rental properties, business ownership, significant investments) and aren't comfortable handling it yourself, professional software or a CPA may be worth the cost. But most people's returns are simpler than they fear.

---

## When to Get a CPA or Tax Professional

A tax professional is worth hiring when:
- You're self-employed with significant income and complex deductions
- You own rental properties
- You had major life events with tax implications (sold a business, received inheritance, went through divorce)
- You received a notice from the IRS and don't know how to respond
- You suspect you've been filing incorrectly

For straightforward returns — W-2 income, standard deduction, one state — free filing options are genuinely adequate. The complexity people feel often reflects unfamiliarity rather than actual complexity.

A certified public accountant (CPA) is higher-credentialed than an "enrolled agent" (EA), who is higher than an unlicensed tax preparer. For significant complexity, a CPA with tax expertise is worth the cost.
