## Tax Brackets: How Progressive Taxation Works

One of the most persistent misconceptions about income tax is how tax brackets actually work. People say things like "I don't want to earn more because it'll put me in a higher bracket and I'll take home less." That's not how it works — and the misunderstanding costs people real decisions.

---

## The Progressive Tax System

The U.S. uses a **progressive tax system**: as income increases, higher portions of that income are taxed at higher rates. But crucially, not all of your income is taxed at the higher rate — only the portion that falls within each bracket.

Think of tax brackets like water filling glasses of different sizes. The first glass fills at one price. The second glass starts filling only after the first is full, at a slightly higher price. And so on.

---

## How It Actually Works (2024 Brackets, Single Filer)

| Bracket | Rate | On Income From... |
|---------|------|-------------------|
| 1st | 10% | $0 to $11,600 |
| 2nd | 12% | $11,601 to $47,150 |
| 3rd | 22% | $47,151 to $100,525 |
| 4th | 24% | $100,526 to $191,950 |
| 5th | 32% | $191,951 to $243,725 |
| 6th | 35% | $243,726 to $609,350 |
| 7th | 37% | Over $609,350 |

*(Brackets adjust annually for inflation. Married filing jointly uses different thresholds.)*

**Example:** Someone earns $60,000 in taxable income.

- The first $11,600 is taxed at 10% = $1,160
- Income from $11,601 to $47,150 is taxed at 12% = $4,266
- Income from $47,151 to $60,000 is taxed at 22% = $2,827

Total tax: $1,160 + $4,266 + $2,827 = **$8,253**

Their **marginal tax rate** is 22% — the rate on their last dollar of income. But their **effective tax rate** is $8,253 ÷ $60,000 = **13.8%** — what they actually pay on average.

---

## The "Moving into a Higher Bracket" Myth

If you earn $47,000 and get a $500 raise to $47,500, you don't pay 22% on all $47,500. You pay 22% only on the $350 above the bracket threshold. The raise increases your total tax by about $77. You take home approximately $423 more.

Earning more always increases your take-home pay. Moving into a higher bracket never makes you worse off.

---

## Taxable Income vs Gross Income

The brackets apply to **taxable income** — not your total earnings.

Taxable income = Gross income − Adjustments − Standard deduction (or itemized deductions)

The **standard deduction** for 2024 is:
- $14,600 for single filers
- $29,200 for married filing jointly

This means a single filer's first $14,600 of income is completely untaxed. If you earn $50,000, your taxable income is approximately $35,400 — not $50,000.

This is why it's important to calculate taxes on taxable income, not gross income. The standard deduction alone significantly reduces most people's tax bill.

---

## Capital Gains Rates

Investment gains (selling stocks, real estate) are taxed differently from ordinary income.

**Short-term capital gains** (assets held less than a year): taxed at ordinary income rates — same brackets above.

**Long-term capital gains** (assets held more than a year): taxed at lower rates — 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on income. This is a significant advantage for long-term investors.

At $60,000 of ordinary income (single), long-term capital gains are taxed at 15%. At lower incomes, they may be taxed at 0%.

---

## Why This Matters

Understanding brackets helps you:

- Make accurate tax estimates
- Evaluate tax-saving strategies (contributions to a 401(k) reduce taxable income, saving taxes at your marginal rate)
- Not make fear-based decisions about earning more
- Plan around long-term capital gains rates when selling investments
