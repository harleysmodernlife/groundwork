## Tax-Advantaged Accounts: IRA, HSA, FSA

The tax code offers several special account types that provide significant tax benefits for saving — for retirement, healthcare, or other purposes. Using these accounts intelligently is one of the most reliable legal ways to reduce your tax burden and build wealth.

---

## The IRA (Individual Retirement Account)

An IRA is a personal retirement savings account with tax advantages. There are two main types: Traditional and Roth. Both have the same annual contribution limit: $7,000 in 2024 ($8,000 if age 50 or older).

**Traditional IRA:**
- Contributions may be tax-deductible (reducing your taxable income now)
- Money grows tax-deferred — no taxes until withdrawal
- Withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income
- Required minimum distributions starting at age 73

Whether you can deduct Traditional IRA contributions depends on whether you have a workplace plan and your income. If you or your spouse have a 401(k) or similar plan at work, the deductibility phases out at certain income levels.

**Roth IRA:**
- Contributions are made with after-tax money (no upfront deduction)
- Money grows tax-free
- Qualified withdrawals in retirement are completely tax-free, including earnings
- No required minimum distributions during your lifetime
- Contributions (not earnings) can be withdrawn anytime without penalty — there's limited flexibility here

Income limits for Roth IRA eligibility: phases out for single filers at $146,000-$161,000 AGI (2024), married filing jointly at $230,000-$240,000.

**Which to choose:**

If your current tax rate is lower than what you expect in retirement (early career, lower income): Roth is usually better. Pay taxes now at the lower rate.

If your current rate is higher than expected retirement rate: Traditional is usually better. Deduct now at the higher rate.

When uncertain, Roth has flexibility advantages (access to contributions, no RMDs, no uncertainty about future tax rates) that often favor it for younger savers.

## The HSA (Health Savings Account)

The HSA is available to people enrolled in a High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP). It has a unique triple tax advantage that no other account offers:

1. **Contributions are tax-deductible** (above-the-line, so no itemizing required)
2. **Growth is tax-free** (invest the balance; earnings accumulate without tax)
3. **Withdrawals are tax-free** when used for qualified medical expenses

2024 contribution limits: $4,150 (self-only coverage), $8,300 (family coverage), plus $1,000 catch-up if 55+.

**Medical expenses that qualify** include most doctor visits, prescriptions, dental, vision, mental health, and many other healthcare costs.

**The long-term strategy:** Pay current medical expenses out of pocket if you can, and let the HSA balance grow invested. Save your receipts — there's no time limit on when you can reimburse yourself for qualified expenses. You can pay for a dental visit at 32 and reimburse yourself from the HSA at 55 if you kept the receipt. In retirement, the HSA can function like a Traditional IRA for non-medical expenses (taxable but no penalty after 65).

The HSA is arguably the best savings account in the tax code for people who are eligible.

## The FSA (Flexible Spending Account)

FSAs are employer-sponsored accounts where you contribute pre-tax dollars for healthcare or dependent care expenses. Unlike HSAs, they're available with any health insurance plan.

**Healthcare FSA:**
- 2024 limit: $3,200
- Use for medical, dental, vision expenses
- "Use it or lose it" (most plans allow a $640 rollover or 2.5-month grace period)
- Funds are available at the start of the year — you can spend the full amount even before you've contributed it all

**Dependent Care FSA:**
- Up to $5,000/year (married filing jointly or single)
- Pay for childcare, after-school programs, or day camp for children under 13 while you work
- Also covers adult dependent care (elderly parent day program, etc.)
- Saves you taxes at your marginal rate on the amount contributed

The healthcare FSA is useful but requires planning — you must estimate your medical expenses at the start of the year to set your contribution, and unused money is at risk. The dependent care FSA is less risky and very valuable for working parents with childcare expenses.

## The 401(k) Connection

Contributions to a 401(k) or 403(b) through your employer are also tax-advantaged:

- Traditional 401(k): pre-tax contributions reduce your taxable income now; withdrawals taxed in retirement
- Roth 401(k): after-tax contributions; withdrawals tax-free in retirement

The 2024 employee contribution limit is $23,000 ($30,500 if 50+). This is separate from and much higher than the IRA limit.

**The priority order most financial advisors recommend:**
1. 401(k) up to the employer match (free money)
2. Max out an HSA if eligible
3. Max out a Roth IRA if eligible
4. Return to 401(k) if you have more to invest

This order is a guideline — your specific tax situation, debt load, and income level all affect what's optimal. But for most working people without complex financial situations, this sequence produces very good outcomes.
