## Renters and Homeowners Insurance

These two types of insurance protect where you live and what you own — and they're often misunderstood, skipped, or inadequate.

---

## Renters Insurance

**Who needs it:** Anyone who rents. Period.

The most common misconception: "My landlord has insurance." They do — on the building. Not on your belongings. If there's a fire, your landlord's policy replaces the walls. Your furniture, clothes, electronics, laptop, jewelry, and everything else you own? Not covered by them.

Renters insurance covers three things:

**1. Personal property.** Your stuff — up to the policy limit — if damaged or stolen by covered perils: fire, theft, certain water damage, vandalism.

Insure for the actual replacement value of your belongings. Walk through your apartment mentally and estimate what it would cost to replace everything. $10,000 is a realistic minimum for most furnished apartments when you add up electronics, furniture, clothing, kitchen equipment.

**Replacement cost vs. actual cash value:** Better policies pay replacement cost (what it costs to buy new). Cheaper policies pay actual cash value (depreciated value). A 3-year-old TV worth $600 new might be "worth" $150 in depreciated value. Choose replacement cost coverage.

**2. Liability.** If someone is injured in your apartment and sues you, renters insurance covers your legal defense and damages up to the policy limit. If you accidentally cause a water leak that damages your neighbor's unit, liability covers that too.

**3. Additional living expenses.** If your apartment becomes uninhabitable after a covered event, this covers temporary housing costs while repairs are made.

**Cost:** $15-30/month for meaningful coverage. One of the best financial values available.

**How to get it:** Any major insurer (State Farm, Allstate, Progressive, Lemonade) offers it. Takes about 10 minutes online. Some landlords require it.

## Homeowners Insurance

If you have a mortgage, your lender requires homeowners insurance — they won't lend on an unprotected asset. If you own your home free and clear, you still need it.

Homeowners insurance covers:

**The structure (dwelling coverage):** Damage to your home from covered perils — fire, wind, hail, lightning, theft, vandalism. Flood and earthquake are typically not included and require separate policies.

**Insure for replacement cost, not market value.** The market value of your home includes the land. Insurance only needs to cover rebuilding the structure. These can be very different numbers.

**Personal property:** Same as renters insurance — your belongings, up to the policy limit.

**Liability:** Covers injuries on your property and lawsuits. If a guest slips and sues you, or your dog bites someone, homeowners liability covers it.

**Additional living expenses:** Temporary housing while covered repairs are made.

**What's typically NOT covered:**
- Flood (requires NFIP or private flood policy — essential in flood-prone areas)
- Earthquake (requires separate policy or rider — essential in seismically active regions)
- Sewer backup (available as a rider)
- Normal wear and tear
- Intentional damage
- Home business liability (requires a rider or separate business policy)

**Standard policy types:**
- HO-3 is the most common — covers the dwelling on an open perils basis (all perils except those specifically excluded) and personal property on named perils
- HO-5 is broader — open perils for both dwelling and personal property, generally preferred for more comprehensive protection

## What Both Have in Common

**Inventory your belongings.** Before you need to make a claim is when to document what you own. Walk through your home with your phone and video everything — every room, every item. Store the video in cloud storage or somewhere not in your home. Claims are far easier when you have documentation.

**Umbrella insurance.** If you have significant assets and want additional liability protection, a personal umbrella policy adds $1-5 million in liability coverage on top of auto and homeowners/renters for $150-350/year. Worth considering once you have meaningful assets to protect.

**Review annually.** Did you add significant valuables (engagement ring, high-end electronics)? Check that your coverage limits still reflect what you own. Major items may need scheduled endorsements for full protection.
