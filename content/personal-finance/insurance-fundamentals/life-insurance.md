## Life Insurance: Term vs Whole

Life insurance is simple in purpose: if you die, your beneficiaries receive a lump sum of money. It's not for you — you're dead. It's for the people who depend on your income.

This purpose-driven clarity also tells you when you need it: when people depend on your income. And when you don't: when no one does.

---

## Who Needs Life Insurance

**You need it if:**
- You have children
- You have a spouse or partner who depends on your income (or would be burdened by debts you carry jointly)
- You have aging parents who depend on you financially
- You have a business partner who would be harmed by your death

**You don't need it if:**
- You're single with no dependents
- You have dependents but enough assets (savings, investments) that your death wouldn't leave them financially vulnerable

The point of life insurance is income replacement. If your death doesn't leave anyone financially short, there's no financial need for life insurance.

---

## Term Life Insurance

Term life insurance covers you for a specific period — 10, 20, or 30 years. If you die during the term, your beneficiaries receive the death benefit. If you outlive the term, the policy expires and nothing is paid out.

**This is the right type of life insurance for most people.**

Why? Because the financial need for life insurance is typically temporary:

- Your children will grow up and become financially independent
- Your mortgage will be paid off
- You'll accumulate savings and investments that could support survivors
- The need for life insurance decreases as your net worth grows

**Cost:** A healthy 30-year-old can get $500,000 of 20-year term coverage for $20-30/month. A healthy 35-year-old, $30-40/month. Premiums increase with age and any health issues.

Buy as much coverage as your dependents would need to replace your income for however many years are relevant. A common rule of thumb: 10-12 times your annual income. Customize based on your specific situation — existing savings, debts, how many years until your youngest is independent.

**When to buy:** As early as possible and when you first have dependents. Premiums are lowest when you're young and healthy. A health issue that develops later can make coverage significantly more expensive or unavailable.

## Whole Life Insurance

Whole life insurance combines a death benefit with a savings component (called "cash value"). You pay higher premiums; part goes to coverage, part accumulates as a tax-advantaged savings/investment vehicle.

The insurance industry markets whole life aggressively because it generates higher commissions than term life. The pitch usually involves the tax advantages of the cash value component.

**The honest assessment:**

Whole life premiums for equivalent death benefit coverage are 5-15 times higher than term life. The cash value grows slowly and often underperforms what you'd earn investing the premium difference in low-cost index funds.

The comparison to evaluate: What if you bought term life (much cheaper) and invested the premium difference? For most people, this produces significantly more wealth than whole life, while providing the same death benefit protection.

**When whole life can make sense:**
- For very high net-worth individuals who have maximized all other tax-advantaged accounts and need additional estate planning tools
- For people with certain specific estate planning needs (like funding an irrevocable life insurance trust)
- For people who are definitively uninsurable by term — some guaranteed-issue whole life policies exist for people who can't qualify for term

For most working people, term life + invest the difference is superior.

---

## Other Types

**Universal life:** Another permanent life product with more flexible premiums. Complex. Variable performance. Generally, the same critique applies as whole life for most people.

**Group life insurance:** Often provided by employers, usually 1-2x salary. Useful benefit but rarely sufficient as your only coverage, and it ends when you leave the job.

---

## Buying Life Insurance

Shop for term life through comparison sites or by working directly with insurers. Many reputable companies now offer fully online applications with no medical exam required up to certain coverage amounts (typically $500,000-1,000,000). Above those thresholds or at older ages, a paramedical exam is usually required.

Price quotes are driven by: age, sex, health status, smoking status, coverage amount, and term length. Getting quotes from multiple insurers is worth it — rates can vary meaningfully for the same profile.
