## Why You Need Insurance Before You Think You Do

There's a common pattern: people delay getting insurance because they're young, healthy, don't own much, or think bad things won't happen to them. Then something happens, and the financial consequences last for years.

The time to have insurance is before you need it. Once you need it, it's too late.

---

## The Illusion of Invulnerability

Young people are statistically less likely to experience serious illness, disability, or death than older people. That's true. What's also true:

- 1 in 4 Americans will become disabled for at least a year before retirement age
- Medical debt is the leading cause of bankruptcy in the United States
- Car accidents happen to people of every age, including people who are safe drivers (because other drivers aren't)
- House fires, theft, and liability lawsuits don't check IDs

The question isn't whether something will go wrong — it's whether you'll have protection in place when it does.

---

## The Cost of Not Having It

**No health insurance:** A three-day hospital stay averages $30,000. An ER visit alone can run $3,000-10,000. A serious illness can generate $100,000+ in medical bills quickly. Without insurance, these bills are yours. Medical debt follows people for years and doesn't go away easily.

**No auto insurance:** If you cause an accident and injure someone, you can be personally liable for their medical bills, lost wages, and legal damages — potentially hundreds of thousands of dollars or more. Your assets and future earnings are at risk. Most states require liability insurance, but required minimums are often insufficient.

**No renters insurance:** Your landlord's insurance covers the building. It does not cover your belongings. A fire, theft, or water damage can destroy everything you own — laptop, furniture, clothes, electronics. Renters insurance typically costs $15-30/month and covers thousands of dollars of property.

**No disability insurance:** If you can't work, you earn nothing. Social Security disability exists but covers less than most people realize, the approval process takes over a year on average, and you must be severely disabled to qualify. Short-term disability, long-term disability, or an adequate emergency fund are essential bridges.

**No life insurance with dependents:** If people depend on your income and you die without life insurance, they face the financial consequences of your absence on top of the grief. Term life insurance is inexpensive when you're young — the window to get it affordably closes as health issues emerge.

## Insurance as Financial Infrastructure

Think of insurance the same way you think of your emergency fund: it's not an investment that generates returns. It's protection against catastrophe.

The goal isn't to "win" with insurance — to collect more than you pay in. The goal is to cap your exposure to events that would otherwise be financially ruinous.

A $20/month renters insurance policy isn't worth celebrating unless your apartment burns down. If your apartment burns down, it's irreplaceable.

---

## The Right Order of Operations

Coverage priority, roughly in order of urgency:

1. **Auto liability** (required by law in most states — non-negotiable)
2. **Health insurance** (catastrophic coverage at minimum; ACA marketplace if no employer plan)
3. **Renters or homeowners insurance** (often required by landlord/mortgage lender)
4. **Life insurance** (critical if you have dependents; unnecessary if no one depends on your income)
5. **Disability insurance** (underrated; your ability to earn income is your most valuable asset)

Get these in place before worrying about the optimization of your investment portfolio. A single uninsured medical event can wipe out years of savings.

---

## The Getting-Started Problem

The most common real barrier to insurance isn't cost — it's complexity and friction. Comparing health plans feels overwhelming. Finding renters insurance requires some research. Life insurance requires an application.

But the barrier is smaller than it seems. Renters insurance can be purchased online in 10 minutes for $20/month. Term life insurance can be quoted and applied for online. Health insurance marketplace plans are searchable at healthcare.gov.

The cost of the research is hours. The cost of going without is potentially your financial life. The math is clear.
