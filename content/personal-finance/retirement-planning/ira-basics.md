## Traditional vs Roth IRA

An IRA (Individual Retirement Account) is a retirement savings account you control — not tied to any employer. It supplements a 401(k) for people who have access to one, and is often the primary retirement account for self-employed people and those without workplace plans.

---

## The 2024 Contribution Limits

- **Annual limit:** $7,000 per person ($8,000 if age 50 or older)
- You must have earned income at least equal to your contribution amount
- The limit is total across all IRAs — you can't put $7,000 in a Roth IRA and $7,000 in a Traditional IRA in the same year

---

## Traditional IRA

**How it works:**
- Contributions may be tax-deductible (see below)
- Money grows tax-deferred — you don't pay taxes on growth until withdrawal
- Withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income
- Required minimum distributions (RMDs) start at age 73

**Deductibility depends on your situation:**

If neither you nor your spouse has a workplace retirement plan: contributions are fully deductible regardless of income.

If you (or your spouse) have a workplace plan, deductibility phases out at higher incomes:
- Single: phases out $77,000-$87,000 AGI (2024)
- Married filing jointly: phases out $123,000-$143,000 AGI (2024)

If you're above these limits and have a workplace plan, you can still contribute to a Traditional IRA — you just don't get the deduction. This is a "non-deductible contribution" and creates a tax basis that you track to avoid double taxation on withdrawal.

---

## Roth IRA

**How it works:**
- Contributions are made with after-tax money (no deduction now)
- Money grows completely tax-free
- Qualified withdrawals in retirement are 100% tax-free — including all earnings
- No required minimum distributions during your lifetime
- Contributions (not earnings) can be withdrawn at any time without penalty or tax

**Income limits for Roth IRA contributions:**
- Single: contribution phases out at $146,000-$161,000 AGI (2024)
- Married filing jointly: phases out at $230,000-$240,000 AGI
- Above these limits, you cannot contribute directly to a Roth IRA

---

## Traditional vs Roth: How to Choose

**Roth is better if:**
- Your current tax rate is lower than your expected retirement rate
- You're early career or in a temporary lower-income period
- You value flexibility (contribution access, no RMDs)
- You're uncertain about future tax rates (locking in today's rates eliminates future risk)

**Traditional is better if:**
- Your current tax rate is higher than your expected retirement rate (typically peak earning years)
- You want to reduce taxable income now
- You're in the phase-out range for Roth and have a workplace plan

**When unclear:** Most financial advisors slightly favor Roth for younger savers because of the flexibility advantage (access to contributions, no RMDs) and because predicting future tax rates decades out is genuinely uncertain. But this isn't a rule.

**The hedge:** If you have a traditional 401(k) at work, adding a Roth IRA gives you tax diversification — some money taxed on the way in, some on the way out.

---

## The Backdoor Roth IRA

If your income is above the Roth IRA limit, there's a legal workaround:

1. Make a non-deductible contribution to a Traditional IRA
2. Convert that Traditional IRA balance to a Roth IRA

You pay no income tax on the conversion (assuming no pre-tax IRA money complicates the calculation). The result: Roth IRA money regardless of income.

**The pro-rata rule:** If you have other pre-tax Traditional IRA money, the conversion is taxed proportionally — you can't just convert the after-tax contribution without accounting for all your IRA balances. This complication is why the backdoor Roth works cleanly for some people and is complicated for others with existing IRA balances.

If you're in this situation, consult a tax professional before executing.

## Where to Open an IRA

Any major brokerage: Fidelity, Vanguard, Charles Schwab. All offer commission-free trading and access to low-cost index funds.

Choosing between them is minor — pick the one where you already have accounts for simplicity, or Fidelity for its zero-expense-ratio funds.

Process: apply online, link a bank account, fund it, and choose your investments. Takes under an hour for most people.
