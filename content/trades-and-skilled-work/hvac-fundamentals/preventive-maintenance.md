## Preventive Maintenance: What to Do and When

HVAC equipment lasts 15-25 years with proper maintenance and fails prematurely without it. Most of the maintenance tasks are simple — the ones that keep equipment running efficiently and prevent expensive breakdowns are the same ones homeowners can do themselves.

---

## Monthly Tasks

### Change or Check the Air Filter

A clogged air filter is the single most common cause of HVAC problems. Restricted airflow:
- Reduces system efficiency (makes the blower work harder)
- Can freeze the evaporator coil (cooling mode)
- Can overheat the heat exchanger (heating mode)
- Reduces air quality (the dirty filter is now pushing dirt past its surface)

**Filter change frequency:**
- 1" fiberglass filters: monthly
- 1-2" pleated filters: every 30-60 days
- 4-5" media filters: every 6-12 months
- Reusable filters: clean monthly

Check the filter by holding it up to light — if you can't see light through it, change it.

Keep a spare filter on hand. Running for a week with an overdue filter costs more in efficiency loss than the filter costs.

## Seasonal Tasks

### Before Cooling Season (Spring)

**Outdoor unit (condenser):**
- Clear debris from around and on top of the unit (leaves, grass clippings, sticks)
- Clean the condenser fins with a garden hose on a gentle spray setting — spray from inside out to push debris outward, not further in
- Straighten bent fins with a fin comb (minor bending affects airflow)
- Ensure at least 2 feet of clearance around the unit
- Check that the unit is level (a slightly unlevel unit causes compressor oil distribution issues over time)

**Indoor system:**
- Inspect the evaporator coil for dust or mold buildup (visible through the access panel near the air handler)
- Check the condensate drain line for algae and debris (see below)
- Test the cooling system a few weeks before hot weather — don't wait for the first hot day to discover a problem

**Refrigerant lines:**
- Check that the insulation on the suction line (large line) is intact — damaged insulation causes condensation and reduces efficiency

### Before Heating Season (Fall)

**Gas furnace:**
- Replace the air filter
- Test the furnace: turn on the heat and let it run for a cycle — ensure heat comes out, no burning smells (a brief dust smell is normal the first time), no unusual noises
- Check all supply registers and return grilles — ensure none are blocked by furniture
- Test carbon monoxide detectors and replace batteries

**Heat pump:**
- Test heating mode operation
- Verify the defrost cycle works (heat pumps in cold weather periodically run a defrost cycle — outdoor unit may frost up briefly; this is normal as long as it clears within a few minutes)

## Annual Tasks (Professional Service)

While many maintenance tasks are DIY, a professional tune-up once a year (once for heating season, once for cooling season for busy systems) catches problems early.

### What a Professional Cooling Tune-Up Includes

- Check refrigerant charge (requires gauges and EPA certification)
- Clean evaporator and condenser coils (chemical cleaning if needed)
- Check capacitors and contactors (common failure points)
- Measure amperage draw on blower and compressor motors
- Check electrical connections for corrosion or looseness
- Verify thermostat calibration
- Inspect ductwork for obvious leaks (if accessible)
- Check condensate drain and clean the trap

### What a Professional Heating Tune-Up Includes (Gas Furnace)

- Inspect the heat exchanger for cracks (CO hazard — this one is critical)
- Clean the burner assembly
- Check igniter condition
- Measure gas pressure and manifold pressure
- Check flue and draft — ensure proper venting
- Inspect blower motor and belt (if belt-drive type)
- Clean flame sensor (dirty flame sensors cause intermittent operation)
- Verify safety controls function

## Condensate Drain Maintenance

In cooling mode, the evaporator coil pulls moisture out of the air. This condensate collects in a drain pan and drains through a condensate drain line to a floor drain, utility sink, or exterior.

Algae and slime grow in condensate drain lines and can cause clogs. A clogged condensate drain overflows the pan, potentially causing:
- Water damage to the air handler and surrounding structure
- Mold growth
- System shutoff (if float switches are installed)

**Monthly in cooling season: pour 1/4 cup of white vinegar into the condensate drain access port** (usually a T-shaped cap in the drain line near the air handler). The vinegar prevents algae growth.

If you have a clog: use a wet/dry vacuum to pull the blockage out from the drain's exit point, or blow compressed air through the line.

## Checking and Cleaning Coils

**Evaporator coil (indoor):** Usually only accessible by removing the air handler access panel. If visibly dirty, it needs professional chemical cleaning. A dirty evaporator coil severely reduces efficiency and cooling capacity.

**Condenser coil (outdoor):** More accessible. Clean annually with a garden hose. For heavily fouled coils, coil cleaner (foam spray) can be applied and rinsed.

**Never use a pressure washer on coil fins.** The fins are very thin aluminum and bend easily — a pressure washer destroys them.

## Common Maintenance Mistakes

**Blocking return air:** Furniture covering return grilles starves the system of airflow. Every return grille needs to be clear.

**Closing supply registers in unused rooms:** This increases duct pressure, causing leaks and reducing system efficiency. Designed for full-house airflow. Close registers can actually increase operating costs.

**Landscaping too close to the outdoor unit:** Shrubs, fences, and structures that reduce airflow over the condenser coil make the system work harder and run hotter. 2 feet minimum clearance on all sides.

**Ignoring the first sign of a problem:** A system that cycles short, makes new noises, or can't maintain temperature is telling you something is wrong. Early diagnosis is less expensive than emergency repair.
