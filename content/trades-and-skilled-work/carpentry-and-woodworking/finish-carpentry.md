## Finish Carpentry: Trim, Doors, and Cabinets

Finish carpentry is where the rough skeleton of the house is hidden and the aesthetic value is added. This work requires a higher level of precision, better tools, and a lot of patience.

### Common Finish Elements
- **Baseboards:** The trim that runs along the bottom of the wall. It protects the wall from vacuum cleaners and kicks, and covers the gap between the drywall and the floor.
- **Casing:** The trim around doors and windows. This hides the rough opening and provides a clean transition to the wall.
- **Crown Molding:** The decorative trim where the wall meets the ceiling. This is the hardest trim to install because it requires a "compound miter" (cutting both the angle and the bevel).
- **Door Jambs and Stops:** The frame that holds the door in place. Ensuring these are perfectly plumb is the only way to get a door that doesn't swing open or shut on its own.

### Precision Techniques
1. **Coping vs. Mitering:** While mitering (cutting 45 degrees) is common, "coping" is the professional way to handle inside corners. You cut the profile of one piece of trim and fit it against the flat face of the other. This prevents gaps when the walls aren't perfectly square.
2. **Nail-Hole Filling:** Use a finish nailer with a small gauge (18g or 23g). Once the trim is up, fill the holes with wood filler or caulk, sand it flush, and paint. The goal is for the fasteners to be completely invisible.
3. **Scribing:** When a wall is wavy but your cabinet is straight, you "scribe" the cabinet to the wall. You use a compass or a scribe tool to trace the wall's contour onto the wood, then shave the wood down to match the wall perfectly.

### The Finishing Touch
Finish work is won or lost in the gaps. Use a high-quality caulk for the seams between the trim and the wall. A clean bead of caulk hides a multitude of sins and makes the final paint job look seamless.
