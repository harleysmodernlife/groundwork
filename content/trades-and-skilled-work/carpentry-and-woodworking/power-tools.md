## Power Tools: Safe Use and Applications

Power tools multiply your efficiency, but they also multiply the risk. The first rule of power tools is respect: never treat a tool as a toy, and never bypass a safety guard.

### The Core Power Tools
- **Circular Saw:** The workhorse of the site. Used for breaking down sheets of plywood and cutting long boards. Always ensure the blade is sharp and the guard snaps back instantly.
- **Miter Saw (Chop Saw):** Designed for precise cross-cuts and angled cuts. This is where you do your trim work and frame cuts. Never put your hand within six inches of the blade's path.
- **Drill/Driver:** Used for boring holes and driving screws. A cordless impact driver is better for long screws and heavy fasteners, while a standard drill is better for precision holes.
- **Table Saw:** The most powerful and most dangerous tool in the shop. Used for ripping long boards to width. Always use a push stick to move the wood past the blade—never use your fingers.
- **Jigsaw:** Used for cutting curves and irregular shapes. It's slower than a circular saw but allows for intricate patterns.
- **Router:** A high-speed tool used for hollowing out areas or creating decorative edges. It requires a steady hand and a clear path.

### Safety Protocol
1. **Eye Protection:** Safety glasses are non-negotiable. A flying wood chip can end your career in a second.
2. **Hearing Protection:** Power tools are loud. Long-term exposure causes permanent damage. Use earplugs or muffs.
3. **Dress for the Job:** No loose clothing, no dangling jewelry, and tie back long hair. Anything that can get caught in a spinning blade is a liability.
4. **The "No-Force" Rule:** If you have to push a tool hard to make it cut, something is wrong. Either the blade is dull, the material is too hard, or you're using the wrong tool. Let the tool do the work.

### Power Management
Keep your cords organized to avoid tripping. If using cordless tools, rotate your batteries and keep them charged. A dead battery in the middle of a critical cut is a recipe for a mistake.
