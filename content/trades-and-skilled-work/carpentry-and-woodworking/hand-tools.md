## Hand Tools Every Carpenter Uses

Power tools are for speed, but hand tools are for precision and control. You cannot be a competent carpenter if you rely solely on a battery pack.

### The Essential Kit
- **Speed Square (Rafter Square):** The most used tool in the belt. It's used for marking 90-degree and 45-degree angles quickly and serves as a fence for your circular saw.
- **Chisels:** Used for carving out recesses (like for door hinges) and cleaning up joints. Keep them sharp; a dull chisel is dangerous because it requires more force and is more likely to slip.
- **Hammers:** You need a framing hammer (heavier, longer neck for driving big nails) and a finish hammer (smaller, used for trim work to avoid marking the wood).
- **Levels:** A 4-foot level for checking walls and a torpedo level for small spaces. If it's not level or plumb, it's wrong.
- **Handsaws:** A pull-saw (Japanese style) for clean cuts and a standard cross-cut saw for rougher work.
- **Clamps:** You can never have enough clamps. They act as your "extra set of hands" to hold pieces together while glue dries or before you drive a screw.

### Tool Maintenance
- **Keep it Clean:** Wipe down your tools after use. Rust on a square or a chisel ruins the accuracy of the tool.
- **Sharpen Regularly:** A sharp tool is a safe tool. Learn to use a whetstone or a honing guide for your chisels and plane blades.
- **Store Properly:** Don't throw your tools in a heap. Use a tool belt for the site and a dedicated chest for the shop.
