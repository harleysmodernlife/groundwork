## Common Pricing Mistakes

Pricing is the most powerful lever in your business. A 10% increase in price can often result in a 50% increase in net profit. Yet, most small business owners treat pricing as a guessing game or a social negotiation. They price based on fear—fear of losing the customer, fear of looking greedy, or fear of being "too expensive."

---

## 1. The "Competitor Match" Trap
Many owners look at what the guy down the street is charging and match it. This is a race to the bottom.
- **The Failure:** You don't know the other guy's costs. He might have a lower rent, a cheaper truck, or a lower standard of living. If you match his price but have higher costs, you are subsidizing your customers' lives with your own profit.
- **The Fix:** Price based on your own costs and the value you provide. If you are better than the competitor, you should be more expensive. If you are cheaper, you are signaling that your quality is lower.

## 2. The "Hourly Rate" Ceiling
Charging by the hour creates a perverse incentive: the more efficient you become, the less money you make.
- **The Failure:** You spend years mastering your craft. You can now do a job in 2 hours that used to take 10. If you charge hourly, you just gave yourself a 80% pay cut for becoming an expert.
- **The Fix:** Move to flat-fee or value-based pricing. Charge for the result, not the time. This rewards efficiency and expertise.

## 3. Fear of the "No"
Many owners underprice because they are afraid the customer will say no. 
- **The Failure:** You price so low that you get every single job. You are now overworked, stressed, and barely profitable. You have a volume problem, not a growth problem.
- **The Fix:** Raise your prices until you start losing about 20% of your leads. This is the "Sweet Spot." It means you are charging a fair market price and have room to be selective about which jobs you take.

## 4. The "Discount" Habit
Offering discounts to "close the deal" is a fast way to destroy your brand value.
- **The Failure:** Once you discount for one customer, that customer will never pay full price again. Worse, they will tell their friends that you "usually give a discount," and every new lead will demand one.
- **The Fix:** Never discount the price; instead, **change the scope**. If a customer says the price is too high, don't lower the price—remove a feature or a service. "I can do it for $800 instead of $1,000, but we'll have to remove the extended warranty and the final cleanup."

---

## The Pricing Audit

Every six months, run this audit on your pricing:

1. **The Profit Check:** Look at your last 10 jobs. Which one had the highest net profit margin? Why? Was it the type of work, the customer, or the pricing model?
2. **The "No" Check:** When was the last time a customer told you that you were too expensive? If the answer is "never," your prices are too low.
3. **The Efficiency Check:** Are you working more hours but making the same amount of money? If yes, your hourly rate has effectively dropped, and it's time to raise your flat fees.

---

## Summary: The Pricing Mindset

Price is a signal of quality. When you price too low, you don't attract "bargain hunters"—you attract "nightmare customers." Nightmare customers are the ones who demand the most, complain the loudest, and pay the least. High prices attract professional customers who value their time and respect your expertise.
