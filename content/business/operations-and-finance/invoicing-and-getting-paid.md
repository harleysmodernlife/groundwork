## Invoicing and Getting Paid

Doing the work is only half the job. The other half is getting the money into your bank account. Many skilled tradespeople are great at the technical work but terrible at the administrative side of getting paid. This leads to "leaking revenue," where you've earned the money on paper, but you never actually collect it.

---

## The Anatomy of a Professional Invoice

An invoice is a legal document. If it is vague or messy, it gives the customer a psychological excuse to delay payment. A professional invoice should include:

- **Unique Invoice Number:** For tracking and auditing.
- **Date of Issue and Due Date:** "Due on Receipt" or "Due within 7 Days." Never leave the due date blank.
- **Itemized List of Services:** Instead of "Repair Work - $500," use "Replacement of Brake Pads and Rotors (Front) - $350; Labor (3 hours) - $150."
- **Payment Methods:** Clear instructions on how to pay (e.g., "Pay via Square link below," "Zelle to [Email]," or "Check payable to [Company Name]").
- **Late Fee Warning:** A brief note stating that late payments will incur a fee (e.g., "Payments not received within 15 days are subject to a 5% late fee").

---

## The Payment Workflow

To eliminate payment delays, you must build the payment process into the project workflow, not treat it as an afterthought.

### 1. The Deposit (The Commitment)
Never start work without a deposit. A deposit serves two purposes: it covers your initial COGS and it ensures the customer is financially committed to the project.
- **Standard:** 25% to 50% upfront for materials and scheduling.

### 2. Progress Payments (The Bridge)
For projects that take longer than a week, use milestone payments. This prevents you from becoming the customer's bank.
- **Example:** 33% at start, 33% at mid-point (inspection), 33% upon completion.

### 3. The Final Invoice (The Close)
Send the final invoice the moment the work is done. Do not wait until the end of the week. The customer's satisfaction is highest the moment the problem is solved—that is when they are most likely to pay immediately.

## Handling Non-Payment

When a customer doesn't pay, the goal is to get the money while maintaining the relationship (if you want to keep them) or severing the tie cleanly (if you don't).

| Stage | Action | Tone |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Day 1-3 Overdue** | Friendly Reminder: "Just checking to see if the invoice got lost in your inbox." | Helpful, assuming a mistake. |
| **Day 7 Overdue** | Firm Reminder: "This invoice is now one week overdue. Please let us know when to expect payment." | Professional, direct. |
| **Day 14 Overdue** | The Phone Call: Call the customer. It is much harder to ignore a human voice than an email. | Assertive, seeking a date. |
| **Day 30 Overdue** | The Demand Letter: A formal letter stating that if payment isn't received, the account will be sent to collections or small claims court. | Legal, final. |

## Common Mistakes

- **The "I'll just bill them later" Trap:** Forgetting to track small parts or extra hours and then feeling "guilty" about charging for them later. Track everything in real-time.
- **Accepting Vague Payment Promises:** "I'll pay you as soon as I get my check on Friday." This is not a payment; it's a hope. Ask for a specific time and method.
- **Fear of Asking for Money:** Remember that you have provided a value. Asking for payment is not "begging"; it is the completion of a professional contract.
