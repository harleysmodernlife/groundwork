## What to Track: A Manual for Business Intelligence and Operational Metrics

Many business owners confuse "checking the bank balance" with "tracking the business." Your bank balance is a lagging indicator—it tells you what happened in the past. To run a business instead of just having a job, you must track leading indicators: the specific metrics that predict where the business is going. Tracking the wrong numbers creates a false sense of security; tracking the right numbers provides the data needed to make objective, unemotional decisions.

---

## 1. Vanity Metrics vs. Actionable Metrics

In business, there is a critical distinction between numbers that make you *feel* good and numbers that tell you the *truth*.

### Vanity Metrics (The Noise)
Vanity metrics are numbers that look impressive on a slide but don't correlate to profit. 
- **Gross Revenue:** The total amount of money that passed through your hands. A business can do $1M in revenue and still go bankrupt if its expenses are $1.1M.
- **Total Leads:** The number of people who emailed you. If you have 1,000 leads but none of them are your Ideal Customer Profile (ICP), the number is meaningless.
- **Social Media Followers:** Likes and follows do not equal deposits. They are a proxy for attention, not a proxy for revenue.

### Actionable Metrics (The Signal)
Actionable metrics are numbers that, when changed, directly impact the bottom line.
- **Net Profit Margin:** The percentage of every dollar that you actually keep after all expenses. If your margin is 10%, you have very little room for error. If it's 40%, you have a stable, scalable business.
- **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):** Exactly how much it costs in marketing and time to get one new paying customer.
- **Conversion Rate:** The percentage of leads that turn into paying customers.

---

## 2. The Growth Equation: CAC and LTV

To know if your business is scalable, you must understand the relationship between what it costs to get a customer and what that customer is worth over time.

### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
**Formula:** `Total Marketing & Sales Spend / Number of New Customers Acquired`.
- **Example:** If you spend $500 on Facebook ads and $500 of your time (at your hourly rate) to get 5 new customers, your CAC is $200 per customer.

### Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)
**Formula:** `Average Order Value * Average Number of Repeat Purchases per Year * Average Customer Lifespan (Years)`.
- **Example:** If a customer spends $500 per visit, visits twice a year, and stays with you for 3 years, their LTV is $3,000.

**The LTV:CAC Ratio:**
- **Ratio < 1:1:** You are paying more to get the customer than they are worth. You are effectively paying for the privilege of doing the work. This is a death spiral.
- **Ratio 3:1:** This is the "Gold Standard" for a healthy business. You are making three times what it costs to acquire the customer.
- **Ratio > 5:1:** You are likely under-investing in growth. You could spend more on marketing to capture the market faster without risking the business.

## 3. Operational Efficiency: The Utilization Rate

For service-based businesses, your most limited resource is time. The **Utilization Rate** tells you how much of your paid time is actually producing revenue.

**Formula:** `(Total Billable Hours / Total Hours Worked) * 100`.

**The Utilization Spectrum:**
- **Below 50%:** You have a massive efficiency problem or a sales problem. You are spending too much time on admin, quoting, or waiting for work.
- **70% - 85%:** The "Sweet Spot." You are producing high revenue but still have 15-30% of your time for administrative tasks, training, and strategic planning.
- **Above 90%:** You are at a breaking point. You have no room for errors, no time for growth, and you are on the verge of burnout. This is the signal that you must either raise your prices or hire your first employee.

## 4. Cash Flow Dynamics: The Cash Conversion Cycle

Profit is an accounting concept; Cash is a physical reality. You can be profitable on paper and still go bankrupt because your cash is trapped.

**The Cash Gap:** This is the time between when you pay for your inputs (parts, labor, rent) and when the customer pays you for the output.

**How to Shorten the Cycle:**
1. **Request Deposits:** Move the payment date forward. A 50% deposit upon booking eliminates the risk of paying for materials out of your own pocket.
2. **Tighten Payment Terms:** Move from "Net 30" (payment due in 30 days) to "Due on Receipt."
3. **Automate Collections:** Use software that sends automatic reminders 3 days before, on the day of, and 3 days after a payment is due.

---

## 5. The Lead-to-Cash Funnel

To fix a revenue problem, you must find the "leak" in your funnel. Track these four stages as percentages:

1. **Lead → Quote:** What % of people who contact you actually get a price? (If this is low, your intake process is too slow).
2. **Quote → Closed Job:** What % of quotes are accepted? (If this is low, your pricing is off or your value proposition is weak).
3. **Closed Job → Completed Work:** What % of jobs are finished on time? (If this is low, you have an operational/labor problem).
4. **Completed Work → Paid Invoice:** What % of jobs are paid within terms? (If this is low, you have a collections problem).

## 6. The Business Owner's Reporting Cadence

Data is useless if it isn't reviewed. Establish a non-negotiable schedule for checking your metrics.

### The Daily Pulse (5 Minutes)
- **Cash Position:** Do I have enough in the bank for tomorrow's obligations?
- **Deliverables:** What is the #1 priority that must be completed today to keep the cash flowing?

### The Weekly Review (30 Minutes)
- **Revenue vs. Target:** Did we hit the weekly goal? If not, why?
- **Lead Volume:** Did we get enough new leads to fill next week's schedule?
- **Utilization Rate:** How many hours were billable vs. wasted?

### The Monthly Audit (2 Hours)
- **Full P&L Review:** What was the Net Profit Margin? Which expense category grew unexpectedly?
- **LTV/CAC Check:** Are we still acquiring customers profitably?
- **The "Red Flag" Scan:** Are we seeing rising revenue but falling profit? (Scaling mistakes).

### The Quarterly Strategic Pivot (Half Day)
- **Tax Projection:** Based on current profit, what is our estimated tax liability for the year?
- **Price Review:** Based on our utilization rate, is it time to raise prices?
- **Goal Setting:** What is the one operational bottleneck we must solve in the next 90 days?

---

## Summary: The Data-Driven Mindset

Running a business by "feel" is a gamble. Running a business by data is a science. When you track your CAC, LTV, and Utilization Rate, you stop guessing and start calculating. You no longer "hope" the business is doing well; you *know* exactly where the leaks are and exactly which lever to pull to fix them.
