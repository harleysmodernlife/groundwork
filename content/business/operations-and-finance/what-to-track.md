## What to Track and Why

Many business owners confuse "checking the bank balance" with "tracking the business." Your bank balance tells you if you have money right now; it does not tell you if your business is actually profitable or if you are slowly bleeding out. To run a business instead of just having a job, you must track four specific categories of data.

---

## 1. Revenue (The Top Line)
Revenue is the total amount of money coming into the business from sales. It is the most deceptive number because it doesn't account for any costs.
- **Gross Revenue:** The total amount billed. 
- **Net Revenue:** Total billed minus discounts, returns, or refunds.
- **Why it matters:** Tracking revenue tells you if there is a market for your product. If revenue is flat or falling, you have a sales or pricing problem, not an operations problem.

## 2. Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)
COGS are the "direct costs" required to produce your product or deliver your service. If you don't do a job, you don't incur these costs.
- **Examples:** Materials, parts, subcontractor labor, shipping costs for the product.
- **The Calculation:** Revenue - COGS = **Gross Profit**.
- **Why it matters:** If your Gross Profit is too low, you are essentially working for free. No amount of "growing the business" will fix a product that costs more to make than you sell it for.

## 3. Operating Expenses (OpEx / Overhead)
These are the "fixed costs" you pay regardless of whether you have one customer or a thousand. These are the costs of keeping the lights on.
- **Examples:** Rent, software subscriptions, insurance, administrative salaries, utilities, marketing.
- **The Calculation:** Gross Profit - OpEx = **Net Profit (The Bottom Line)**.
- **Why it matters:** This is where most businesses fail. They have a great product (high Gross Profit) but an bloated overhead (high OpEx) that eats every cent of profit.

## 4. Accounts Receivable and Payable (The Gap)
This is the money that is "owed" to you or "owed" by you. This is where the danger of cash flow lives.
- **Accounts Receivable (AR):** Money customers owe you for work already completed.
- **Accounts Payable (AP):** Money you owe to suppliers or utilities that hasn't been paid yet.
- **Why it matters:** You can be "profitable" on paper (high Net Profit) but go bankrupt because your AR is too high—meaning your profit is trapped in your customers' bank accounts instead of yours.

---

## Summary Tracking Table

| Metric | What it tells you |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Revenue** | Is there demand for what I'm selling? |
| **Gross Profit** | Is my pricing high enough to cover my direct costs? |
| **Net Profit** | Is the business actually making money after all bills are paid? |
| **AR/AP** | Do I have enough actual cash to survive the next 30 days? |

---

## The "Red Flag" Indicators

If you see these patterns in your tracking, act immediately:

- **Rising Revenue + Falling Net Profit:** You are growing, but you are becoming less efficient. You are likely scaling your mistakes.
- **High Net Profit + Low Bank Balance:** You have a collections problem. Your customers aren't paying you fast enough.
- **Flat Revenue + Rising OpEx:** You are in a "death spiral." Your overhead is growing while your income is stagnant. Cut costs immediately.
