## Where Your Customers Actually Are

Most business owners spend their marketing budget on "awareness"—trying to get their name in front of as many people as possible. This is a waste of resources. You don't need awareness; you need **intent**. You want to find people who are already experiencing the pain your business solves.

---

## Intent-Based Marketing

There are two types of customers: those who know they have a problem (High Intent) and those who don't know they have a problem yet (Low Intent). Your goal is to ignore the low-intent crowd and dominate the high-intent channels.

### High-Intent Channels (The "I Need This Now" Crowd)
These are the places people go when they are actively searching for a solution.
- **Google Search / Google Maps:** The gold standard. When someone types "emergency plumber near me," they have a leak in their house *right now*. This is the highest-converting lead possible.
- **Nextdoor:** People go here specifically to find trusted local providers. A recommendation on Nextdoor is worth ten Facebook ads because it comes with built-in community trust.
- **Local Facebook Groups:** The "Community" or "Word of Mouth" groups. People post their problems and ask for help. This is a real-time feed of high-intent leads.
- **Industry-Specific Forums:** (e.g., a local car enthusiast group). If you specialize in a certain type of vehicle or equipment, these forums are where the high-value, knowledgeable customers hang out.

### Low-Intent Channels (The "I Might Need This Someday" Crowd)
These are places where people go for entertainment or social connection. You can find customers here, but the sales cycle is much longer.
- **Instagram/TikTok:** Great for building a brand and showing off work, but rarely where someone goes to find a repairman in an emergency.
- **General Facebook Feed:** Most people are looking at family photos, not searching for a contractor. Ads here are "disruptive"—you are interrupting their scrolling to tell them they might need your service.

## Mapping Your ICP to a Location

To find your customers, you must match their behavior to the channel. 

| If your ICP is... | They are likely at... | Your Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Emergency Buyer** | Google Maps / Search | Optimize your Google Business Profile and get 5-star reviews. |
| **The Community-Driven Buyer** | Nextdoor / FB Groups | Be the helpful expert. Answer questions before asking for the job. |
| **The High-End Enthusiast** | Specialized Forums / Clubs | Share deep technical knowledge. Prove you are a specialist, not a generalist. |
| **The Visual/Aesthetic Buyer** | Instagram / Pinterest | Post high-quality "After" photos and focus on the end result. |

## The "Physical" Channel (The Real World)

Digital marketing is crowded. In many service industries, the most effective channels are physical and analog.

### 1. The Strategic Partner (The Referral Loop)
Find a business that serves the same ICP but provides a different service. 
- **Example:** A house cleaner knows when a client's faucet is leaking. A window tinter knows when a client's AC is failing.
- **The Play:** Build a relationship with these providers. Give them a reason to refer you (a referral fee or a reciprocal referral). This is the most efficient way to get pre-vetted, high-trust leads.

### 2. The "Sighting" Strategy
Keep your eyes open while you are in the field. 
- **Example:** You're doing a job and notice the neighbor has a rusted-out fence or a car on blocks. 
- **The Play:** Don't be a pest, but leave a professional card or a simple note: "Hey, I'm doing work for your neighbor and noticed your [Problem]. I specialize in fixing that. If you ever want a quote, here's my number."

### 3. High-Traffic Hubs
Identify where your ICP physically congregates. 
- **Example:** If you fix specialty gym equipment, spend time at local CrossFit boxes. If you do high-end detailing, find the local "Cars and Coffee" meetups.

## Common Mistakes

### 1. The "Shotgun" Approach
Trying to be active on every platform simultaneously. This leads to burnout and mediocre presence. Pick the top two channels where your ICP has the highest intent and dominate them before adding a third.

### 2. Ignoring the "Last Mile"
Spending thousands on ads to get people to your website, but then taking three days to reply to a lead's text. In high-intent marketing, speed is the primary competitive advantage. The first person to answer the phone usually gets the job.

### 3. Confusing Reach with Revenue
Getting 10,000 views on a TikTok video is a vanity metric if none of those people live in your service area or fit your ICP. 10 views from the right people in your zip code are worth more than a million views from across the world.
