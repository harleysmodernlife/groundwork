## Quarterly Estimated Taxes

The US tax system is "pay-as-you-go." When you are an employee, your employer withholds taxes from every paycheck. When you are self-employed, no one is withholding for you. If you wait until April 15th to pay your entire year's tax bill, the IRS will hit you with **underpayment penalties** and interest.

To avoid this, the IRS requires business owners to make four estimated tax payments throughout the year.

---

## The Quarterly Schedule

Despite being called "quarterly," the deadlines are not perfectly every three months. Mark these dates in your calendar:

- **Q1 (Jan 1 - Mar 31):** Due April 15
- **Q2 (Apr 1 - May 31):** Due June 15
- **Q3 (Jun 1 - Aug 31):** Due Sept 15
- **Q4 (Sept 1 - Dec 31):** Due Jan 15 (of the following year)

---

## How to Calculate Your Payment

You don't need a perfect calculation, but you need to be "close enough" to avoid penalties. There are two primary methods:

### 1. The Safe Harbor Method (The Easiest Path)
If you pay 100% of the tax you owed *last year* (or 110% if your income is high), the IRS will not penalize you for underpayment this year, even if you make ten times more money. 
- **The Play:** Take your total tax bill from last year's return, divide it by 4, and pay that amount every quarter. This is the safest way to avoid penalties.

### 2. The Current Income Method (The Accurate Path)
If you have no prior year's data or your income has dropped significantly, you estimate your current year's profit.
- **The Play:** Calculate your profit for the current quarter. Estimate your tax bracket (e.g., 25% for income tax + 15.3% for SE tax = 40%). Pay that percentage of your profit.

---

## The Workflow for Quarterly Payments

Do not try to do this manually on April 14th. Use this system:

1. **The Tax Bucket:** Open a separate savings account specifically for taxes. 
2. **The Percentage Transfer:** Every time a customer pays you, immediately transfer 25-35% of the profit into the Tax Bucket. 
3. **The Payment:** On the 15th of the deadline month, log into the IRS Direct Pay website and transfer the required amount from the Tax Bucket to the IRS.

---

## Common Mistakes

### 1. Using the Tax Bucket for Cash Flow
This is the most common way business owners go bankrupt. The money in your tax account is not your money; it is the government's money that you are simply holding. Using it to buy tools or pay rent is a high-interest loan from the IRS that will eventually come due with penalties.

### 2. Missing a Deadline
Even if you are short on cash, pay *something*. A partial payment is better than no payment. If you can't pay the full estimate, pay what you can and document why you were short. This shows a good-faith effort and can sometimes help in negotiating penalty waivers.

### 3. Overpaying
While underpaying leads to penalties, overpaying is a zero-interest loan to the government. If you find that you are consistently overpaying your estimates, adjust your percentage downward for the next quarter to keep more cash in your business.
