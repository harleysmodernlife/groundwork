## Onboarding That Works

Most businesses think onboarding is just "showing the new hire where the bathroom is and giving them a laptop." That is not onboarding; that is orientation. True onboarding is a structured process designed to move a new hire from "expensive liability" to "productive asset" as quickly as possible.

---

## The Three Phases of Onboarding

Effective onboarding happens in stages. If you dump everything on a new hire in the first four hours, they will forget 80% of it.

### Phase 1: The Pre-Board (Before Day 1)
Eliminate the "first-day friction." The new hire should not spend their first three hours waiting for a password or a desk.
- **The Kit:** Hardware, software accounts, and a physical workspace should be ready. 
- **The Welcome:** Send a "Welcome Email" with the start time, parking instructions, the dress code, and a rough agenda for the first day.
- **The Paperwork:** Use an e-signature tool (like HelloSign or DocuSign) to get the tax forms and contracts signed *before* they walk through the door.

### Phase 2: The First Week (Integration)
Focus on context and culture, not just tasks. The goal is to make them feel like part of the team while they learn the basics.
- **The Buddy System:** Pair them with a high-performer who isn't their manager. This gives them a "safe" person to ask the "stupid" questions.
- **The Quick Win:** Give them a small, achievable project in the first 48 hours. This builds confidence and proves they can contribute.
- **The Tool Tour:** A guided walkthrough of your project management (Trello, Asana), communication (Slack, Email), and documentation (Notion, Wiki) systems.

### Phase 3: The First 90 Days (Optimization)
This is where you move from training to performance. Use a 30-60-90 day plan with specific, measurable goals.
- **Day 30:** Focus on learning. Goal: "Can explain our product/service and navigate all internal tools without help."
- **Day 60:** Focus on contribution. Goal: "Can handle a standard client/project from start to finish with minimal supervision."
- **Day 90:** Focus on ownership. Goal: "Has identified one inefficiency in our process and proposed a viable solution."

## The Onboarding Checklist

Use a standardized checklist for every hire to ensure nothing is missed. 

| Category | Item | Verified? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Admin** | Signed Contract, W-4/I-9, Direct Deposit form | [ ] |
| **Access** | Email, Slack, CRM, Password Manager, Project Tool | [ ] |
| **Culture** | Company Mission, Org Chart, Communication Norms | [ ] |
| **Technical** | Software Installation, Hardware Setup, Security Training | [ ] |
| **Performance** | 30-60-90 Day Goals, First Project Assigned | [ ] |

## Common Failures

- **The "Sink or Swim" Method:** Giving a new hire a manual and telling them to "figure it out." This leads to mistakes, frustration, and high early-turnover rates.
- **The Manager Disappearance:** The manager is excited to hire someone, then immediately disappears into their own work, leaving the new hire floating for three days. Onboarding is a management task, not an HR task.
- **Ignoring the "Why":** Teaching someone *how* to use the software but not *why* the company does it that way. Without the "why," the new hire will try to "fix" things that aren't broken, creating friction with the existing team.
