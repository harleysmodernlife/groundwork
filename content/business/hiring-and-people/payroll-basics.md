## Payroll Basics

Payroll is the most dangerous part of business operations. If you mess up a customer's invoice, you lose a bit of money. If you mess up your payroll, you lose your best employees and potentially attract the attention of the IRS and Department of Labor. Payroll is not just "sending money"; it is a complex system of tax withholding, reporting, and compliance.

---

## The Core Components of a Paycheck

You must understand the difference between what the employee *sees* and what the business *pays*.

### 1. Gross Pay
The total amount earned before any deductions. This is the number agreed upon in the employment contract (e.g., $25/hour or $60,000/year).

### 2. Net Pay ("Take-Home Pay")
What actually hits the employee's bank account. This is Gross Pay minus all withholdings.
- **Mandatory Withholdings:** Federal income tax, State income tax, Social Security (6.2%), and Medicare (1.45%).
- **Voluntary Withholdings:** Health insurance premiums, 401k contributions, and union dues.

### 3. The Employer Burden (The Invisible Cost)
The business pays more than just the gross salary. These are the additional taxes and costs the employer must cover.
- **FICA Match:** The employer must match the employee's Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%) contributions. Total: 7.65% on top of gross pay.
- **FUTA/SUTA:** Federal and State Unemployment Taxes. These vary by state and by the company's history of layoffs.
- **Workers' Compensation:** Insurance that covers on-the-job injuries. This is a mandatory cost in almost every state.

## Payroll Schedules and Methods

Choosing the right frequency affects your cash flow and your employees' satisfaction.

| Schedule | Frequency | Pros | Cons |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Weekly** | Every Friday | High employee satisfaction; great for hourly workers. | High administrative burden; requires constant cash flow. |
| **Bi-Weekly** | Every other Friday | Standard for most US companies; balances admin and cash. | Some employees struggle with the 3-week gap. |
| **Semi-Monthly** | 1st and 15th | Predictable for budgeting; aligns with rent/mortgage dates. | Requires calculating different days per month. |
| **Monthly** | Last day of month | Lowest administrative burden. | Very difficult for employees to budget; unpopular. |

## The Modern Payroll Stack

Do not attempt to do payroll manually in a spreadsheet. The risk of a math error leading to a tax penalty is too high. Use a dedicated payroll service.

- **Full-Service Providers (Gusto, ADP, Paychex):** These tools handle the calculations, the direct deposits, and the automatic filing of payroll taxes with the government. They are the gold standard for small to mid-sized businesses.
- **The Integrated Approach:** Use a payroll tool that syncs directly with your accounting software (e.g., Gusto syncing to QuickBooks). This ensures your Profit & Loss statement is always accurate without manual data entry.

## Critical Compliance Rules

- **The 1099 vs. W-2 Divide:** You cannot simply call someone a "contractor" to avoid paying payroll taxes. If they are an employee, they must be on payroll. Misclassification is a primary target for IRS audits.
- **Overtime Law (FLSA):** Most hourly employees must be paid 1.5x their regular rate for any hours worked over 40 in a workweek. There are very few legal "exemptions" to this rule.
- **Pay Records:** You are legally required to keep detailed records of hours worked and payments made for several years. If you are audited, "I remember paying them in cash" is not a valid defense.
