Conformity is the tendency to adjust one's beliefs, attitudes, or behaviors to match those of a group. It is one of the most powerful and pervasive forces in social life — people conform far more than they realize, and often without conscious awareness.

---

## 1. Why People Conform

Social psychologists identify two distinct reasons people conform:

### Informational Influence

Sometimes we conform because we genuinely believe the group has better information than we do. If you walk into a room and everyone else is looking at the ceiling, you look up too — because something might actually be there. **Informational influence** is strongest when:
- The situation is ambiguous
- Others are perceived as more knowledgeable
- The stakes are high

Informational conformity can improve decision-making. Expert consensus is generally worth following. The problem arises when people defer to groups that are wrong.

### Normative Influence

Other times, we conform not because we think the group is right, but because we want to be accepted and avoid social rejection. **Normative influence** is driven by the need to belong. We follow social norms — unwritten rules about appropriate behavior — to fit in and avoid disapproval.

This explains why people laugh at jokes they don't find funny, dress according to group standards, and stay silent when they disagree with a crowd.

---

## 2. Asch's Conformity Experiments

Solomon Asch's classic studies (1951) demonstrated the power of normative influence in a striking way.

Participants were shown a standard line and asked to match it to one of three comparison lines — a simple perceptual judgment with an obvious correct answer. The catch: each participant was seated among several confederates (actors posing as participants) who unanimously gave the wrong answer.

Results:
- About **75%** of participants conformed on at least one trial
- On average, participants conformed about **37%** of the time
- Only **25%** never conformed at all

When participants were given the chance to write their answers privately, conformity dropped dramatically — revealing that normative, not informational, pressure drove most of the effect.

Asch also found that **unanimity** mattered enormously. When even one confederate gave the correct answer, conformity plummeted. A single dissenter can make disagreement feel possible.

---

## 3. Sherif's Autokinetic Effect Studies

Muzafer Sherif (1936) studied informational conformity using a different method. He placed participants in a dark room with a stationary point of light — which appears to move due to an optical illusion (the autokinetic effect). Participants estimated how far the light moved.

When alone, people developed idiosyncratic estimates. When placed in groups, their estimates converged into a shared **group norm** — which then persisted even when people were later tested alone. This demonstrated that people genuinely internalize group standards, not merely comply publicly.

## 4. Factors That Affect Conformity

**Increases conformity:**
- Larger group size (up to about 4–5; more people don't add much)
- Unanimous group — no dissenter
- High group cohesion
- Ambiguous situations
- Low individual confidence
- Public rather than private responses

**Decreases conformity:**
- Having an ally — even one person agreeing with you
- Higher individual expertise
- Anonymous or private responses
- Collectivist vs. individualist culture (conformity tends to be higher in collectivist societies, though findings vary)

---

## 5. Types of Conformity

- **Compliance**: Publicly going along while privately disagreeing. The most common form in Asch studies — people said the wrong answer but didn't believe it.
- **Internalization**: Genuinely changing your private belief to match the group. Occurs especially when informational influence is strong.
- **Identification**: Conforming to the norms of a group you admire or want to belong to, without necessarily applying those norms elsewhere.

---

## 6. Conformity in Everyday Life

Conformity explains why:
- Fashion trends spread and die
- Restaurant reviews influence whether others enjoy their meal
- Bystanders often fail to help in emergencies when others are passive
- Political opinions cluster within social networks
- People are less likely to litter in clean environments

Understanding conformity is essential for recognizing when you're being shaped by social pressure — and when to resist it. The research suggests that one of the most powerful forms of social courage is simply speaking up first. Once someone deviates from the majority, others feel freer to do the same.

---

## Summary

Conformity is the adjustment of behavior or belief to match a group, driven by informational influence (the group may know more) and normative influence (the desire to belong). Asch's experiments demonstrated that even on clear-cut tasks, most people conform to a unanimous wrong majority at least some of the time. Group size, unanimity, and public vs. private settings all shape how much people conform — but a single dissenter can dramatically reduce the effect.
